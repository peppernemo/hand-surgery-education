Upper Limb Amputation, TMR, and Prosthetics

Basic Science

  • 1.6M Americans with major amputation (doubling by 2050); >500K upper extremity

  • 50-90% of UE amputations are trauma-related (vs LE = peripheral vascular disease)
  • Any nerve transection = neuroma unless prevented
  • Do not suture flexors to extensors over bone end (quadrigia)
  • Myodesis (muscle to bone) vs myoplasty (agonist-antagonist sutured) — physiologic tension critical for myoelectric drive

Digital Amputation

  • Volar-biased skin flap (thicker, better-innervated)
  • Osteotomy mm proximal to skin level
  • Digital nerve management: traction neurectomy or end-to-end coaptation (latter decreases neuroma)
  • Disarticulation: preserve articular cartilage; trim volar + lateral condyles

Thumb Amputation

  • 40-50% of total hand function
  • Preserve length aggressively
  • Moberg flap: advance volar skin with both NV bundles; up to 2 cm; dorsal necrosis risk
  • FDMA flap (1st dorsal metacarpal artery): from dorsum P1 of index
  • Larger defects: reverse radial forearm or PIA flap

Ray Amputation

General

  • Single-ray amputation: 28% grip loss, 13% key pinch loss, 26% opposition strength loss
  • Digital amputation proximal to FDS insertion → severe flexion impairment

Index Ray

  • Generally improves function (thumb opposes long finger; 1st web unrestricted)
  • Oblique MC cut distal to ECRL/FCR insertion
  • Distal index amputation with proximal phalanx preserved = better DASH than ray amputation (manual laborers)

Middle Ray

  • Must address adductor pollicis origin from 3rd MC (weakens key pinch)
  • Ray transposition (Carroll): index → 3rd MC base; web-saving incision; step-cut osteotomy
  • No transposition (Steichen/Idler): bony resection + intervolar plate imbrication; capitate closing wedge maintains parallel rays

Ring Ray

  • Adductor unaffected; transposition: small → ring MC base (Posner)
  • LeViet translocation: closing wedge capito-hamate (technically challenging)

Small Ray

  • Preserve hypothenar muscles for padding + closure
  • Preserve 5th MC base for ECU + FCU insertions

Partial Hand — Starfish Procedure (Gaston)

  • Transmetacarpal amputation 3 cm proximal to native MCP
  • Dorsal interossei isolated + transferred to dorsal MC periosteum
  • Volar plates + flexor sheaths interposed (minimize EMG cross-talk)
  • Intrinsics are natural MCP flexors → immediate independent finger control with minimal training

Wrist Disarticulation vs Transradial

Feature Wrist Disarticulation Transradial
Pronation/supination 120° preserved Reduced
Prosthetic fit DRUJ flare aids suspension but style issues Better myoelectric fit
Length Full 6-8 cm removed
Abandonment Higher Lower

Targeted Muscle Reinnervation (TMR)

Concept (Kuiken/Dumanian)

  • Redirects severed peripheral nerves to expendable motor branches in residual muscle
  • Reinnervated muscle = “biologic amplifier” for surface EMG electrodes
  • No reported neuroma in TMR-treated nerves

Prerequisites

  1. Donor nerve: sufficient length, no functional distal targets
  2. Recipient muscle: healthy, superficial, expendable, redundant function
  3. Robust soft tissue over coaptation; completely denervate recipient first

Transradial TMR

  • Median → palmaris longus or FCR motor branch (hand close)
  • Ulnar → FCU motor branch (hand open/intrinsics)
  • Adipofascial flaps separate coaptation sites

Transhumeral TMR (5 signals)

  • Lateral biceps (preserved) — elbow flexion
  • Medial biceps + median — grasp
  • Lateral triceps + radial — digital extension
  • Brachialis + ulnar (when available)

Acute TMR

  • Single operation at initial amputation: prevents neuroma + phantom pain, faster prosthetic control

  • TMR at the time of initial amputation is the standard of care when feasible — zero reported neuromas in TMR-treated nerves, less phantom pain, and faster prosthetic fitting

Complications

Neuroma

  • Symptomatic in 10-25% of amputations
  • Conservative first 6 mo: desensitization, neuropathic meds
  • Surgical options: implantation (muscle/bone/vein), centro-central coaptation, TMR (preferred), RPNI (free muscle graft into individual fascicles)

Phantom Limb Pain

  • 84% early phantom sensations; 67% phantom pain; severe in only 15% long-term
  • Treatment: TCAs, anticonvulsants; TMR may reduce (acute TMR = least phantom pain)

Heterotopic Ossification

  • Combat-related: up to 63% (blast injury)
  • Excision in 12-19%; recurrence uncommon

Prosthetics

Types

Type Key Features
Passive (cosmetic) Silicone, realistic appearance
Body-powered Cables + harness; 6-8 lbs grip; light, cheap, durable
Myoelectric Surface EMG → motor; up to 30 lbs grip; cosmetic advantage

Pattern Recognition

  • Multiple EMG patterns → multiple functions; TMR maximizes degrees of freedom
  • Direct control: 1 muscle pair = 1 plane; mode switch (co-contraction) for 2nd DOF

Rejection Rates (Biddiss)

  • Electric 23%, passive 39%, body-powered in between
  • 75% have difficulty; 20% use no prosthesis

Timing

  • < 1 month = “golden period” for upper limb fitting
  • Fit within 6 mo → 16x more likely to continue long-term use

Composite Tissue Allotransplantation (CTA)

  • 50 transplants worldwide; all regain protective sensibility; 90% tactile

  • Functional outcomes superior to prosthetics in compliant patients
  • Double hand transplant: $318,961/QALY — exceeds cost-effectiveness threshold
  • Lifelong immunosuppression with all attendant risks

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Thumb = 40-50% of hand function — preserve length aggressively
  • Never suture flexors to extensors over bone end
  • TMR prevents neuroma and phantom pain — do it acutely when possible
  • End-to-end coaptation of digital nerves decreases neuroma vs traction neurectomy
  • Prosthetic fitting within 1 month is critical — 16x higher continued use
  • Index ray amputation often improves function; distal index amp + preserved P1 may be better for laborers
  • Starfish procedure enables immediate intuitive myoelectric finger control
  • CTA functional results are superior but cost-effectiveness and immunosuppression burden are significant barriers

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What percentage of total hand function does the thumb represent? A: 40-50%.
  2. Q: What is the “golden period” for upper limb prosthetic fitting? A: < 1 month after amputation.
  3. Q: What is TMR and what is its primary advantage for neuroma prevention? A: Targeted muscle reinnervation redirects severed nerves to expendable motor branches — zero reported neuromas in treated nerves.
  4. Q: What is the Starfish procedure? A: Dorsal interossei transfer to dorsal MC periosteum at transmetacarpal amputation — enables immediate independent myoelectric finger control.
  5. Q: What is the grip strength difference between myoelectric and body-powered prostheses? A: Myoelectric up to 30 lbs vs body-powered 6-8 lbs.
