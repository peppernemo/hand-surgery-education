Metacarpal Fractures

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Physes: distal end for index-long-ring-small MC; thumb MC physis at PROXIMAL end (occasional pseudophysis distally)
  • Vascular: nutrient artery to diaphysis middle third
    • Thumb + index: ulnar aspect
    • Long, ring, small: radial aspect
  • MC head: epiphyseal arteriole + collateral ligament association; arteriole absent in 35% → pericapsular dependence (AVN risk)
  • MC head cam-shaped — palmar diameter > dorsal → collaterals tight in flexion (intrinsic-plus position protects)
  • Shortening 2 mm → 7° extensor lag + 8% intrinsic loss; 10 mm → 50% intrinsic power loss
  • Border MCs (thumb, index, small) more prone to shortening
  • 1° MC malrotation = 5° finger malrotation — poorly tolerated

Workup

Exam

  • Cascade — fingertips aim to scaphoid tubercle in flexion; scissoring = malrotation
  • Open fight bite: cover Eikenella corrodens (human bite organism); add anaerobes for soil/debris

Imaging

  • AP, lateral, oblique standard
  • Brewerton view (MCP flexed 60°, dorsum on plate, beam 15° ulnar-to-radial) for MC head articular surface
  • 30° pronated lateral + 30° supination AP for 4th/5th CMC joints
  • CT for comminution, articular surface, CMC fracture-dislocation, suspected hamate/capitate

Classification

MC Head — McElfresh & Dobyns (10 types)

  • Epiphyseal, collateral ligament avulsion, osteochondral, oblique, vertical, horizontal, comminuted, boxer’s with joint involvement, loss of substance, AVN

Treatment — General

Surgical Indications

  • Open fracture
  • Clinical malrotation > 5°
  • Multiple displaced fractures (relative)
  • Polytrauma (relative)
  • Segmental loss
  • Shaft: shortening > 4 mm, angulation > 30°, rotation > 5°

Reduction Maneuvers + Splints

MC Head Fractures

Surgical Indications

  • Articular step-off > 1 mm
  • Comminution

  • 25% articular involvement

  • Epiphyseal injury

Technique

  • Dorsal approach over MCP
  • Split extensor tendon OR sagittal band
  • Preserve collaterals (stability + blood supply)
  • Subchondral fixation; buried K-wires for tiny articular fragments
  • Minimize hardware over joint
  • Outcomes: Barton 41% conservatively treated articular fractures fair/poor; Lee 13 pts DASH 4.2, TAM 265°

MC Neck Fractures

Boxer’s Fracture (5th MC neck)

  • Young adult males; direct axial blow
  • Apex dorsal deformity (intrinsic pull on distal fragment)
  • Always check rotation — never accept any malrotation

Barrera: boxer’s fracture < 70° angulation with no malrotation → buddy tape, no reduction.

Reduction

  • Jahss maneuver for displacement
  • Immobilize intrinsic-plus traditionally; recent data: MCP extension + PIPs free yields equivalent outcomes

Surgical Indications (Neck)

  • Index/long > 15°, scissoring
  • Ring > 30°, scissoring
  • Small > 70° sagittal, scissoring

MC Shaft Fractures

Patterns

  • Axial/bending → transverse
  • Torsional → oblique/spiral
  • Apex dorsal with shortening + angulation + rotation

Closed Reduction

  • Hematoma block (1% lidocaine)
  • 3-point mold: dorsal at apex, volar prox + dist
  • PIPs free; recheck at 1 wk → cast 2 wk → brace total 6 wk

CRPP (Percutaneous Pinning)

  • Acute, minimal exposure, fewer adhesions
  • Configurations: retrograde, antegrade IM, transverse to adjacent MC
  • Galal RCT (transverse vs IM, 5th MC neck > 30°):
    • Equivalent outcomes
    • Transverse faster (10 vs 32 min)
    • IM higher complications (backout, infection, dorsal ulnar sensory injury)
  • Buried K-wires < infection than exposed

Lag Screws (long oblique spiral, length ≥ 2× shaft width)

  • Yao: 2× 2.0 mm screws = 3× 1.5 mm screws biomechanically (preferred — cost, time)
  • Avoid plate → fewer adhesions

Headless Compression Screws (IM antegrade)

  • Hug systematic review (693 MC + 265 phalangeal): 100% union at 5.7 wk, 3.2% complications, DASH 3.7, TAM 243°
  • Supichyangur RCT: HCS RTW at 3 wk, better early ROM vs K-wire
  • Technique: sagittal band split, dorsal capsulotomy, entry at dorsal one-third MC head, cannulated drill, HCS
  • Avery cadaver: single IM screw > 2 retrograde K-wires
  • Long-term arthritis risk at insertion unknown — small cartilage footprint documented

Plate Fixation

  • Failed CR, poor bone, comminution
  • Grisdela hardware removal risk by plate position (81 patients):
    • 50% removed if within 1 cm of MCP
    • 30% removed if within 0.5 cm of CMC
  • Yalizis: 16 Australian rules football pros RTP at avg 2 wk, no complications
  • Tendon adhesions up to 31%

External Fixation

  • Severe comminution, soft tissue, contamination, bone loss

  • 2× 2.0 mm half pins; 45° dorsal-ulnar or dorsal-radial avoids extensors

  • Bone grafting (iliac crest corticocancellous) or Masquelet induced membrane (2-stage) for segmental loss

  • When plate position is close to a joint, counsel patient about high removal rate: 50% within 1 cm of MCP, 30% within 0.5 cm of CMC (Grisdela)

MC Base Fractures

  • Index/long/ring base: rare; ECRL/ECRB avulsion fragments — displaced/articular = fix
  • 5th MC base most common — frequently with CMC dislocation
  • Crush mechanism → soft tissue, NV compromise, compartment syndrome

Imaging

  • 30° pronated lateral for 4th/5th CMC subluxation
  • AP articular parallelism (overlap = subluxation)
  • Dorsal rim of hamate fracture = red flag for CMC fx-dislocation

Surgical Indications

  • Associated dislocation
  • 4th/5th: intra-articular step-off > 1 mm
  • Fx-dislocation with residual subluxation post-reduction

Technique

  • Non-displaced: cast 6 wk
  • CRPP to adjacent MC ± hamate
  • ORIF with locked T-plate
  • Bridge plate for comminution/bone loss

Post-op / Rehab

  • CRPP: 3+ wk immobilization (biomechanically weaker)
  • HCS/lag screw: protected motion → early ROM
  • Plate: early motion to prevent adhesions
  • Edema control + intrinsic mobilization

Complications & Outcomes

  • Stiffness = most common complication
  • Malunion, nonunion, hardware failure, infection, NV injury
  • Tendon adhesions (plate > screw)
  • Dorsal ulnar sensory nerve injury (esp 5th MC pin)
  • Open fracture: low infection rate even > 12 hr (Tulipan) — debride when soft tissue OK; not emergent unless contaminated

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Check rotation FIRST — every patient, every visit
  • Brewerton view for MC head articular surface
  • Border MCs (thumb, index, small) less protected by intermetacarpal ligaments
  • Avoid hardware near MCP/CMC if alternatives exist
  • HCS IM: dorsal one-third MC head entry, small cartilage footprint, faster RTW
  • Open fight bite over MCP — irrigate, cover Eikenella + anaerobes, leave open for delayed closure; check for extensor tendon laceration

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What is the Jahss maneuver? A: MCP + PIP flexed 90°, dorsally directed pressure on PIP, volar pressure on MC shaft to reduce a metacarpal neck fracture.
  2. Q: Per the Martínez-Catalán study, how should an isolated 5th MC neck fracture with < 70° angulation and no rotation be treated? A: Soft wrap + buddy taping (non-inferior to reduction + casting; faster RTW).
  3. Q: What is the impact of plate position near MCP/CMC on hardware removal rate (Grisdela)? A: 50% removal if within 1 cm of MCP; 30% if within 0.5 cm of CMC.
  4. Q: Why is MC malrotation poorly tolerated? A: 1° of MC malrotation produces 5° of finger malrotation, causing scissoring and functional loss.
  5. Q: What is the suspected diagnosis when a dorsal hamate rim fracture is seen? A: 4th/5th CMC fracture-dislocation — get CT.
