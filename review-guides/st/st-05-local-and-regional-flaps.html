Local + Regional Flaps for Hand Reconstruction

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Random pattern flaps: subdermal plexus perfusion; length-to-width ratio ≤ 2:1
  • Axial pattern flaps: named vessel; can mobilize on pedicle; each artery terminates in angiosome; choke arteries + oscillating veins connect adjacent angiosomes
  • Cutaneous (groin), fasciocutaneous (radial forearm), myocutaneous types

Classification — Reconstructive Ladder

  1. Primary closure → 2. Delayed primary closure → 3. Skin grafting → 4. Local cutaneous flaps → 5. Fasciocutaneous flaps → 6. Local/distant pedicled flaps → 7. Free tissue transfer

Workup

  • Clean, fully debrided wound bed before reconstruction; crush/infection → serial debridement first
  • Create template of defect; design flap 10% larger than defect
  • Noncritical sections can heal by secondary intention or skin graft

Treatment

Local Random Flaps

Indications: areas with skin elasticity + redundancy (dorsum of hand); defects ≤ 2-3.5 cm²

Contraindications: gross contamination, infection, significant surrounding tissue damage

V-Y Advancement (Atasoy): distal fingertip defects; ~1 cm advancement; disadvantages = volar pulp scars, decreased sensation

Rotation flap: curvilinear around pivot; best for triangular defects; arc = 4x width of defect; most frequent mistake = too small → tension → ischemia

Z-Plasty: contracture release (NOT open wounds); 60° = 75% length gain; 4-flap Z-plasty = 150% gain — best for first web space contractures

Rhomboid (Limberg): 60°/120° angles; best for dorsal hand defects

Pedicled Random Flaps (Digital)

Flap Indication Key Detail
Cross-finger (Gurdin/Pagman) Volar oblique amputations (ring/small) Donor = dorsal P2 adjacent finger; divide pedicle 14-21 days; PIP contracture risk
Reverse cross-finger Dorsal defects Adipofascial; skin flap elevated separately
Thenar (Flatt) Volar oblique index/long High PIP contracture risk; avoid in elderly/arthritis

Local Axial Flaps

Moberg (bi-pedicled): distal thumb defects up to 1.5 cm; both NV bundles preserved; modification = transverse incision at MCP base + V-Y closure to avoid IP contracture

Homodigital PDA flaps: Allen test required first; design 4-5 mm larger; preserve thick adipofascial pedicle tissue; PIP flexion contracture risk

First Dorsal Metacarpal Artery (FDMA/Kite/Foucher): dorsal/volar thumb + 1st web space; FDMA = branch of radial artery distal to EPL in snuffbox; 75% runs above FDI; harvest with FDI fascia to ensure FDMA inclusion; preop Doppler to confirm course

Reverse DMA (Quaba): based on distal perforator 0.5-1 cm proximal to MCP; covers defects beyond PIP; don’t skeletonize perforator

HTA Flap (Teoh): lateral skin from adjacent digit; preserves digital nerve + pulp at donor; single-stage; 2PD 3-5 mm; 100% survival in 40-flap series

  • For first web space contractures, 4-flap Z-plasty at 60° is the most effective release — 150% length gain. If severe, plan for coverage with groin flap, reverse radial forearm, or free flap rather than serial Z-plasties.

Forearm-Based Pedicled Flaps

Reverse Radial Forearm Flap (RRFF): - Based on retrograde palmar arch flow; Allen test mandatory - Major perforator cluster 2 cm proximal to radial styloid - Workhorse for dorsal hand defects - Disadvantages: radial artery sacrifice, donor scarring, exposed flexor tendons - RAPF (perforator flap) alternative preserves radial artery

Posterior Interosseous Artery (PIA) Flap: no radial sacrifice; designed on line from lateral epicondyle to ulnar styloid; 7-14 perforators; venous congestion is known risk

Groin Flap (Smith 1972): based on SCIA; 2-stage distant pedicled for degloving trauma; divide pedicle at 3 weeks; defatting in 85%; PE risk >age 50 from prolonged immobilization

  • Always confirm Allen test before any flap that sacrifices the radial artery

Complications & Outcomes

  • Arterial insufficiency: pallor, poor refill; check for kinks; if failed → delay flap for choke vessel dilation; ICG angiography to verify
  • Venous congestion: brisk bleeding, firm veins, blue/swelling; severe → free transfer or supercharging
  • Monitoring: clinical judgment + trained nursing remains best

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Design flaps 10% larger than defect — tight insetting = ischemia
  • Rotation flap most common error: too small
  • Z-plasty is for contracture release only, never primary wound closure
  • Allen test before any flap that uses or sacrifices the radial or ulnar artery
  • FDMA flap: harvest with FDI fascia — 25% of the time FDMA runs within the muscle
  • Thenar flap: avoid in elderly or patients with arthritis (PIP contracture)
  • Groin flap in patients >50: DVT/PE risk from prolonged immobilization

Board-Style Questions (4)

  1. Q: What is the maximum length-to-width ratio for a random pattern flap? — A: 2:1.

  2. Q: A 30-year-old has a 1.2 cm volar thumb tip defect with exposed bone. What is the most appropriate local axial flap? — A: Moberg advancement flap (bi-pedicled, covers distal thumb defects up to 1.5 cm with sensate coverage).

  3. Q: What percentage of length gain does a 60° Z-plasty provide? — A: 75% (4-flap Z-plasty at 60° provides 150%).

  4. Q: What must be confirmed before elevating a reverse radial forearm flap? — A: Allen test confirming a patent, complete superficial palmar arch for ulnar-dominant hand perfusion.
