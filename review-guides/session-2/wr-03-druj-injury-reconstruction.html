DRUJ Injury and Reconstruction

Review Guide — Source: Handthology

Hybrid format: tight board-prep bullets layered with Barrera-voice clinical pearls. Target length: 1-3 pages. Drop unused sections; do NOT pad.

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • DRUJ: diarthrodial trochoid joint; intrinsically unstable; ulnar head (convex, 10 mm curvature) articulates with sigmoid notch of radius (concave, 15 mm curvature)
  • Sigmoid notch morphology (axial plane): flat face (42%), C-type (30%), S-type (14%), ski slope (12%)
  • Tolat coronal types: type I parallel (55%), type II oblique (33%), type III reverse oblique (12%)
  • Ulnar fovea: center of forearm rotation axis; attachment site for deep RUL fibers (ligamentum subcruentum)
  • TFCC components: central articular disc, dorsal + volar RULs, meniscus homologue, ulnar collateral ligament, ulnocarpal ligaments, ECU subsheath floor
  • RULs = primary DRUJ stabilizers; each has superficial (styloid) and deep (foveal) components; both must be injured for severe unidirectional instability
  • Forearm rotation: 150-170° arc; < 10% ulnar head contact at extremes of rotation; max contact (60%) at neutral
  • Force transmission: radius carries majority; ulna 3-34% depending on ulnar variance

Classification

  • Palmer classification of TFCC tears:
    • Class I (traumatic): IA = central perforation (no DRUJ instability); IB = ulnar avulsion (can destabilize DRUJ); IC = distal avulsion (ulnocarpal ligaments); ID = radial avulsion
    • Class II (degenerative): IIA = wear; IIB = wear + chondromalacia; IIC = perforation + chondromalacia; IID = + LT perforation; IIE = + ulnocarpal arthritis

Workup

History

  • Dorsal/volar dislocation mechanism: hyperpronation → dorsal; hypersupination → volar
  • Associated injuries: distal radius fracture (most common), Galeazzi fracture, Essex-Lopresti injury
  • Ulnar styloid fractures accompany 60% of distal radius fractures; base fractures with > 2 mm displacement or foveal involvement may destabilize DRUJ

Exam

  • Ballottement/shuck test: stabilize radius, translate ulna dorsal-palmar in neutral, pronation, supination; compare contralateral
  • Piano key sign: pronated forearm, press ulna volarly → springs back like piano key
  • DRUJ compression test: compress ulna against sigmoid notch + rotate forearm → pain/crepitus
  • Fovea sign: press soft spot between FCU, ECU, triquetrum, ulnar head → pain = TFCC pathology
  • Table-top test: push up from seated → dorsal dimple sign = instability

Imaging

  • True lateral: pisoscaphocapitate relationship; ulna normally 2 mm dorsal to radius; > 6 mm = instability
  • CT (gold standard): bilateral comparison in neutral/supination/pronation
  • MRI/MR arthrography: foveal attachment assessment; sensitivity debated
  • Arthroscopy: trampoline test (central disc tension), hook test (foveal competence), suction test

Treatment

Non-operative

  • Acute isolated DRUJ dislocation: closed reduction + above-elbow immobilization 4-6 weeks in position of stability (dorsal dislocation → supination; volar → pronation)
  • Subacute/chronic: wrist brace, FCU/ECU strengthening

Barrera: For TFCC injuries with a stable DRUJ, use the standard treatment ladder: 4 weeks rigid brace → Wrist Widget + OT → MRI without contrast if still symptomatic → CSI (Kenalog 10 mg/mL + 1% lido, never Depo) → surgery only as last resort with positive MRI and refractory symptoms.

Operative

TFCC Repair - Peripheral repair: outside-in arthroscopic technique; debride synovitis, PDS sutures through capsule + disc - Foveal repair (deep): arthroscopic bone tunnel repair (preferred) or open anchor into fovea - Open foveal repair: hockey stick incision; silhouette DRUJ capsulotomy; suture anchor into fovea - Comparable outcomes open vs arthroscopic

DRUJ Ligament Reconstruction (Adams procedure) - Indication: chronic instability with irreparable TFCC - Palmaris longus tendon graft through bone tunnels in radius (dorsal-to-volar) and ulna (fovea-to-lateral neck) - Graft passed around ulnar neck in opposite directions, tensioned in neutral rotation, tied - Post-op: long-arm orthosis → cast; short-arm at 4 wk; activities at 4 mo; heavy lifting at 6 mo - Results: 12/14 symptom relief, 24% grip strength increase; failures from sigmoid notch deficiency - Biomechanical limitation: does not restore stability if IOM also injured

Associated Injury Patterns

  • Galeazzi fracture: DRUJ instability in 55% if fracture < 7.5 cm from articular surface; stabilize radius first, then assess DRUJ
  • Essex-Lopresti: radial head fracture + IOM rupture + DRUJ disruption; ulnar-positive variance is a clue; radial head replacement + DRUJ repair ± IOM reconstruction
  • Distal radius malunion: most common cause of chronic DRUJ dysfunction; dorsal angulation > 20-30° and radial shortening > 3-5 mm → DRUJ instability

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Both dorsal and volar RULs must be injured for complete unidirectional instability
  • After distal radius ORIF, always check DRUJ stability intraoperatively
  • Ulnar styloid tip fractures are generally benign; base fractures may destabilize the DRUJ
  • The hook test is the most sensitive/specific arthroscopic test for foveal detachment (Atzei)
  • CT is superior to lateral radiographs for diagnosing DRUJ subluxation (10° rotation can falsely normalize lateral)
  • When reconstructing the DRUJ, address any sigmoid notch deficiency or distal radius malunion first

Board-Style Questions (3-5)

  1. Q: Which Palmer class TFCC tear causes DRUJ instability and involves avulsion from the ulnar attachment? — A: Class IB (ulnar avulsion/peripheral tear).
  2. Q: A Galeazzi fracture is defined as a fracture of the distal third of the radius with what associated injury? — A: Dislocation of the DRUJ (dorsal or volar).
  3. Q: What position of immobilization provides maximal DRUJ stability after reduction of a dorsal DRUJ dislocation? — A: Supination.
  4. Q: What is the gold standard imaging study for evaluating DRUJ instability? — A: CT with bilateral comparison in neutral, supination, and pronation.
  5. Q: In an Essex-Lopresti injury, what three structures are disrupted? — A: Radial head, interosseous membrane, and DRUJ (TFCC).
