Rheumatoid and Inflammatory Arthropathies

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Systemic autoimmune inflammatory synovitis; ~1% US prevalence
  • Autoantibodies: RF + ACPAs (anti-citrullinated protein antibodies)
  • Wrist: 50% within 2 yr, >90% at 10 yr
  • Reactive synovium erodes ligaments, cartilage, tendons, bone
  • DMARDs have dramatically reduced surgical volume

Classification

Larsen Radiographic Score

Grade Description
0 Normal
1 Periarticular osteoporosis + swelling
2 Mild narrowing + erosion
3 Moderate destruction
4 Severe destruction + joint space loss
5 Arthritis mutilans

Wrightington Wrist Classification

Grade Pathology Surgery
1 Osteoporosis, cysts, erosion Synovectomy
2 Carpal instability Soft tissue + partial arthrodesis
3 Destruction + subluxation Arthroplasty or arthrodesis
4 Severe radial destruction Arthrodesis

Workup

History

  • Disease duration, prior surgeries, current DMARDs
  • Cervical spine instability — mandatory preop assessment

Exam

  • Volar/dorsal swelling, crepitus (tenosynovitis)
  • Prominent distal ulna → impending extensor rupture
  • Bunnell test for intrinsic tightness (PIP flexion limited with MCP extended)
  • Check for CTS from flexor tenosynovitis

Imaging

  • Standard wrist series; Larsen grading
  • MRI/US helpful to distinguish tendon rupture DDx (sagittal band, PIN palsy, cervical)

Other

  • 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria: active synovitis ≥ 1 joint + serology (RF/ACPA) + acute phase reactants
  • SLICC criteria for SLE; CASPAR criteria for PsA

Treatment

Perioperative DMARD Management

Continue Hold
Methotrexate Sulfasalazine, azathioprine (1 wk)
Hydroxychloroquine Leflunomide (1-2 d pre, restart 1-2 wk post)
NSAIDs (D/C 1 wk) Etanercept (1 wk)
Infliximab, golimumab (1 mo)
Rituximab (2 mo)

Non-operative

  • Splinting, activity modification, steroid injection for early synovitis
  • Team approach: rheumatology, primary care, hand therapy

Barrera: CSI = Kenalog 10 mg/mL + 1% lido (never Depo).

  • Useful early for isolated tenosynovitis or single-joint synovitis before surgery is warranted

Operative — Staged Proximal to Distal

Wrist

  • Tenosynovectomy (dorsal compartments) — early intervention; doesn’t alter progression
  • Chamay radiolunate fusion — radiolunate arthritis with midcarpal sparing; ~40% develop midcarpal degeneration but doesn’t cause failure
  • Total wrist arthrodesis — end-stage; slight extension + ulnar deviation; most common salvage
  • Total wrist arthroplasty — 4th-gen porous-coated; bilateral = dominant arthroplasty, nondominant arthrodesis

DRUJ

  • Darrach (distal ulna excision) — reliable pain relief; oblique cut beveled proximally on radial side
  • Sauve-Kapandji — preferred over Darrach when ulnar translocation present; DRUJ fusion + proximal shaft pseudarthrosis (10-14 mm resection)
  • DRUJ arthroplasty (Aptis/Scheker) — semi-constrained; for painful DRUJ or symptomatic Darrach stump

Extensor Tendon Rupture

  • Vaughan-Jackson syndrome: attritional rupture starting ulnar side (EDM/small EDC)
  • Primary repair rarely possible — tendon transfers:
    • EDM alone → side-to-side to ring EDC
      • Ring → EIP to small EDC
      • Long → FDS ring to ring/small EDC, EIP to long
    • All EDCs + EIP → FDS ring + middle

Flexor Tendon Rupture

  • Mannerfelt lesion: FPL attenuated over scaphoid osteophyte
  • FPL: ring/middle FDS transfer via extended CT approach

MCP Joint

  • Crossed intrinsic transfer — passively correctable ulnar drift without arthritis
  • Silicone arthroplasty — subluxation + drift with arthritis; bone shortening + alignment + intrinsic release; favorable outcomes for pain/ROM/appearance
  • Pyrocarbon: 32% complication, 29% reoperation — avoid in RA (ligamentous laxity)
  • Arthrodesis primarily for thumb MCP (neutral, 10-20° flexion)

PIP Joint

  • Swan neck: synovitis → intrinsic tightness, volar plate laxity; FDS tenodesis (Wei/Terrono) for flexible deformity
  • Boutonniere: central slip attrition; nighttime PIP extension splint; extensor rebalancing; unreliable results (Kiefhaber)
  • Silicone arthroplasty: for bony erosion + pain; arc limited to ~60°

Other Inflammatory Arthropathies

Psoriatic Arthritis

  • Seronegative; DIP most affected; 20% present before skin rash
  • CASPAR criteria (≥ 3/5): psoriasis hx, nail changes, dactylitis, RF-negative, juxta-articular new bone
  • “Pencil-in-cup” deformity on x-ray
  • DIP arthrodesis: K-wires × 8 wk or headless compression screws

SLE

  • 90% present with arthritis (joint laxity), unlike RA: bony destruction + tendon rupture uncommon

  • Avoid wrist arthroplasty (soft tissue laxity)
  • Silicone MCP arthroplasty for recurrent drift

Scleroderma

  • Raynaud’s, sclerodactyly, fingertip ulcers, calcinosis
  • Periarterial sympathectomy (adventitial stripping 1.5-2 cm) for refractory digital ischemia
  • Calcinosis cutis — surgical debulking for symptomatic deposits

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Methotrexate + hydroxychloroquine continue perioperatively — all others hold
  • Vaughan-Jackson starts ulnar-side — inspect EDM first
  • Sauve-Kapandji over Darrach when ulnar translocation present
  • Cervical spine clearance before any procedure under GA
  • Pyrocarbon fails in RA — ligamentous laxity intolerance
  • PsA can present before skin rash — check nails + DIP joints
  • SLE deformities are soft tissue, not erosive — splint + rebalance before surgery

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: Which DMARDs should be continued perioperatively in RA? A: Methotrexate and hydroxychloroquine.
  2. Q: What is Vaughan-Jackson syndrome? A: Sequential attritional rupture of extensor tendons starting with EDM/small EDC due to caput ulna dorsal prominence.
  3. Q: When is Sauve-Kapandji preferred over Darrach? A: When ulnar translocation of the carpus is present — Darrach worsens instability.
  4. Q: What deformity is pathognomonic for psoriatic arthritis on x-ray? A: “Pencil-in-cup” deformity (proximal phalanx tapered, distal expanded).
  5. Q: What is the Mannerfelt lesion? A: Attritional FPL rupture over a scaphoid osteophyte in RA.
