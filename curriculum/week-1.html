Week 1 · Hand Exam & Consult Survival

Resident track, week 1 of 7 · Intern track, week 1 of 4

Every decision downstream of a hand consult starts with the exam. The goal this week is simple: by Friday you can do a complete, systematic hand exam on any consult in under 10 minutes, describe it in language the attending can act on, and put on the right splint before you leave the bedside.

Core (required)

Resource Where
Kenney RJ, Hammert WC. Physical examination of the hand. J Hand Surg Am 2014;39(11):2324-34. Drive bundle: Kenney - 2014 - Physical Examination of the Hand.pdf

That is the whole week 1 reading. Spend the saved time examining hands: do the full exam on every consult this week, even when the chief complaint is focal. Dr. Barrera will run the “hand exam in 10 minutes” walkthrough live at your first check-in.

Self-test (attempt before opening the key)

  1. Name the 5 flexor tendon zones and the anatomic landmark that bounds each.
  2. What are the 10 structures that pass through the carpal tunnel?
  3. List the intrinsic muscles of the hand by nerve supply (median vs ulnar).
  4. What is the cutaneous innervation of the hand: which nerve supplies the radial dorsum, the palmar thumb through radial ring, the dorsal small finger, and the palmar ulnar ring and small?
  5. How do you isolate FDS from FDP on exam? What is the pitfall with the small finger?
  6. A patient cannot lift the thumb off a flat table but can still extend the thumb MCP. Which tendon is out, and what muscle is compensating?
  7. Describe how you test the intrinsics in a suspected ulnar neuropathy. Be specific about finger positioning.
Answer key (open after attempting all questions)
  1. Flexor zones: Zone I distal to the FDS insertion on the middle phalanx (FDP only). Zone II FDS insertion to the A1 pulley at the distal palmar crease (“no man’s land,” FDS + FDP). Zone III distal palmar crease to the distal edge of the transverse carpal ligament (lumbrical origins). Zone IV within the carpal tunnel. Zone V proximal to the carpal tunnel.
  2. Carpal tunnel contents: 4 FDS + 4 FDP + 1 FPL + median nerve = 10. FCR runs in its own sheath outside the canal; palmaris longus, ulnar nerve/artery, and radial artery are all outside.
  3. Median (recurrent motor branch): APB, opponens pollicis, FPB superficial head, radial 2 lumbricals (index + middle). Ulnar (deep motor branch): everything else: hypothenars, all 7 interossei, ulnar 2 lumbricals, adductor pollicis, FPB deep head.
  4. Radial dorsum: superficial radial nerve. Palmar thumb-index-middle-radial ring: median. Dorsal small + dorsal ulnar ring: dorsal cutaneous branch of ulnar. Palmar ulnar ring + small: ulnar.
  5. Hold all non-tested fingers in full extension (blocks FDP through the common muscle belly); the patient flexes the PIP of the test finger. Pitfall: 15-20% of people lack an independent FDS to the small finger, so an isolated “negative” small-finger FDS test is not evidence of injury.
  6. EPL rupture; EPB is compensating (EPB extends the MCP). EPL is the only tendon that retropulses the thumb off the plane of the palm. Classic setting: attritional rupture at Lister’s tubercle after a nondisplaced distal radius fracture.
  7. Froment’s: pinch a paper; IP flexion is positive (FPL substituting for adductor pollicis). Wartenberg’s sign: small finger rests abducted. Crossed fingers (index over middle): first dorsal + second palmar interossei. Card test: abduction/adduction against resistance.

Run the check-in

Book 15-20 minutes with Dr. Barrera (default: Friday after 6:00 rounds). You present, he asks. Bring one real consult from your week.

Cases to walk through:

  1. The ED calls: “volar forearm laceration from glass, bleeding controlled.” Talk through your bedside exam from the doorway to the phone call back to your senior: what you inspect, every motor and sensory test you run, and how you report it.
  2. A 62-year-old woman fell on an outstretched hand. Wrist films are read as negative but she is tender in the snuffbox. Walk through your exam, what you tell her, and how she leaves the ED.
  3. You are asked to “rule out flexor tenosynovitis” in a swollen finger. Walk through the exam that confirms or excludes it and what else is on your differential for a red, swollen digit.

Rapid-fire:

  • Normal 2PD at the fingertip? (Report digits thumb to small.)
  • How do you perform and interpret an Allen’s test?
  • How do you isolate EIP from EDC?
  • What splint goes on a suspected scaphoid injury, and what goes on a “safe position” hand?
  • Which nerve territory does the palmar cutaneous branch of the median cover, and why does it matter?
