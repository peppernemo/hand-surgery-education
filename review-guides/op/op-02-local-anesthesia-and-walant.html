Local Anesthesia + WALANT

Anatomy / Basic Science

Nerve Physiology

  • Resting potential -70 mV; threshold -55 mV triggers voltage-gated Na+ channel opening → depolarization
  • Repolarization via K+ efflux to -95 mV; Na/K ATPase restores gradient
  • Myelinated nerves: saltatory conduction (nodes of Ranvier) — faster
  • Conduction rate proportional to axon cross-sectional area

Local Anesthetic Mechanism

  • Reversibly block voltage-gated Na+ channels → prevent depolarization
  • Differential blockade by fiber size/myelination: pain → cold → heat → touch → deep pressure → motor

Classification

Amide vs Ester

  • Amides (have “i” before “-caine”): lidocaine, bupivacaine, prilocaine, mepivacaine — metabolized by liver
  • Esters: cocaine, tetracaine, benzocaine, procaine — hydrolyzed by plasma pseudocholinesterase

Pharmacology

  • ↑ Lipid solubility → faster onset
  • ↑ Protein binding → longer duration
  • pKa determines onset: lower pKa = more uncharged base = faster diffusion (lidocaine pKa 7.9 = rapid; bupivacaine pKa 8.1 = slower)
  • NaHCO3 buffering raises extracellular pH → more uncharged base → faster onset + less pain
  • All locals except cocaine are vasodilators → epinephrine added for hemostasis, prolonged effect, decreased systemic absorption
  • Max dose: 7 mg/kg lidocaine with epinephrine

Workup

Toxicity — LAST (Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity)

  • Progression: lightheadedness → circumoral paresthesias/metallic taste → tinnitus/slurred speech → nystagmus/tremors → seizures → coma/arrest
  • Treatment: ABCs + IV lipid emulsion (bolus then infusion) + seizure management
  • Bupivacaine particularly cardiotoxic (avid for cardiac Na+ channels) — bradyarrhythmias, wide QRS, negative inotropy, cardiac collapse

Methemoglobinemia

  • Primarily prilocaine (metabolite ortho-toluidine); rare with lidocaine; no reported cases with WALANT
  • MetHb > 3-5 mg/dL → cyanosis, chocolate-colored blood
  • Treatment: O2 + IV methylene blue 1-2 mg/kg over 5 min (repeat q60min, max 7 mg/kg)

Allergic Reactions

  • Very rare; most from ester subtype (PABA metabolite) or methylparaben preservative
  • True lidocaine allergy essentially non-existent (< 20 case reports in 20 years)

Epinephrine Toxicity

  • Antidote: phentolamine — 1.5 mg diluted in 1 mL 2% plain lidocaine, inject subcutaneously until site pinks up

Treatment

WALANT — Wide Awake Local Anesthesia No Tourniquet

  • Tumescent local anesthesia floods surgical field; no tourniquet, no sedation
  • Wait ≥ 30 min for optimal vasoconstriction — inject 2-3 patients ahead
  • Even 0.25% lidocaine with 1:400,000 epinephrine provides adequate analgesia and hemostasis
  • Fingers: max 2 mL volar/dorsal at P1 and P2; < 1 mL at P3
  • Always inject proximal to distal (anesthetize nerves first)

Key Advantages

  • No preop testing, fasting, IV, or medication changes

  • Safer than sedation for ASA 4 patients

  • No need to discontinue anticoagulation

  • Intraoperative active motion verification — reduces flexor tendon rupture, sets tendon transfer tension

  • No tourniquet let-down bleeding; often no cautery needed

  • Simple procedures (CTR, K-wires) can move to clinic without increased infection

  • WALANT sequence: local injection → 30-min wait → prep/drape → operate

Barrera: CSI = Kenalog 10 mg/mL + 1% lidocaine (never Depo-Medrol) — separate from the WALANT local.

Typical Volumes (1% lido + 1:100k epi + bicarb 10:1)

Procedure Volume Injection site
CTR 20 mL 10 mL between ulnar/median nerves; 10 mL under incision
Trigger finger 4 mL Subcutaneous beneath incision center
SIMPLE finger block 2 mL Volar mid-proximal phalanx
Metacarpal fracture 40 mL All around MC at dissection/hardware sites
DRF plating 80-100 mL of 0.5% Under incision + “walk the bone” along periosteum

WALANT Tips for Painless Injection

  • Buffer with NaHCO3, warm solution, start with 30G half-inch needle on 3 mL syringe
  • Insert perpendicular to skin; inject ahead of needle into tumesced tissue
  • Sensory distraction: flick/pinch skin, cooling, inject during deep inspiration

WALANT Complications

  • White fingertip: usually self-resolves; phentolamine 1-2 mg in saline if no cap refill
  • Most common cause of digital loss from epi: patient burns fingertip with hot water trying to restore sensation
  • Adrenaline rush: ≥ 3% of patients — warn in advance
  • Fainting: inject supine; abort with knee flexion + Trendelenburg

Regional Anesthesia

Brachial Plexus Blocks

Block Coverage Key note
Interscalene Shoulder Always blocks phrenic nerve
Supraclavicular Any UE incl. shoulder Less phrenic block; pneumothorax < 0.04% with US
Infraclavicular Elbow and below Reliably blocks musculocutaneous
Axillary Forearm + hand Often misses musculocutaneous (needs separate block)
  • All UE blocks need separate intercostal brachial nerve block for tourniquet tolerance
  • Bier block: not recommended given WALANT/nerve block advances (limited by tourniquet pain + systemic toxicity risk)

Peripheral Blocks

  • Median at wrist: between FCR and PL, 2-3 cm proximal to crease
  • Ulnar at elbow: 3-5 cm proximal to elbow (avoid subcutaneous position behind medial epicondyle — neuritis risk)
  • Radial at wrist: field block 5-7 cm proximal to radial styloid, lateral to radial artery

Anesthesia in Pregnancy

  • Nonurgent surgery: second trimester preferred

  • Local anesthetics not known teratogenic → WALANT/regional without sedation preferred in first trimester

  • Emergent: proceed as indicated

  • WALANT eliminates the need for an anesthesia team, recovery room, or overnight stay. For flexor tendon repairs and tendon transfers, intraoperative active motion verification sets tension and reduces rupture risk.

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Amides have “i” before “-caine” — lidocaine, bupivacaine, prilocaine, mepivacaine
  • LAST is progressive — circumoral paresthesias and metallic taste are early warning signs before seizures
  • Bupivacaine is the most cardiotoxic local; avoid for WALANT (also has 30 hr numbness)
  • Phentolamine is the specific antidote for both epinephrine-related ischemia and LAST-related vasoconstriction
  • Always inject proximal to distal for WALANT — anesthetize the nerve before the operative field
  • Big nerves (median at wrist) may take up to 100 min for full anesthesia without US guidance — tumesce epineurium early

Board-Style Questions (5)

  1. Q: A patient develops circumoral paresthesias and metallic taste during a WALANT CTR. What is the diagnosis and first-line treatment? — A: LAST (local anesthetic systemic toxicity); IV lipid emulsion bolus then infusion.
  2. Q: Which local anesthetic is most cardiotoxic and why? — A: Bupivacaine — it has high avidity for cardiac Na+ channels, causing refractory arrhythmias.
  3. Q: Which brachial plexus block always causes ipsilateral phrenic nerve palsy? — A: Interscalene block.
  4. Q: A patient’s fingertip is white and without capillary refill 2 hours after a digital block with lidocaine + epinephrine. What is the antidote? — A: Phentolamine (1.5 mg in 1 mL 2% plain lidocaine, inject subcutaneously).
  5. Q: What is the most common cause of fingertip tissue loss after a digital block with epinephrine? — A: Patient accidentally burning the fingertip with hot water while trying to restore sensation.
