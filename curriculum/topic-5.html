Topic 5 · Tendon

Flexor tendon & pulleys · extensor mechanism · mallet · trigger finger & De Quervain’s

The flexor pulley system alone is 8% of OITE hand questions, tied with carpal tunnel for the most-tested single item. Add the extensor mechanism (6%) and this is the highest-yield anatomy week of the cycle.

Interns own: the flexor zones, testing FDS/FDP against resistance, and the mallet splinting talk (never let the tip drop).

Test yourself

  1. Name the annular and cruciate pulleys, the two that matter most, and how much venting is acceptable.
  2. Classify jersey finger (Leddy I-IV). Which type must be repaired within 7-10 days and why?
  3. Describe the OR plan for a zone II flexor laceration: anesthesia, core and epitendinous technique, splint position, when therapy starts.
  4. Doyle zones for the extensor mechanism: the classic injury at zones I, III, and V, and the exam test for a central slip injury.
  5. A fresh film in a “jammed finger a month ago” shows a dorsal base fragment with the distal phalanx subluxated volarly. What is the operative trigger in a bony mallet?
  6. Grade trigger finger (Green), give first-line treatment with numbers, and the injection formulation used in this practice.
  7. How do you tell De Quervain’s from thumb CMC arthritis at the bedside, and what is first-line for De Quervain’s?
Answers
  1. A1-A5, C1-C3. A2 (proximal phalanx) and A4 (middle phalanx) prevent bowstringing; venting up to ~25% of A2 or all of A4 is acceptable to let a repair glide.
  2. Leddy I: FDP retracts to the palm, both vincula torn, no nutrition, the muscle shortens: repair within 7-10 days. II: retracts to the PIP, long vinculum intact, weeks of grace. III: bony fragment caught at A4. IV: fracture plus tendon avulsed off the fragment; fix both.
  3. WALANT or block (WALANT lets you watch the repair glide actively). Bruner or midlateral extension, retrieve without grabbing the tendon through the sheath, 4- or 6-strand core (3-0/4-0 braided) plus running 5-0/6-0 epitendinous, vent as needed. Dorsal blocking splint: wrist ~30 flexion, MCPs ~60, IPs straight. Early active protocol with therapy from day 3-5; the therapy IS the operation.
  4. Odd zones over joints: I DIP = mallet; III PIP = central slip → boutonniere; V MCP = sagittal band rupture (middle finger, radial side most common; relative-extension splinting if acute) or fight bite until proven otherwise. Central slip test: Elson: PIP flexed 90 over a table edge, extend the middle phalanx against resistance; a rigid DIP means the central slip is out.
  5. Volar subluxation of the distal phalanx, not fragment size, is the operative indication (extension-block pinning). Soft-tissue mallet and non-subluxated bony mallet: full-time DIP extension splinting 6-8 weeks, then weaning; one dropped tip resets the clock.
  6. Green I pre-triggering pain, II active triggering, III locked requiring passive unlock, IV fixed contracture. First line: corticosteroid injection into the sheath at A1, ~80% success first injection, ~50% second; up to two before A1 release; sooner for grade IV, lower threshold to operate in diabetics. Formulation here: 0.75 mL 1% lidocaine + 0.25 mL Kenalog 40 (10 mg), 1 mL total, never Depo-Medrol.
  7. De Quervain’s: dorsoradial tenderness over the first compartment at the styloid, positive Finkelstein/Eichhoff, younger, classically postpartum. CMC arthritis: thumb-base pain with pinch, positive grind, older, x-ray changes. The tender spots are a centimeter apart. De Quervain’s first line: thumb spica orthosis + injection into the first dorsal compartment (watch for the separate EPB subsheath as the cause of failures).
