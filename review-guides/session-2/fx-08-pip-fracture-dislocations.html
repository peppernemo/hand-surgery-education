PIP Joint Dislocations and Fracture-Dislocations

Review Guide — Source: Handthology

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Bicondylar hinge; ROM 0-120°
  • 85% of grasp motion comes from PIP
  • Primary stabilizers: tongue-in-groove articular geometry + proper collateral ligaments
  • Secondary: volar plate, accessory collaterals, surrounding soft tissue

Workup

Exam

  • Inspect alignment; skin puckering = soft tissue entrapment = irreducible
  • Tendon integrity (resting tension + tenodesis)
  • Full extension test critical for volar dislocations (boutonniere risk)
  • Dorsal dislocation: usually unstable in extension; palmar dislocation: unstable in flexion
  • Document degree of flexion needed for congruent joint

Elson Test (central slip)

  • PIP held flexed 90° → patient extends against resistance → examiner tests DIP for resistance to flexion
  • Positive = rigid DIP extension (intact central slip = lateral bands relax = DIP floppy; ruptured = lateral bands tighten = DIP stiff)

Collateral Ligament Grading

Grade Pain Stress Status
1 + No laxity Sprain
2 + Laxity + firm endpoint Incomplete
3 + > 20° + instability Complete

Imaging

  • True lateral PIP critical
  • Dorsal V sign = subtle dorsal subluxation
  • True AP for coronal

Classification of Injuries

  • Strain/sprain (collateral or volar plate)
  • Dislocations (dorsal, lateral, volar) — no fracture
  • Fracture-dislocations
  • Pilon fracture = V + dorsal margin fractures of middle phalanx base; splay in both planes

Pure Dislocations

Lateral Dislocation

  • Reducible → buddy tape + early protected ROM
  • Irreducible → volar rotatory category

Dorsal Dislocation (most common)

  • Reduce with traction + volar pressure
  • Generally stable after reduction without fracture
  • Simple + stable: buddy tape or 10° extension-block × 3-4 wk
  • Unstable in extension → dorsal blocking splint + immediate active flexion

Volar Dislocation (rare, unstable)

  • Boutonniere risk from central slip injury
  • Often involves central slip + volar plate + ≥ 1 collateral
  • Splint PIP in full extension × 3 wk with serial lateral XR for re-dislocation
  • Re-dislocation → pin PIP in extension × 3-4 more wk
  • Total ~6-8 wk splinting

Volar Rotatory Dislocation

  • Proximal phalanx rotates around intact collateral
  • Often irreducible (condyle trapped between central slip + lateral band OR collateral button-holes)
  • Operative if irreducible
  • Any volar dislocation: check central slip integrity (Elson test if possible at presentation; MRI/US if unclear); splint full extension for 3 wk before declaring boutonniere risk averted

Dorsal Fracture-Dislocations of PIP (Volar Lip Fracture of Middle Phalanx)

Decision based on % articular involvement + flexion needed for stability:

Articular % Stability Treatment
< 30% Usually stable Dorsal block splint, extend 10°/wk
30-50% Tenuous Dorsal block splint OR surgery
> 50% Unstable Surgery
> 30° flexion needed for congruence Unstable Surgery

Surgical Options (no clear superiority)

  1. Dorsal block pinning (Waris 41 pts at 5 yr: PIP arc 80°, 6° contracture, 29% recurrent subluxation, 10% severe arthritis)
  2. Dynamic external fixation (“pins + rubber”): ligamentotaxis, immediate motion; 15-25% pin track infection, 25% early arthritis
  3. ORIF: large fragments, small screws, rigid early ROM; PIP arc 70-85%; failure up to 9%
  4. Volar Plate Arthroplasty (Eaton-Malerich): excise volar lip, advance volar plate; contraindicated > 50% articular (instability risk); PIP arc 70-90°
  5. Hemi-Hamate Osteochondral Autograft (Williams-Hastings): dorsal distal hamate matches volar lip cup; for > 50% volar lip defect, comminution, or salvage; PIP arc 65-85°

Barrera: hemi-hamate autograft CPT = 26746 + 20900, NOT 26546. [[reference_hemihamate_cpt]]

Pilon Fractures

  • Complete dissociation of articular surface from middle phalanx base
  • Dorsal-palmar + radio-ulnar splay → inherently unstable
  • Primary goal: joint stability + alignment (anatomic articular congruity is secondary)
  • Options: ORIF, DEF, combinations
  • Locking + condylar plates successful
  • Aggressive ORIF risks may outweigh DEF benefits
  • PIP motion 80-90° outcomes

Post-op / Rehab

  • Early motion within 1 wk prevents adhesions
  • Adjacent joint motion immediately (DIP stiffness often underreported)
  • Stable construct → active-assisted exercises at 3-6 wk
  • Flexion contracture stretching + nighttime extension splinting at 4-6 wk
  • Elastic garments for edema; desensitization
  • Counsel all patients pre-op about permanent PIP girth increase, some loss of motion, and arthritis risk regardless of treatment

Complications & Outcomes

  • Stiffness most common (every option)
  • Permanent PIP girth increase
  • Flexion contracture
  • Swan neck (volar plate detachment → loss of checkrein)
  • Posttraumatic arthritis
  • Tenolysis often needed
  • Salvage: arthrodesis, silicone arthroplasty, resurfacing arthroplasty

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Document degree of flexion needed for joint congruence — drives treatment
  • Volar dislocation = check central slip → splint extension 3 wk before declaring safe
  • Dorsal V sign on lateral = subtle dorsal subluxation

  • 50% volar lip articular involvement → hemi-hamate autograft is one of the standard options (along with DEF, ORIF, VPA depending on fragment quality)

  • Dorsal block splint extends 10°/wk to full extension at 6-8 wk
  • Pilon = stability first, congruity second
  • Hemi-hamate CPT = 26746 + 20900 (NOT 26546)
  • Adjacent joint motion immediately to avoid DIP stiffness
  • Never tell a patient they’ll have full motion back

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What % of grasp motion comes from the PIP joint? A: 85%.
  2. Q: Describe the Elson test and how to interpret it. A: PIP held 90° flexed; patient extends against resistance; examiner tests DIP for resistance. Rigid DIP extension = positive = central slip rupture.
  3. Q: At what % of articular involvement does a dorsal PIP fracture-dislocation become unstable and require surgery? A: > 50% volar lip articular involvement is unstable; 30-50% is tenuous; < 30% usually stable.
  4. Q: What is the donor site for the hemi-hamate osteochondral autograft? A: Dorsal distal hamate — its concavity matches the volar lip cup of the middle phalanx base.
  5. Q: What is the major contraindication to volar plate arthroplasty (Eaton-Malerich)? A: Articular surface involvement > 50% (joint becomes unstable without bony buttress).
