Interns own: grind test vs Finkelstein (a centimeter apart), the tabletop test, and reading a SLAC wrist off the PA film.

Test yourself

  1. Stage SLAC wrist and give the operation matched to each stage. What is the key difference in SNAC?
  2. Stage thumb CMC arthritis (Eaton) and give the treatment ladder for stage III.
  3. A 65-year-old has a palmar cord to the ring finger: MCP 45 degrees, PIP 30 degrees. Compare collagenase, needle aponeurotomy, and fasciectomy for him, and name the cord that endangers the neurovascular bundle.
  4. A 24-year-old laborer has dorsal wrist pain; films show a sclerotic, collapsing lunate with neutral ulnar variance. Stage and treat by stage.
  5. Which deformities define the rheumatoid hand, and which tendon ruptures first in Vaughan-Jackson syndrome?
  6. Why does proximal row carpectomy stop working in SLAC/SNAC stage III, and what do you offer instead?
  7. When is wrist fusion the right answer over motion-preserving surgery?
Answers
  1. SLAC: I radial styloid-scaphoid, II entire radioscaphoid joint, III capitolunate/midcarpal (radiolunate spared until last). Stage I: styloidectomy ± SL treatment. Stage II: PRC or scaphoidectomy + four-corner fusion. Stage III: four-corner (PRC no longer possible with a diseased capitate head) or total wrist fusion. SNAC is the same cascade from a scaphoid nonunion; the proximal scaphoid-radius joint is preserved early because it stays reduced.
  2. Eaton I widening/synovitis, II narrowing with debris under 2 mm, III joint obliterated with larger osteophytes and STT spared, IV pantrapezial. Stage III ladder: CMC orthosis, activity modification, NSAIDs, injection; surgery when function fails despite that: trapeziectomy ± LRTI or suture-button suspensionplasty. The x-ray stage does not gate the injection.
  3. Collagenase: office-based, good at the MCP, weaker and less durable at the PIP, recurrence ~47% at 5 years. Needle aponeurotomy: fastest recovery, highest recurrence. Limited fasciectomy: best for PIP disease and durability (recurrence ~20-30%), at the cost of a real operation. Combined MCP + PIP disease favors fasciectomy; the PIP never fully corrects and say so up front. The spiral cord displaces the bundle central, volar, and proximal.
  4. Kienböck’s. Lichtman: I MRI-only, II sclerosis without collapse, IIIA collapse with normal carpal alignment, IIIB collapse with fixed scaphoid flexion, IV pancarpal arthritis. Treat: I-II immobilization trial then joint-leveling (radial shortening osteotomy for ulnar-negative wrists); IIIA leveling ± revascularization; IIIB PRC or scaphocapitate fusion; IV salvage (PRC if the capitate head is good, else fusion).
  5. Ulnar drift at the MCPs, swan-neck and boutonniere digits, caput ulnae at the DRUJ. Vaughan-Jackson: attritional extensor ruptures over the ulnar head, EDM/EDC small finger first, marching radially. Fix the wrist problem (Darrach/ulnar head) and transfer, or the next tendon ruptures too.
  6. PRC makes the capitate head the new articular partner of the lunate fossa; stage III means that capitate head is already arthritic, so the transplant surface is bad. Offer scaphoidectomy + four-corner fusion (preserves the radiolunate joint) or total wrist fusion.
  7. The high-demand laborer who needs painless power over motion, failed motion-preserving surgery, pancarpal disease including radiolunate, or the flail/neuropathic wrist. Position: ~10-15 degrees extension.
