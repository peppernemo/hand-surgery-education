Imaging of the Hand, Wrist, and Upper Extremity

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Radiography = initial modality of choice for suspected bony pathology (trauma, arthritis, congenital)
  • Minimum 2 orthogonal views; beam centered on capitate (wrist PA) or 3rd MC head (hand PA)
  • Adequate lateral wrist confirmed when pisiform volar surface lies midway between volar capitate and distal scaphoid pole

Workup

Imaging — Radiography

Standard Views

  • PA hand/wrist, lateral, oblique (30° pronated for dorsal/ulnar wrist; 30° supinated for pisotriquetral)
  • Shoulder requires true orthogonal: 40° posterior oblique AP + axillary lateral + scapular Y

Special Views

  • Scaphoid view: PA with 30° ulnar deviation — elongates scaphoid, improves sensitivity for nondisplaced fractures
  • Carpal tunnel view: supinated wrist dorsiflexed 30-35°, beam along 3rd MC axis
  • Stress views: Wolf thumb CMC stress view, clenched fist PA (SL widening)

Key Measurements

  • Scapholunate angle (lateral): normal 30-60°
  • Radiolunate angle: ~10° flexion normal (range: 15° flexion to 20° extension)
  • Lunocapitate angle: abnormal if > 15°
  • Gilula’s arcs (PA): Arc I = proximal row convexity, Arc II = distal proximal row concavity, Arc III = capitate/hamate convexity; disruption = joint integrity loss
  • Carpal height ratio: 0.54 ± 0.03
  • Ulnar variance: measure in neutral rotation (pronation lengthens, supination shortens); power grip PA uncovers dynamic positive variance

Elbow

  • AP + lateral + radial head view; lateral in flexion shows fat pads
  • Sail sign = elevated anterior fat pad = effusion
  • Posterior fat pad visible = effusion (normally hidden in olecranon fossa)
  • Raised fat pads + trauma + no visible fracture = suspect occult intra-articular fracture

Imaging — CT

  • Superior osseous detail; multiplanar reconstruction for complex fracture patterns
  • Scaphoid union: PPV 99%; reformat along scaphoid long axis for humpback deformity
  • Radiographs over/underestimate articular step-off in 30% of intra-articular DRFs
  • Gold standard for fracture healing when radiographs indeterminate
  • Radiation doses: 0.03 mSv (hand) to 2.06 mSv (shoulder) — well below 3 mSv annual background
  • CT arthrography more accurate than MRI/MR arthrography for partial SLIL and LTIL tears
  • For occult scaphoid fracture, CT confirms cortical injury but MRI is preferred — it directly evaluates marrow edema and associated soft tissue injury

Imaging — MRI

  • 3.0T = current standard of care (2x SNR vs 1.5T)
  • Superb contrast resolution for soft tissue, marrow, ligaments
  • Scaphoid AVN (13-50% incidence, predominantly proximal fifth): diffusely decreased T1 in proximal pole ≤ skeletal muscle signal = high specificity; dynamic gadolinium MRI is not superior to unenhanced
  • MR arthrography: gold standard for shoulder labroligamentous pathology; superior to conventional MRI for joint pathology (shoulder, elbow, wrist)
  • MR neurography (3.0T): better anatomic localization than EMG; abnormal nerve = focal enlargement, T2 hyperintensity, fascicular abnormality, course deviation
  • Titanium hardware preferred over stainless steel for MRI compatibility
  • Specialized positions: ABER (anteroinferior labrum), FABS (distal biceps), supination/pronation axial (ECU subsheath)

Imaging — Ultrasound

  • Flexor tendon laceration: 90% accuracy
  • Displaced thumb UCL tear: 100% accuracy
  • RC full-thickness tear: 88% accuracy (MRI 95%) — no statistically significant difference; meta-analysis: both > 90%
  • CTS: median nerve CSA at carpal tunnel inlet (pisiform level) = most sensitive/specific location; more accurate and cost-effective than NCS
  • Cubital tunnel: CSA cutoff 10 mm² → sensitivity 93%, specificity 98%
  • De Quervain’s EPB separate subcompartment reliably detected — risk factor for nonoperative failure
  • Sonoelastography: quantifies tissue stiffness; healthy tendons stiffer, diseased tendons softer

Imaging — Other

  • Bone scintigraphy: highly sensitive, low specificity; high NPV; positive results need MR/CT characterization
  • SPECT-CT: hybrid imaging increases accuracy over scintigraphy alone
  • Digital tomosynthesis: viable alternative for radiographically occult scaphoid fractures

Treatment

Image-Guided Intervention

  • US-guided arthrocentesis > blind, especially small hand joints
  • US-guided radiocarpal injection: 100% accuracy vs 76% palpation-guided (better DASH/Mayo/VAS)
  • Glenohumeral US-guided injection: 93% accuracy vs 73% landmark
  • US-guided barbotage for calcific tendinitis: minimal benefit vs subacromial steroid alone
  • Percutaneous sclerotherapy efficacious for low-flow vascular malformations
  • Ultrasound in experienced hands can replace MRI for many tendon and nerve pathologies — real-time, inexpensive, and no scheduling required

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Radiography first for bony injury; occult scaphoid fracture rate after negative initial XR = 9-33%
  • CT > MRI for cortical injury, but MRI best overall for occult fractures (marrow edema + soft tissue)
  • Ulnar variance must be measured in neutral rotation — pronation falsely lengthens the ulna
  • Negative imaging does not rule out the clinical diagnosis; positive imaging does not always correlate clinically
  • MRI is method of choice for arthritic joints (especially inflammatory) — early erosions, synovitis, marrow edema
  • Elbow MR arthrography is gold standard for the throwing athlete
  • CT arthrography is a valid alternative when MRI is contraindicated or has failed

Board-Style Questions (4)

  1. Q: What is the most sensitive and specific location for measuring median nerve CSA on ultrasound in suspected CTS? — A: Carpal tunnel inlet at the level of the pisiform.
  2. Q: A lateral wrist radiograph shows a scapholunate angle of 75°. What does this indicate? — A: DISI pattern (dorsal intercalated segment instability); normal SL angle is 30-60°.
  3. Q: Gilula’s arc II is disrupted on a PA wrist radiograph after a fall. What pathology should be suspected? — A: Carpal instability — perilunate dislocation or intercarpal ligament disruption.
  4. Q: What is the PPV of CT for scaphoid fracture union? — A: 99%.
