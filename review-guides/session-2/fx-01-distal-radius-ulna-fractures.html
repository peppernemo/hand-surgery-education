Distal Radius and Ulna Fractures

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Two articular facets (scaphoid + lunate); ridge between = membranous SL ligament insertion
  • Volar/ulnar bone densest → less comminution; ideal start point for ORIF reduction
  • Volar radiocarpal ligaments: RSC, LRL, SRL — short, parallel to radius long axis, resist ulnar translation
  • Dorsal extrinsic: dorsal radiotriquetral + dorsal intercarpal — oblique, V-shape over dorsal triquetral tubercle
  • DRUJ stability = 20% bone + 80% soft tissue; volar + dorsal radioulnar ligaments → fovea + ulnar styloid
  • Distal oblique bundle of IOM present in 40% — additional DRUJ stabilizer (Moritomo)
  • TFCC thickness inversely proportional to ulnar positive variance
  • Coronal shift of distal radius predicts DRUJ instability more reliably than ulnar styloid fracture

Classification

  • AO/OTA: A (extra-articular), B (partial articular), C (complete articular)
  • Melone: based on fracture lines through scaphoid facet + lunate facet (± coronal split)
  • Column theory (Rikli/Regazzoni): radial (scaphoid facet + radial half), intermediate (lunate facet + sigmoid notch), ulnar (distal ulna + TFC) — reconstruct intermediate first
  • Volar marginal (volar ulnar corner) fragment = unstable, radiocarpal + DRUJ subluxation risk → always anatomic ORIF

Workup

Exam

  • Open wound near apex of deformity
  • Acute CTS vs nerve contusion: contusion = immediate fixed deficit; acute CTS = evolving pain + paresthesia
  • DRUJ stability check: always compare with contralateral side (clinic + preop)

Imaging

  • AP, lateral, oblique — measure ulnar variance, radial inclination (22°), volar tilt (11°), sigmoid notch obliquity
  • Volar teardrop angle (Medoff) — confirms volar lunate facet reduction
  • CT after reduction for articular or distal displaced fractures + comminution + associated carpal fractures

Treatment

Initial — All Fractures

  • Hematoma block with 1% lidocaine (rapid onset, resolves for post-reduction exam)
  • Traction 8-10 lb with finger traps; palmar translation of neutral lunate is key
  • Padded 3-point molded sugar-tong splint, wrist neutral
  • One concerted reduction attempt; repeated manipulation causes harm without benefit in elderly

AAOS Surgical Indications

  • Articular displacement > 2 mm
  • Radial shortening > 3 mm
  • Dorsal angulation > 10°
  • Loss of radial inclination > 5°
  • Plus: volar ulnar corner fragment, sigmoid notch involvement, associated fractures

Non-operative

  • Cast 3-4 wk → removable splint by wk 4 if alignment stable
  • Pad DRSN + DUCN; avoid extreme flexion; ensure digital ROM
  • Weekly films first 3-4 wk
  • Elderly low-demand patient: gentle attempt at reduction acceptable; deformity well tolerated
  • Combined distal radius + ulna fracture in elderly → consider surgery (loss of strut → significant deformity/nonunion)

Operative

Volar (FCR) Approach — Workhorse

  • 8 cm incision over FCR
  • Protect LABC nerve (radial), PCBM nerve (ulnar; may travel within FCR sheath or superficial — inspect fat carefully)
  • Release distal BR insertion to reduce deforming force
  • PQ released radially + distally
  • Extended FCR (Orbay): best visualization of volar ulnar corner
  • Plate proximal to watershed line; distal pegs 75% of volar-dorsal distance
  • Intraop fluoro views: AP, lateral, tilt views (10° lunate, 30° scaphoid), pronated + supinated oblique, dorsal tangential, near-axial sigmoid
  • Subtract 1-2 mm from drill measurement to avoid dorsal prominence

Dorsal Midline

  • For complex articular fractures requiring direct joint visualization
  • Protect SBRN + SBUN; 3rd compartment incision to retract EPL (transpose at closure)
  • PIN neurectomy + ligament-sparing capsulotomy
  • 2.0-2.4 mm dorsal plates; low profile, subperiosteal

Fragment-Specific (Medoff)

  • Multiple small plates form fixed-angle triangular constructs (plate proximal screws + K-wire through distal hole into opposite cortex)
  • Excellent for highly comminuted; orthogonal plating common

External Fixator / Dorsal Bridge Plate

  • Severe comminution, soft tissue compromise, polytrauma needing weight bearing
  • Index MC pins (2.5 mm Shanz; first at metaphyseal flare, second distal parallel)
  • Radius pins through ECRL/ECRB interval (bare area, 4 fingerbreadths proximal to styloid)
  • Check extrinsic tightness at end (passive IP flexion shouldn’t extend MCPs = no over-distraction)
  • Bridge plate removed at ~3 mo via 180° twist + screw removal

Arthroscopy (del Piñal — dry technique)

  • 6R viewing portal (stable platform on intact ulna)
  • After provisional volar plate + K-wire fixation
  • Direct visualization of articular reduction; treat concomitant ligament injuries

DRUJ Management

  • Examine contralateral side preop AND in OR after radius fixation
  • Ulnar styloid fixation NOT routine after volar plate ORIF unless clinical DRUJ instability
  • Options if unstable: foveal TFCC repair, ulnar styloid ORIF (hook plate / tension band), DRUJ cross-pinning, supination/neutral splinting
  • If cross-pinning DRUJ: 0.062” K-wires, 4 cortices, neutral rotation, exit pin both sides of skin so a broken pin can still be retrieved

Distal Ulna

  • Reduced + aligned ulna can heal without internal fixation when radius stable on volar plate
  • Unstable displaced ulnar head/neck → 2.0-2.4 mm plate (ulnar or volar position)
  • Preserve ulnar head; resection = salvage

Post-op / Rehab

  • Volar plate: protected wrist motion + grip strengthening early
  • External fixator removed ~6 wk in clinic
  • Bridge plate at ~3 mo
  • Maximum recovery 6-12 mo from injury

Complications & Outcomes

Major Complications

  • Acute CTS — urgent release (distinguish from contusion — contusion is non-progressive)
  • EPL rupture — most common after nonop, esp minimally displaced; treat with EIP-to-EPL transfer
  • Flexor tendon rupture (FPL, index FDP) — too-distal volar plate (past watershed); use US to monitor (small plate-to-tendon ratio = urgent removal)
  • Malunion — radial shortening + dorsal angulation most common
  • Nonunion — rare; workup metabolic/mechanical/infection; vitamin D, smoking cessation
  • CRPS 3-10% — aggressive rehab, neuropathic meds, sympathetic blocks, CTR
  • Ligament injuries: SL 4.7-46%, LT 12-34%, TFCC 39-82% (peripheral with ulnar styloid usually doesn’t need repair)

Outcomes (RCTs)

  • Surgical vs non-op in > 65 yo: better radiographs with surgery, similar function at 1 yr, more complications with surgery
  • Pinning vs ex-fix: no difference
  • Volar plate vs pinning: better DASH at 3 mo, equal at 1 yr
  • Bridging vs non-bridging ex-fix: McQueen showed non-bridging better; later studies equivocal
  • AAOS CPG: unable to recommend one technique over another

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Don’t miss volar marginal fragment — primary indication for ulnar-side approach (between flexor tendons + FCU)
  • Coronal shift must be corrected to restore sigmoid notch + DRUJ tension
  • BR tendon release dramatically reduces deforming force
  • Hardware too distal (within 3 mm of volar lip) = flexor rupture risk
  • Watch for prominent dorsal screws — Lister tubercle blocks them on standard lateral; use dorsal tangential view
  • Younger active patients benefit most from surgery; very low-demand elderly accept deformity well
  • Combined distal radius + ulna fracture in elderly = consider surgery despite age (loss of strut → high deformity + nonunion risk)

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What are the AAOS surgical indications for distal radius fractures? A: > 2 mm articular step-off, > 3 mm shortening, > 10° dorsal angulation, > 5° loss of radial inclination.
  2. Q: Which tendon ruptures most commonly after nonoperative treatment of minimally displaced distal radius fracture? Treatment? A: EPL — EIP-to-EPL transfer (attritional, can’t primary repair).
  3. Q: What is the watershed line and why does it matter? A: Distal-most ridge of volar radius; plate placement distal to it predisposes to flexor tendon rupture (esp FPL, index FDP).
  4. Q: When is ulnar styloid fixation indicated after volar plate ORIF of distal radius? A: Only when clinical DRUJ instability after radius fixation; routine styloid fixation not needed.
  5. Q: What’s the most reliable radiographic predictor of DRUJ instability? A: Coronal plane shift of the distal radius (not ulnar styloid fracture presence).
