Thumb CMC (Basal Joint) Arthritis

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Reciprocal concave saddle joint; lacks bony congruity → ligament-dependent stability
  • 16 ligaments; 7 primary stabilizers
  • Current view: DRL + POL = primary stabilizers (not the AOL/“beak ligament” as historically taught)
    • DRL (dorsoradial ligament): thickest, strongest, shortest; restrains dorsal migration in flexion-adduction
    • POL = posterior oblique
  • AOL histology = loose passive tissue; DRL histology = dense organized active tissue
  • AOL degeneration = secondary, not causative
  • Joint compression force at TMC = 12× tip pinch force (Cooney + Chao)
  • Degeneration starts volar → progresses dorsally; dorsal joint spared even in advanced disease

Epidemiology

  • 1 in 4 women / 1 in 12 men develop radiographic disease
  • F:M = 10:1
  • Higher in Caucasian > Asian (flatter Asian hand surface)
  • ↑ prevalence with age

Workup

Clinical

  • Postmenopausal female with radial-sided thumb/hand pain, no trauma
  • Pain with pinching, grasping, keys, writing, sewing
  • CTS coexists in 26-29% (up to 46-80% in women, diabetics, workers’ comp)

Exam

  • Advanced: adduction contracture + dorsal subluxation; CMC prominence (synovitis + osteophytes)
  • Palpate CMC dorsal to APL insertion (vs STT — palmar over scaphoid tuberosity)
  • Rule out de Quervain’s (Finkelstein), FCR tendonitis (palmar trapezial ridge), trigger thumb
  • Trapezial dysplasia in young females: instability without arthritis from increased trapezial articular slope

Provocative Tests

  • Crank test: axial load + passive flex/ext of MC → pain + crepitus
  • Grind test: axial load + rotation of MC through flexed MCP → pain + crepitus
  • Adduction-extension test (Gelberman): specific for OA — pain with adduction to plane of index MC OR extension to plane of palm
  • Laxity exam: compare bidirectional translation to contralateral

Imaging

  • Robert’s view (hyperpronated AP, dorsal thumb on plate) — visualizes all 4 trapezial facets
  • CMC view, lateral, oblique
  • Stress views (Wolf — thumbs together with nails parallel)

Classification — Eaton + Littler

Stage Findings
I Normal/widened joint (synovitis); subluxation on stress
II Narrowing; osteophytes < 2 mm
III Further narrowing + subchondral sclerosis; osteophytes > 2 mm; STT NORMAL
IV Pantrapezial (CMC + STT)
  • Poor correlation clinical:radiographic — treat by symptoms, not film

Treatment

Nonoperative

  • Activity modification + thumb proprioception/strengthening = effective
  • Splinting: hand-based opponens, IP free
    • Marotta meta-analysis: long custom splint best for pain; short custom best for function
    • Initial near-full-time × 3-4 wk → wear during exacerbations
  • NSAIDs: insufficient evidence
  • Steroid injection: NO DIFFERENCE vs saline
  • HA, PRP, autologous fat: insufficient evidence
  • Counsel patients before any CSI: RCT evidence shows no difference vs saline; many patients still report symptomatic relief

Barrera: CSI = Kenalog 10 mg/mL + 1% lido (never Depo).

Operative

Stage I — Ligament Reconstruction

  • Indication: instability + minimal erosion
  • Rule out trapezial dysplasia first (soft tissue alone insufficient → bony reconstruction needed)
Eaton-Littler
  • Volar incision; protect sensory branches + radial artery
  • Drill hole through MC base perpendicular to thumbnail
  • ½ FCR harvested at wrist (radial half = ulnar half in forearm due to rotation)
  • Pass through hole, suture to dorsal MC periosteum under tension; pronate + abduct + palmarly reduce MC base
  • Pass remainder under APL → under other half of FCR → loop back, suture to itself
  • 4 wk thumb spica
DRL Imbrication
  • Dorsal approach; identify thickened DRL
  • 2.5 mm suture anchor OR bone tunnel → advance DRL
  • Reduce thumb in extension + palmar abduction

Stages II-IV — Trapeziectomy Family

Many procedures with ~90-95% good-to-excellent results — no single technique clearly superior

Trapeziectomy Alone (Gervis)
  • Risk: proximal migration → weakness + instability
  • Pain relief comparable to complex procedures in recent series
LRTI (Burton + Pellegrini 1986)
  • Trapeziectomy + FCR through MC drill hole reconstructs beak + interposes
  • 9.4-yr outcomes: grip ↑ 92.5%, pinch ↑ 50%, 95% excellent pain relief, 11-13% arthroplasty space loss
Thompson APL Suspensionplasty
  • Trapeziectomy + APL dorsal slip → MC tunnel → 2nd MC tunnel → ECRL weave
Weilby Suspensionplasty
  • ½ FCR woven between APL + remaining FCR; grip 80% normal, durable
Suture Suspensionplasty (DelSignore-Accardi)
  • No graft needed; 91% satisfaction
Suture Button (Mini TightRope)
  • 1st → 2nd MC base suspension; radiolucent suture

LRTI Technique Pearls

  • Approach: Wagner volar (junction glabrous/non-glabrous) or dorsal
  • Trapezium excised completely (protect FCR)
  • Minimal MC articular resection (more dorsal than volar)
  • Drill hole: dorsal cortex 1 cm distal to articular surface, aim proximal + ulnar
  • Suture thumb reduced without excessive traction (avoid proximal migration)
  • EPB to proximal MC augments abduction + minimizes MCP hyperextension
  • Short-arm thumb spica × 1 mo; ROM at 1 mo; strengthening 3 mo; full activity 3 mo; continued improvement 1 yr

Implant Arthroplasty / Total Joint Replacement

  • Concept: less postop pain, shorter rehab, improved strength
  • Failed designs: silicone (synovitis), Gore-Tex (synovitis), metal-on-metal Motec (42% 2-yr revision), Artelon (foreign body)
  • Newer promising:
    • ARPE (Biomet): 92.7% 5-yr survival
    • MAIA: 88% survival at 12 yr, 5% dislocations
    • Touch (Kerimedical): good 3-yr results
  • Raj systematic review: prosthesis better function + strength vs trapeziectomy; similar pain; higher complications + revisions
  • Inadequate if STT also arthritic
  • Failed implant → secondary trapeziectomy = comparable to primary

Arthrodesis

  • For young manual laborers, Stage III/early IV, tetraplegic reconstruction
  • Bamberger 39 fusions: 5 delayed unions, 3 nonunions, 72% ↓ ab/adduction, 61% ↓ flex/ext, 20° STT progression in 2/12
  • Position: 30-40° abduction, 15-20° extension, 10° pronation (distal phalanx rests on middle phalanx of clenched fist)

TMC Denervation

  • Minimally invasive; fast pain relief; early RTW
  • Targets: articular branch SBR, palmar cutaneous median articular branch, thenar branch of median, Cruveilhier LABC branch

Post-op / Rehab

  • LRTI: thumb spica 4 wk → IP ROM immediate, MCP/CMC at 1 mo, strengthening 3 mo, full activity 3 mo
  • TMC arthroplasty (implant): immediate ROM, full activity 6 wk
  • Continued improvement up to 1 yr

Complications & Outcomes

Persistent Pain (most common)

  • Typical 3-6 mo resolution
  • Hypertrophic scar / cutaneous neuroma → desensitization
  • CRPS → pain management consult
  • Proximal migration after LRTI difficult to revise

MCP Hyperextension

  • ≤ 30° painless → no treatment

  • 30° or painful → palmar capsulodesis (volar plate imbrication) + EPB transfer + K-wire 3-6 wk, OR fusion if collateral instability + arthritis

Implant

  • De Quervain’s, cup loosening, dislocation; revision < 10%

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • DRL + POL = primary stabilizers (NOT AOL despite traditional teaching)
  • Steroid injection = no better than saline (counsel pre-injection)
  • Treat by symptoms, not radiograph (poor correlation)
  • LRTI = 90-95% good-to-excellent at 9-yr; long-term gold standard
  • Implants promising but more complications — long-term data still developing
  • Always check for coexisting CTS, de Quervain’s, trigger thumb
  • Rule out trapezial dysplasia before soft tissue-only reconstruction
  • Robert’s view = essential first film
  • Hemi-hamate CPT for PIP and stem fixation CPT for CMC are distinct — coding accuracy matters

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: Which ligament is the primary stabilizer of the thumb CMC joint per current evidence? A: The dorsoradial ligament (DRL), with contribution from the POL — NOT the anterior oblique (AOL/“beak”) despite historic teaching.
  2. Q: What does the Eaton-Littler stage IV designation indicate? A: Pantrapezial arthritis — both CMC AND STT joints involved.
  3. Q: Per RCT data, what is the efficacy of corticosteroid injection for CMC OA? A: No difference vs saline placebo.
  4. Q: Which is the workhorse procedure for advanced CMC arthritis and what are its long-term outcomes? A: LRTI (Burton-Pellegrini); 9.4-yr outcomes include 92.5% grip improvement, 95% excellent pain relief, 11-13% arthroplasty space loss.
  5. Q: What is the ideal position for thumb CMC arthrodesis? A: 30-40° abduction, 15-20° extension, ~10° pronation (distal phalanx rests on middle phalanx of clenched fist).
