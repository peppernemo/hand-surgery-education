Topic 1 · Nerve I: Compression

Carpal tunnel · cubital tunnel · radial tunnel, PIN & Wartenberg · Guyon’s · pronator/AIN

Carpal tunnel syndrome is the single most-tested hand item on the OITE (8% of all hand questions by itself), and compression neuropathy is the highest-volume elective work in hand surgery. Know the sites, the exam findings, and the operative decision points for all three nerves.

Interns own: the CTS exam (Durkan, Phalen, Tinel), the CTS-6 items, and recognizing acute carpal tunnel syndrome after trauma as an emergency.

Test yourself

  1. Walk through the CTS-6: the six items and the cutoff, and what the score means for ordering electrodiagnostics.
  2. A 55-year-old with a year of nocturnal median-territory numbness, positive Durkan, early thenar atrophy, CTS-6 of 18. Do you need EDX, and what do you offer?
  3. Name the five sites of ulnar nerve compression at the elbow, proximal to distal, and grade this patient: intrinsic atrophy, positive Froment and Wartenberg.
  4. When do you transpose the ulnar nerve instead of decompressing in situ?
  5. A 40-year-old has lateral elbow pain with resisted supination and tenderness 4 cm distal to the lateral epicondyle, normal EDX. Compare this with the patient who has weak finger extension and radial wrist deviation on extension. Name both syndromes and their management.
  6. How do you distinguish AIN palsy, high median nerve palsy, and CTS on exam alone?
  7. A distal-radius-fracture patient develops progressive median numbness and escalating pain 6 hours after reduction. Diagnosis and next step?
Answers
  1. Median-territory numbness 3.5 · nocturnal numbness 4.0 · thenar weakness/atrophy 5.0 · Phalen 5.0 · 2PD loss 4.5 · Tinel 4.0; maximum 26. Score above 12 = ~80% probability of CTS; with a consistent picture, EDX is not required before offering release. Twelve or less, get EDX.
  2. No EDX needed (CTS-6 of 18 with a classic picture). Offer carpal tunnel release; open and endoscopic are equivalent long term. Atrophy argues for earlier surgery, and counsel that dense numbness and thenar bulk recover slowest and may be incomplete.
  3. Arcade of Struthers, medial intermuscular septum, retrocondylar groove, Osborne’s ligament between the FCU heads, deep FCU fascia. Atrophy with motor deficit = McGowan III: operate.
  4. Subluxing nerve, revision surgery, or structural compression; otherwise in situ decompression is the modern default (trial data show equivalence for primary idiopathic cases).
  5. First patient: radial tunnel syndrome, pain without motor loss, normal EDX common; treat with activity modification, then release for refractory cases. Second: PIN palsy, a motor syndrome (finger drop, radial deviation because ECRL survives); look for a compressive lesion (lipoma, ganglion) and decompress if no recovery. Wartenberg’s is the third radial entity: purely sensory, superficial radial nerve irritation.
  6. AIN: no OK sign (FPL, index FDP), no sensory loss. High median: same motor loss plus FDS/PT/FCR and sensory loss in the radial 3.5 digits including the thenar base. CTS: sensory symptoms sparing the thenar base (palmar cutaneous branch runs over the ligament), thenar motor only, no extrinsic weakness.
  7. Acute carpal tunnel syndrome. Emergent carpal tunnel release; this is pressure, not neurapraxia, and progression over hours is the tell. Splint out of flexion and take down the tight dressing while booking the OR.
