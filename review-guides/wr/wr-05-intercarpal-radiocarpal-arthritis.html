Intercarpal and Radiocarpal Arthritis

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Scaphoid = “intercarpal connecting rod” between proximal and distal rows (Gilford 1943)
  • SLAC pattern (Watson 1984): SL injury → scaphoid volar flexion + lunate extension → progressive degenerative cascade
    • Stage I: radial styloid–scaphoid
    • Stage II: scaphoid fossa
    • Stage III: capitolunate (capitate/hamate migration into lunate fossa)
    • Stage IV: pancarpal
    • Radiolunate articulation typically spared (congruous hemisphere) — basis for 4CF
  • SNAC (scaphoid nonunion advanced collapse): analogous pattern from scaphoid nonunion
  • Other etiologies: inflammatory arthritis, LT injury, Kienböck, posttraumatic, OA

Workup

History

  • Pain, loss of motion, progressive wrist dysfunction
  • Timing of pain: morning → inflammatory; evening → posttraumatic/OA
  • Prior wrist trauma (may be remote), previous injections, smoking history

Exam

  • Point tenderness localization, provocative maneuvers (Watson shift, LT ballottement, intercarpal shear, compression)
  • Evaluate DRUJ, tendon pathology, neuropathy, pisotriquetral joint, thumb CMC/STT joints
  • Grip strength comparison

Imaging

  • PA, lateral, oblique sufficient for most patients
  • CT: bone quality assessment; MRI: specific articulation involvement (motion-sparing decision-making)
  • Direct inspection at surgery (open or arthroscopic) remains superior to all imaging

Treatment

Non-operative

  • First-line regardless of stage: splinting, PT (strengthening + stabilization), activity modification, CSI
  • Trial of 3-6 months before surgical intervention

Barrera: CSI = Kenalog 10 mg/mL + 1% lido (never Depo).

  • Intra-articular injections can be both diagnostic and therapeutic — complete but temporary relief confirms the pain generator and justifies proceeding to the appropriate motion-sparing procedure.

Proximal Row Carpectomy (PRC)

  • Indications: SLAC I-II, perilunate sequelae, Kienböck — with preserved proximal capitate and lunate fossa cartilage
  • Contraindications: stage III-IV SLAC/SNAC, capitate head AVN, RSC ligament insufficiency (ulnar carpal translation)
  • Capitate morphology matters: type II lunate (hamate facet) → V-shaped or spherical capitate → greater mismatch with lunate fossa → higher degeneration rate
  • Technique: dorsal approach, ligament-sparing capsulotomy, PIN neurectomy; remove scaphoid first (assess RSC integrity)
  • No radial styloidectomy (protects RSC ligament origin)
  • Post-op: volar orthosis 3-6 weeks → PT at 3 months
  • Outcomes: > 85% survival free of revision at 20 years; progressive radiocapitate arthritis (does not correlate with clinical deterioration)
  • Diseased capitate options: capsular interposition (Eaton), OATS plug from lunate/triquetrum, pyrocarbon resurfacing

4-Corner Arthrodesis (4CF) with Scaphoid Excision

  • Indications: SLAC/SNAC all stages, preserved radiolunate articulation, capitate head AVN, RSC incompetency
  • Contraindications: radiolunate arthrosis, Kienböck, active infection
  • Must correct DISI (flex wrist over towel → neutral lunate rotation → radiolunate pin)
  • Fixation: headless compression screws or recessed dorsal circular plate
  • Post-op: cast 6-8 weeks; no > 5 lb until 12 weeks
  • 3-corner / lunocapitate (LC) arthrodesis: fewer bones in fusion mass → lower nonunion risk; excise triquetrum → easier reduction + eliminates pisotriquetral arthritis source

PRC vs 4CF Decision-Making

  • PRC preferred: preserved proximal capitate, Kienböck, smokers (no fusion to fail)
  • 4CF preferred: preserved proximal lunate, stages III-IV SLAC/SNAC, capitate AVN, RSC incompetency
  • PRC: better motion; 4CF: better grip strength; conversion to total wrist fusion: 4CF 10% vs PRC 5%
  • 4CF: 10-20% higher complication rate (nonunion, radiocarpal impingement)

Radioscapholunate (RSL) Arthrodesis

  • Indication: radiolunate ± radioscaphoid arthrosis with spared midcarpal joints
  • Distal scaphoidectomy improves flexion/radial deviation + union rate (0% vs 5% nonunion)
  • Dart-thrower’s motion generally preserved
  • Progressive radiographic midcarpal arthrosis does not correlate with clinical deterioration

Partial Wrist Denervation

  • PIN + AIN neurectomy through small dorsal incision; excise 1-2 cm of each
  • 85% satisfaction; 90% would choose same treatment again
  • Option for pancarpal arthritis where comorbidities preclude major surgery
  • Excise AIN as distal as possible to avoid pronator quadratus denervation

Total Wrist Arthrodesis

  • Gold standard for pancarpal arthritis; slight extension + ulnar deviation
  • Precontoured dorsal plate, 3rd MC → radius; fix distally first
  • PRC can be added to reduce fusion joints + provide autograft
  • Nonunion < 5%; tendon irritation ~15% (most common reoperation)
  • “Functional wrist ROM” for ADLs: 35° flex-ext, 25° radial-ulnar deviation — these are lost

Total Wrist Arthroplasty (TWA)

  • 4th-generation implants (Universal 2, ReMotion, Maestro): 5-yr survival > 90%, complications < 10%
  • Best for low-demand, older patients with pancarpal arthritis ± ipsilateral elbow/shoulder arthritis
  • Goal: ~70° arc of motion, balanced stability
  • 10-yr survival: 92% (revision as endpoint), 75% (including radiographic loosening)

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Radiolunate articulation is usually spared in SLAC/SNAC — always inspect it
  • RSC ligament integrity is critical for PRC; if damaged, convert to 4CF or pin
  • For 4CF, correct the DISI deformity before final fixation — otherwise impingement is inevitable
  • Smokers have higher nonunion risk with any arthrodesis — PRC may be safer
  • Type I lunate + type I capitate = best PRC candidates (smallest radius mismatch)
  • Denervation is an underutilized, minimally invasive option for any wrist arthritis

Board-Style Questions (3-5)

  1. Q: In SLAC arthritis, which articulation is typically spared and serves as the basis for 4-corner arthrodesis? — A: The radiolunate articulation.
  2. Q: What is the most important structure to preserve during proximal row carpectomy to prevent ulnar carpal translation? — A: The radioscaphocapitate (RSC) ligament.
  3. Q: A patient with stage II SLAC wrist, type I lunate, preserved proximal capitate, and a 30 pack-year smoking history. PRC or 4CF? — A: PRC (smoker → higher nonunion risk with 4CF; preserved capitate + type I lunate = ideal PRC candidate).
  4. Q: What two nerves are excised in partial wrist denervation? — A: Posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) and anterior interosseous nerve (AIN).
  5. Q: What is the nonunion rate after total wrist arthrodesis? — A: Less than 5%.
