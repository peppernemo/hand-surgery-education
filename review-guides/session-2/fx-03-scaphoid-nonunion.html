Scaphoid Nonunion

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Overall scaphoid fracture nonunion rate ~15%; proximal pole fractures nonunite at ~34% without operative fixation (Eastley meta-analysis — matches FX-02). Once a proximal pole nonunion is established, persistent nonunion despite open grafting + fixation = up to ~68% (Ramamurthy 2007) — drives the case for early definitive ORIF rather than salvage grafting later.
  • 97% of untreated nonunions → SNAC (Mack, Ruby, Inoue)
  • Retrograde blood supply via dorsal ridge perforators from radial artery; minor volar tubercle supply from superficial palmar branch
  • RSC ligament = fulcrum for humpback deformity at waist nonunion
  • 75% of scaphoid covered in cartilage; dorsal ridge = non-articular bone (osteophyte/impingement site)
  • Dorsal ridge osteophytes → loss of wrist extension (classic clinical sign of waist nonunion)

Biomechanics

  • Untreated waist nonunion: distal pole flexes + pronates + ulnar deviates; proximal pole + lunate extend = DISI pattern
  • Oka 3D CT: nonunions distal to dorsal SL fibers → 36° proximal extension + 19° supination, lunate 18° + 6°, triquetrum 12° + 3°, dorsal translation 3.1 mm scaphoid + 2.6 mm capitate
  • Proximal pole nonunions = no humpback (proximal to dorsal SL fibers → distal pole doesn’t flex)
  • Amadio: lateral intrascaphoid angle > 45° → 54% DJD

Workup

Exam

  • Loss of wrist extension; pain doing push-ups; snuff box + volar tubercle tenderness
  • Watson shift; midcarpal instability with forced ulnar deviation
  • Giugale: wrist extension increases palpable volar scaphoid surface area 168%
  • Mallee meta-analysis: snuff box sens 0.87-1.00; Parvizi combined tests → spec 74%, sens 100%

Imaging

  • AP, lateral, oblique, navicular (AP ulnar deviation)
  • CT in scaphoid long axis (sagittal reformat) = standard preop:
    1. Precise location
    2. Bone loss + cystic erosions
    3. Deformity (lateral intrascaphoid angle, height-to-length)
    4. Sclerosis of proximal pole
  • MRI: T1 dark marrow = AVN; Rancy challenges whether AVN actually impacts outcomes
  • 50% bridging trabeculae on CT = healed (Guss)
  • 16-27% of scaphoid fractures have normal initial radiographs

Classification

  • By location: distal pole, waist, proximal pole — drives surgical approach + graft type
  • By chronicity: fibrous (nascent) → established nonunion with sclerosis/cysts
  • By deformity: humpback present/absent
  • Nakamura 4 failure predictors: > 5 years from injury, proximal pole, proximal sclerosis, poor scaphoid/carpal reduction

Treatment

Indications

  • Active symptomatic patient with confirmed nonunion
  • Humpback deformity, bone loss, prior failed surgery, AVN

Contraindications (relative)

  • Significant medical comorbidities, uncontrolled CRPS, fixed carpal malalignment, advanced SNAC, asymptomatic radiographic malunion in low-demand patient
  • Active smoking

Approach by Location

  • Waist with humpback → volar (FCR extended) + structural graft + retrograde or antegrade screw
  • Proximal pole → dorsal + cancellous or vascularized inlay + antegrade screw
  • Distal pole → volar

Volar Approach

  • FCR-radial artery interval; preserve RSC ligament insertion on proximal pole
  • Debride to healthy bone; reduce with ulnar deviation + distal pole supination
  • Laminar spreader for severe bone loss
  • Provisional fixation: central guidewire ± K-wires capturing both poles; pin proximal pole to lunate / distal to capitate

Dorsal Approach

  • Longitudinal incision, just distal + ulnar to Lister
  • Release EPL; retract 4th compartment ulnarly
  • Berger ligament-sparing capsulotomy
  • Wrist flexion exposes proximal pole
  • Limit distal dissection to preserve dorsal capsular blood supply
  • Use wider reamer or “micro” screw for tenuous proximal pole (avoid comminution)

Bone Graft Options

Non-vascularized (workhorse)

  • Cancellous from distal radius (local; ideal — backfill donor with allograft/substitute to prevent fracture)
  • Corticocancellous wedge (Russe/Fernandez) for humpback
  • Iliac crest if larger structural graft needed

Vascularized Pedicled

Flap Pedicle Use
1,2 ICSRA (Zaidemberg) Branch between 1st and 2nd compartments Proximal pole or rotated volar for waist (with limited styloidectomy < 5-6 mm)
Volar carpal artery Distal edge of PQ from radial artery Volar wedge for waist humpback
Dorsal capsular (Sotereanos) Capsule-based, posterior interosseous extension Proximal pole, esp with AVN; robust hardy pedicle, no specific artery dissection

Free Vascularized

  • MFC / MFT — descending genicular artery (DGA) off SFA
  • MFT mimics proximal pole curvature (osteochondral)
  • Doi: 100% union for waist nonunion with humpback
  • Aibinder: MFC 89% union at 16 wk vs 1,2 ICSRA 79% at 26 wk vs iliac crest 71% at 19 wk

Fixation Options

  • Headless compression screw (gold standard); 4 steps: guidewire → measure → ream → insert
  • Two screws for additional bending stability (Garcia)
  • Scaphocapitate K-wire (0.062”) or screw to block midcarpal flexion forces
  • Volar scaphoid plate (Dodds) — for recalcitrant nonunions, buttress against flexion, supports volar wedge graft

NOT Recommended

  • BMP-2/OP-1 abandoned — Brannan: unacceptably high HO + wrist stiffness rates
  • Dorsal inlay graft for humpback (no structural support → failure)

Arthroscopic Repair (Oh, Slade)

  • Comparable union + functional outcomes vs open
  • Preserves extrinsic ligaments + capsular vascularity
  • Best for fibrous nonunions or those without humpback/bone loss

Salvage

  • Distal scaphoid pole excision (Malerich): 17/19 favorable at 15 yr — good option in chronic nonunion with radioscaphoid arthrosis

Surgical Planning Imaging

  • Preop CT in scaphoid long axis is mandatory — defines location, bone loss, deformity, sclerosis
  • Intraop fluoro views: AP, AP+UD, lateral, semipronated, semisupinated

Post-op / Rehab

  • Short-arm orthosis 10-14 d
  • Cast extension to 6 wk total common
  • CT at ~3 mo for ≥ 50% bridging before RTW heavy lifting / RTP for athletes
  • Scaphoid heals by direct bone healing (intrasynovial bone) — minimal callus

Complications & Outcomes

  • Persistent nonunion, infection, hardware failure, CRPS, progression to SNAC
  • Slade: treatment within 6 mo of original injury = significantly higher union (minimal bone loss/sclerosis)
  • Fernandez: 30/37 with mild-moderate DJD at 6 yr after wedge graft, but none progressed to SNAC after union
  • Pinder systematic review (48 studies, 1,602 pts): no statistical difference:
    • Screws 88%, K-wires 92%, no internal fixation 79% union
    • Sample sizes underpowered — would need 3,400 patients for 80% power
  • Alluri vascularized graft meta-analysis: 92% complete satisfaction, MMWS + DASH improved 53% + 82%

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Volar wedge graft for humpback; dorsal inlay graft for proximal pole — match graft to deformity
  • Don’t reduce screw → fragment small proximal pole; use wider reamer or micro screw
  • AVN on MRI does NOT predict outcome (Rancy) — don’t let it drive decision
  • BMP-2 = avoid
  • Limited radial styloidectomy < 5-6 mm preserves RSC ligament
  • Treat early — < 6 mo from injury has highest union rate
  • For chronic nonunion with isolated radioscaphoid arthrosis, distal pole excision is a low-morbidity salvage

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What’s the rate of persistent nonunion after operative grafting + fixation for an established proximal pole scaphoid nonunion? A: ~68% (Ramamurthy 2007) — drives the case for early definitive ORIF when the fracture is still fresh, rather than salvaging the nonunion later.
  2. Q: Why is a volar wedge graft preferred over dorsal inlay for waist nonunion with humpback? A: Volar wedge provides structural support against flexion deformity; dorsal inlay doesn’t and tends to fail.
  3. Q: What four factors predict failure of scaphoid nonunion surgery (Nakamura)? A: > 5 yr from injury, proximal pole, proximal sclerosis, poor scaphoid/carpal reduction.
  4. Q: Which vascularized graft uses the descending genicular artery and has the highest union rate? A: Medial femoral condyle (MFC) free flap — Aibinder reported 89% at 16 wk.
  5. Q: What two adjunctive procedures stabilize the scaphoid against humpback flexion forces? A: Volar scaphoid plate (Dodds) and scaphocapitate pinning/screw.
