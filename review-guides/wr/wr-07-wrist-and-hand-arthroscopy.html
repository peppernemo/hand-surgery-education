Wrist and Hand Arthroscopy

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Wrist arthroscopy = gold standard for diagnosing intra-articular and ligamentous conditions
  • Confined space requires precise portal placement — being a few mm off limits access + damages cartilage
  • Traction: 4.5-5.5 kg (10-12 lb) via finger traps; apply traction BEFORE drawing portal landmarks

Key Arthroscopic Anatomy

  • TFCC components: central articular disc, dorsal + volar RULs (superficial → styloid, deep → fovea), ulnocarpal ligaments, meniscus homologue, UCL, ECU subsheath floor
  • Peripheral 10-40% of articular disc is vascularized (ulnar artery branches) — peripheral tears CAN heal
  • Prestyloid recess: normal synovial pouch — do not confuse with peripheral tear
  • Ligament of Testut (radioscapholunate): vestigial neurovascular structure, not a true stabilizer

Setup and Portals

Dorsal Wrist Portals

Portal Location Use
3-4 Between 3rd/4th compartments, 1 cm distal to Lister tubercle Primary radiocarpal viewing
4-5 Ulnar to 4th compartment, in line with ring finger Radiocarpal working
6-R Radial to ECU Radiocarpal alternative; TFCC debridement
6-U Ulnar to ECU Radiocarpal alternative
1-2 Proximal snuffbox Radial styloid access; radial artery + SBR nerve risk
Radial midcarpal 1 cm distal to 3-4 Midcarpal viewing; STT access
Ulnar midcarpal 1 cm distal to 4-5 Midcarpal viewing; easier landmark
  • Midcarpal portals: needle inserted horizontally (vs sloping for radiocarpal)
  • 1-2 portal: highest risk — blunt dissection mandatory (radial artery + superficial radial nerve)

Volar Portals

  • VR (volar radial): through FCR sheath, between RSC + LRL ligaments → visualizes volar SLIL
  • VU (volar ulnar): ulnar to FDS, protect ulnar NV bundle → visualizes volar LTIL
  • Inside-out: volar ulnar midcarpal is 3.7 mm from ulnar artery; volar radial midcarpal is 1 mm from median nerve

DRUJ Portals

  • Proximal DRUJ: between EDM and ECU; 1.9 mm scope; wrist in supination, reduced traction
  • Only DRUJ arthroscopy can confirm foveal attachment integrity (Atzei)

Arthroscopic Assessment

Foveal TFCC Tests

  • Hook test: displace ulnar edge centrally with probe — easy displacement = foveal incompetence (most sensitive/specific)
  • Trampoline test: compress central disc — loss of buoyancy = positive
  • Suction test: negative pressure to assess laxity

Carpal Instability (Geissler Classification)

Grade Radiocarpal Midcarpal
I Loss of concavity (bulge) Retained alignment
II Convex ligament Rotatory malalignment; probe fits but can’t twist
III Gap between bones Probe inserted + twisted 90°
IV Detached / “drive-through” Scope passes freely RC → MC
  • Rimington cadaveric study: physiologic laxity broader than originally described (48% had Geissler III SLIL) — cannot diagnose pathologic instability in isolation

TFCC Injury Management (Palmer Classification)

Class IA (Central Perforation)

  • No DRUJ instability; debride unstable flap if failed splint/CSI
  • Do not debride volar/dorsal RULs (critical DRUJ stabilizers)

Class IB (Ulnar Avulsion)

  • Outside-in technique: scope 3-4, sutures through ECU sheath floor + disc; 2-0 PDS; tie in supination
  • All-arthroscopic (FasT-Fix): PLL blocks with pretied sliding knots; 11/12 excellent at 1 yr (Yao)
  • Foveal tears: transosseous sutures or suture anchors into fovea

Barrera: For TFCC peripheral tears (Palmer IB), I follow the conservative ladder first: 4 weeks rigid brace → Wrist Widget + OT → MRI without contrast → CSI → arthroscopic repair as last resort. Most stable-DRUJ TFCC injuries respond to bracing. Reserve surgery for refractory symptoms with positive MRI.

Class IC (Distal/Ulnocarpal Avulsion)

  • Includes ulnotriquetral split tears (Berger)
  • Outside-to-inside repair with meniscal Mender II needle; 84% good/excellent at 21 months (Clark)

Class ID (Radial Avulsion)

  • Treatment controversial — radial TFCC is relatively avascular
  • Repair has NOT shown functional improvement over debridement

Class II (Degenerative)

  • IIA/IIB (no perforation): ulnar shortening osteotomy
  • IIC (perforation): ulnar shortening OR arthroscopic wafer procedure (debride < 4 mm ulnar head bone through central tear)
  • IID (LT perforation): ulnar shortening (traction reduces LT instability)
  • IIE: treat DRUJ arthritis
  • Wafer vs ulnar shortening: similar outcomes; wafer has lower complication rate (11% vs 35%) and better grip strength

Other Arthroscopic Applications

Ganglionectomy

  • Dorsal ganglions: usually overlie SLIL; portal adjacent to (not through) ganglion
  • Excise 1 cm dorsal capsule adjacent to SLIL until extensors visible — stay dorsal to SLIL
  • Recurrence: 0-10% (systematic review: 8% arthroscopic vs 10% open; statistically equivalent)

Distal Radius Fractures

  • Threshold for congruity: historically 2 mm, recent evidence supports 1 mm
  • Arthroscopy: direct visualization + concurrent SL/TFCC injury diagnosis and treatment
  • Abe series: 35% had > 2 mm residual step-off after fluoro-only reduction

Scaphoid Fractures

  • Dorsal percutaneous technique: starting point = middle third of SLIL insertion on proximal pole
  • Screw at least 4 mm shorter than measured
  • Nonunion with cystic changes: ream + inject DBM through bone biopsy needle → 14/15 healed
  • Humpback nonunion (Wong/Ho): correct DISI, morselized bone graft via midcarpal portal, fibrin glue, release traction for capitate compression → 90% union at 14 weeks

TM (Thumb CMC) Arthroscopy

  • 1-R (volar) + 1-U (dorsal) portals; 1.9 mm scope; fluoroscopy confirms placement
  • Radial artery within few mm of EPB at TM joint — high injury risk
  • Badia classification: Stage 1 (intact cartilage) → 2 (ulnar eburnation) → 3 (diffuse full-thickness loss)
  • Partial trapeziectomy: 3-4 mm bone removed ± suture button suspensionplasty (Mini TightRope)
  • Edwards: excellent outcomes at 4 years without interposition (fibrocartilage cap forms)
  • Synovectomy alone independently associated with secondary arthroplasty → couple with partial trapeziectomy

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Draw portals AFTER applying traction (landmarks shift)
  • Mark veins before tourniquet inflation
  • Dry arthroscopy: avoids fluid extravasation risk; better for mini-open combined procedures
  • 1-2 portal = highest-risk portal (radial artery + SBR nerve)
  • Geissler grading alone cannot diagnose pathologic instability — correlate clinically
  • For TFCC class ID (radial tears): debridement may equal repair in outcomes
  • Arthroscopic scaphoid fixation: use screw at least 4 mm shorter than measured length

Board-Style Questions (3-5)

  1. Q: What is the most sensitive and specific arthroscopic test for foveal TFCC detachment? — A: The hook test (Atzei).
  2. Q: During arthroscopic wafer procedure for Palmer class IIC degenerative TFCC tear, what is the maximum amount of ulnar head bone that should be removed? — A: Less than 4 mm.
  3. Q: A Geissler grade III interosseous ligament tear is defined by what finding on midcarpal arthroscopy? — A: A probe can be inserted into the gap between carpal bones and twisted 90°.
  4. Q: What is the recommended starting point for dorsal percutaneous arthroscopic scaphoid screw placement? — A: The middle third of the SLIL insertion on the proximal pole of the scaphoid.
  5. Q: During arthroscopic dorsal ganglionectomy, what structure must be protected while debriding dorsal capsule? — A: The scapholunate interosseous ligament (SLIL) — debride dorsal to it, not through it.
