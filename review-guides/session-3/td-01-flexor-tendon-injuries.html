Flexor Tendon Injuries

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • FDS: dual origin (medial epicondyle + proximal radius); median nerve; dual blood supply (radial + ulnar arteries)
  • FDP: IO membrane + volar ulna; ulnar artery; dual innervation — ulnar nerve (small + ring), AIN (middle + index)
  • FPL: IO membrane + distal radius; AIN; radial artery dominant
  • Linburg-Comstock anomaly: tendinous connection FDP-index to FPL — 31% unilateral, 16% bilateral; must recognize during exploration
  • Carpal tunnel arrangement: FDP deepest (coronal), FPL most radial, FDS index + small deep to ring + middle
  • Pulley system: 5 annular (A1-A5), 3 cruciate (C1-C3); A2 + A4 = critical (skeletally based); thumb equivalent = oblique pulley
  • Vincula at cruciate pulley level — preserve during repair
  • Healing: intrinsic pathway (epitenon → restores gliding, desired) vs extrinsic pathway (synovial sheath → adhesions)

Classification

Kleinert-Verdan Zones:

Zone Anatomy
I Distal to FDS insertion (FDP only)
II A1 to FDS insertion (“no man’s land”); Tang subzones IIa-IId
III Distal TCL to proximal A1 (high NV injury rate)
IV Within carpal tunnel
V Forearm proximal to CT
  • Thumb: T1-T4 mirror finger zones

Leddy-Packer (Zone I FDP avulsions): I = retraction to palm; II = retraction to PIP; III = bony fragment at A4

Workup

History

  • Ideal repair within 7 days; hand dominance, mechanism, position at injury
  • Emergency only if digital blood supply compromised

Exam

  • Loss of resting cascade, absent tenodesis effect → laceration
  • Test FPL, FDS, FDP independently; FDS to small absent ~6%
  • NV exam first (2PD, Semmes-Weinstein, Doppler)
  • US: 83% sens / 85% spec for high-grade zone II partial lacerations
  • 50% rule for partials: < 50% trim edges only; > 50% repair

Treatment

Operative — By Zone

Zone I: - ≥ 1 cm distal stump → direct core + epitendinous repair - < 1 cm stump → direct end-to-end with 10-12 strands of 4-0 nylon (Tang shift away from pullout button) - Bony avulsion fragment → screw, IO wire, or suture

Zone II (most variable outcomes): - Standard: ≥ 4-strand repair with 3-0/4-0 braided nonabsorbable + epitendinous - M-Tang 6-strand with sparse peripheral sutures becoming preferred globally - Pulley venting ≤ 2 cm total, palmar cut — no significant bowstringing - Digital extension-flexion test intraop: no gap on extension, no impingement on flexion - FDS repair debated; only FDP in zone IIc (narrowest sheath)

Zones III-V: better outcomes; mandatory NV exam; larger gauge sutures

FPL: often retracts into carpal tunnel/forearm; 87% good/excellent with 6-strand M-Tang + pulley vents

Barrera: OR setup sequence — antibiotics → tourniquet placed → prep/drape → local → Esmarch + inflate. Skip Esmarch exsanguination in tumor or infection cases; elevate only.

Biomechanical Principles

  • Strand count > caliber: 4-0 eight-strand beats 3-0 four-strand by 43%
  • Optimal purchase: 7-10 mm; locks/grasps ≥ 2 mm
  • Gap > 3 mm detrimental to strength accrual
  • Suture: nylon/Supramid preferred for zone I/II; FiberWire too rigid, Ethibond too extendable

Post-op / Rehab

Early active motion preferred — Trumble RCT: place-and-hold > passive (156° vs 128° TAM)

Nantong protocol (Tang): - Start day 4-5; 4-6 sessions/day × 15-20 min - 10+ passive ROM cycles → 40-60 runs active flexion to half/two-thirds initial range - Half a fist; avoid extreme flexion; out-of-splint motion OK - Week 3-4: aim full active ROM - Splint: wrist neutral/slight flexion, MCP ~30° flexion

Starr systematic review: active = more ruptures (5% vs 4%) BUT better ROM (6% vs 9% stiffness)

WALANT: enables intraop active motion check; patient sees repair → better compliance

Complications & Outcomes

  • Dy systematic review: 6% reoperation, 4% rupture, 4% adhesions
  • Tenolysis for limited active motion despite full passive — wait ≥ 3-6 months
  • Re-rupture → re-repair if early; otherwise two-stage reconstruction
  • > 3 weeks delayed: usually two-stage reconstruction needed

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • FDS to small finger absent in ~6% — always test individually
  • Linburg-Comstock can cause unexpected FPL retraction when exploring FDP-index
  • Zone IIc is the narrowest sheath — repair FDP only
  • Gap > 3 mm at repair = detrimental; always check with intraop extension-flexion test
  • Children: repair when possible; 4-week immobilization if non-compliant; 96% excellent outcomes (Sikora)
  • Strand count matters more than suture caliber

Board-Style Questions (5)

  1. Q: A patient sustains a Zone I FDP avulsion that retracts to the palm with no bony fragment. What is the Leddy-Packer type, and what is the critical timing concern? — A: Type I; must repair urgently as vincular blood supply is disrupted by retraction past the lumbrical origin.

  2. Q: During zone II flexor tendon repair, how many core suture strands are recommended, and what gap threshold is considered detrimental? — A: ≥ 4 strands; gap > 3 mm impairs strength accrual.

  3. Q: A 4-0 eight-strand repair is compared to a 3-0 four-strand repair. Which is stronger, and by how much? — A: The 4-0 eight-strand is 43% stronger — strand count is more critical than suture caliber.

  4. Q: What is the most important intraoperative check after completing a zone II flexor tendon repair? — A: Digital extension-flexion test — confirm no gap on extension and no impingement on flexion through the pulley system.

  5. Q: Which two annular pulleys must be preserved to prevent bowstringing, and what is the maximum total length of pulley that can be safely vented? — A: A2 and A4; up to 2 cm total venting on the palmar aspect is safe.
