Base of Thumb Fractures (Bennett, Rolando) + Thumb Ligament Injuries

Review Guide — Source: Handthology

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Thumb CMC = unsupported by adjacent MCs → more unstable, but greater opposition/circumduction
  • 2 phalanges → distal-to-proximal pinch line; force vector passes directly through CMC
  • 80% of 1st MC fractures occur at base (poor proximal fixation)
  • CMC stabilizers: volar (anterior) oblique ligament (“beak”), dorsoradial ligament
  • MCP collaterals: proper + accessory; proper attaches 3 mm distal to articular surface + 3 mm from volar cortex
  • Distal UCL tear in 90% (vs more variable RCL tear site)
  • Stener lesion: UCL fully torn + retracted + adductor pollicis aponeurosis interposed → blocks healing → SURGICAL
  • RCL: no Stener-equivalent (abductor pollicis aponeurosis more dorsal, doesn’t interpose)

Imaging

  • Robert’s view (true AP — thumb dorsum on cassette, beam 15° distal-to-proximal) — best for CMC joint
  • CT for comminuted Rolando, MC head fractures
  • Stress radiographs or US/MRI for ligament injuries

Distal Phalanx Fractures

Tuft

  • Crush mechanism; subungual hematoma common
  • Nail bed repair often reduces fracture
  • Splint 2-3 wk (IP free!)
  • Open: I&D, nail bed repair, K-wire if displaced or comminuted
  • Nonunion usually asymptomatic; excision if symptomatic

Shaft

  • Non-displaced: splint 2-3 wk; healing 8-10 wk
  • Displaced: K-wire; can use HCS for nonunion (100% union at 9 wk)

Mallet (Distal Phalanx Articular Avulsion)

  • Splint extension 6 wk full-time + 6 wk half-time (matches finger mallet)
  • Surgical indications: > 50% articular surface, volar subluxation
  • Outcomes: nonop = good motion; op tends to lose more motion

Proximal Phalanx Fractures

  • Better tolerance for deformity than other digits (CMC + MCP compensate)
  • Acceptable: 20-30° coronal/sagittal; rotation not tolerated
  • Surgical: 15-20° coronal, 20-30° sagittal, any rotation, open, intra-articular subluxation/step > 2 mm

Ulnar Avulsion Fractures (Proximal Phalanx Base — UCL avulsion)

  • Many series support nonoperative treatment (24/30 displaced — Kuz et al — good with thumb spica × 4 wk)
  • Surgical indications: bony Stener (fragment proximal to adductor aponeurosis), > 30% articular surface
  • Open reduction preferred (percutaneous pinning frustrating; small fragments)
  • Authors’ preferred: suture anchor + Krakow stitch in UCL using fragment as ripstop, thumb 30° flexion

Thumb Metacarpal Fractures

25% of all MC fractures occur in thumb; 80% at base

Epibasal (Extra-articular Base)

  • Apex dorsal (adductor pollicis + flexor pollicis brevis + APB)
  • Reduce > 30° angulation (compensatory MCP hyperextension if untreated)
  • Reduction: traction + extension + pronation + direct dorsal pressure
  • Usually stable in thumb spica; CRPP if reduction not maintained

Bennett Fracture (Intra-articular 2-Part)

  • Volar ulnar fragment held by anterior oblique (beak) ligament → trapezium
  • MC shaft + radial dorsal articular surface subluxates dorsally, proximally, radially (APL pull)
  • Always unstable — even if reducible in cast

Treatment

  • Anatomic reduction (≤ 1 mm step) → 83% asymptomatic at 7 yr; > 1 mm → only 46% asymptomatic
  • Fluoroscopy underestimates true displacement (Capo cadaveric)
  • Closed reduction (traction + abduction + pronation + pressure on MC base) + CRPP usually sufficient
  • Pin configuration: K-wire perpendicular to MC long axis across CMC into trapezium (or 2nd MC base if ulnar fragment small); second K-wire from radial MC shaft across CMC into trapezium
  • ORIF if > 2 mm articular step persists (Wagner approach, screw if fragment allows)
  • Arthroscopic-assisted reduction (Culp) — direct articular visualization
  • Bennett fracture is inherently unstable; CRPP under fluoro after closed reduction is the standard
  • Goal is anatomic reduction (< 1 mm step) — sub-anatomic reductions have ~2× symptomatic rate at 7 years

Rolando Fracture

  • Y/T-pattern at base; volar-ulnar fragment + dorsal radial fragment
  • More comminuted variant → “all comminuted base of 1st MC” colloquially
  • Poor prognosis regardless of treatment (Langhoff — radiographic arthritis 6/11, symptoms in 6/16 without correlation to reduction quality)
  • Goal: trapezio-MC joint reduction (not necessarily perfect articular reduction)
  • Treatment options:
    • 2 large fragments: ORIF with T/L-plate
    • Comminuted: oblique traction K-wire (transverse to 2nd MC) + ligamentotaxis
    • External fixation for severe comminution or soft tissue compromise
    • Locking plate for metaphyseal comminution

Thumb Ligament Injuries

CMC Dislocation

  • Rare; flexed + axially loaded MC; usually dorsal
  • Closed reduce → percutaneous pin in thumb spica × 4 wk
  • Persistent instability → Eaton FCR ligament reconstruction
  • Chronic with OA → arthrodesis or arthroplasty

MCP Collateral Ligament Injuries

  • UCL 90%, RCL 10-42%
  • Grading: I (pain, no laxity), II (laxity + firm endpoint), III (soft endpoint, complete)

UCL (Skier’s Thumb)

  • Valgus on abducted MCP
  • Test: valgus stress at 0° (accessory) + 30° flexion (proper); > 35° laxity OR > 15° vs contralateral = unstable
  • Gamekeeper’s thumb = chronic attritional UCL insufficiency
Treatment
  • Grade I-II: thumb spica × 5-6 wk
  • Grade III (with Stener) → surgical (Stener prevents healing)
  • Grade III without Stener controversial — non-op in low-demand may be acceptable
  • Approach: S-incision; identify UCL + adductor aponeurosis + bony fragment
  • Repair: ligament advancement, transosseous tunnels/buttons, suture anchor (sliding for re-tensioning) — Krakow stitch
  • Internal brace (suture tape) augmentation: faster RTP for athletes (De Giacomo)
  • Werner 2-suture anchor in college football: skill players RTP 7 wk, linemen 4 wk
  • Chronic: tendon graft reconstruction or adductor pollicis advancement
  • Chronic with OA: arthrodesis

RCL

  • Forceful adduction
  • Treatment principles same as UCL (no Stener equivalent)
  • Reconstruction with APB advancement

IP Joint Dislocations

  • Usually dorsal; closed reduction usually successful
  • Block to reduction = volar plate interposition or FPL noose
  • Pin × 2-3 wk if unstable; early ROM at 1 wk

Post-op / Rehab

  • Bennett CRPP: thumb spica × 4 wk → cast 6 wk total → therapy → unrestricted 10-12 wk
  • UCL repair: immobilize 4-6 wk → gentle ROM → strength at 6-8 wk → unrestricted RTP 10-12 wk
  • Plate/screw stable construct: AROM at 5-10 days
  • Most patients can transition to removable thumb spica by 4-6 wk if x-rays + clinical exam confirm stability; stiffness > instability is the more common complication

Complications & Outcomes

  • Most common complication = stiffness (not instability)
  • CMC: posttraumatic arthritis with sub-anatomic reduction
  • UCL chronic gamekeeper’s → MCP OA → arthrodesis
  • Nerve injury from K-wire (median nerve injury reported with Bennett pinning)
  • Tendon adhesions with dorsal plate
  • Bennett anatomic outcomes: 83% asymptomatic at 7.3 yr if ≤ 1 mm; 46% if not

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Robert’s view = essential for thumb base
  • Bennett deforming forces: APL (proximal/radial/dorsal), adductor pollicis (adduction)
  • Reduction maneuver: traction + abduction + pronation + dorsoradial MC base pressure
  • Never accept any rotational deformity in thumb
  • Stener lesion = absolute surgical indication; non-Stener grade III controversial
  • Use 30° MCP flexion for UCL repair tensioning
  • Bony Stener (fragment proximal to adductor aponeurosis) = surgical
  • Acute Stener becomes scarred ≥ 3-4 wk — repair may still work if ligament minimally displaced
  • For any acute thumb injury, get Robert’s view + stress views; Stener can sometimes be palpated; US is excellent confirmation

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What deforming force on the distal Bennett fragment causes its dorsal/proximal/radial subluxation? A: Abductor pollicis longus (APL).
  2. Q: What is a Stener lesion? A: Complete UCL rupture with retraction and interposition of the adductor pollicis aponeurosis — blocks healing, surgical indication.
  3. Q: What angle of laxity on valgus stress confirms thumb MCP UCL instability? A: > 35° absolute or > 15° more than contralateral.
  4. Q: What is the prognostic significance of articular step-off in Bennett fracture reduction? A: ≤ 1 mm = 83% asymptomatic at 7 yr; > 1 mm = 46% asymptomatic.
  5. Q: Why does the RCL not produce a Stener-equivalent lesion? A: The abductor pollicis aponeurosis lies more dorsal and doesn’t interpose between the torn RCL and bone.
