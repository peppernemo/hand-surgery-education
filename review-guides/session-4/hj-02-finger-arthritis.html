Finger Arthritis (PIP and DIP)

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • PIP + DIP similar; differ in dimensions + DIP mobility
  • Unlike MCP, osseous shape plays major role in PIP/DIP stability
  • Not a perfect hinge — small abduction/adduction + rotation
  • Instantaneous rotational axis (not fixed)
  • Radial PIP (index, middle): more stability needed for thumb pinch
  • Ulnar PIP (ring, small): more mobility for grasp
  • Anatomical implants cannot fully replicate this complexity

Classification — Kellgren-Lawrence

  1. Doubtful narrowing, possible osteophytic lipping
  2. Definite osteophytes + narrowing
  3. Moderate osteophytes, narrowing, sclerosis ± deformity
  4. Large osteophytes, marked narrowing, severe sclerosis, definite deformity

Workup

  • Clinical + radiographs sufficient
  • Fusiform swelling, tender joints, stiffness
  • Mucoid cysts ± nail deformity at DIP
  • CT only for post-traumatic PIP considering joint-preserving osteotomy

Treatment

Nonoperative

  • NSAIDs, activity modification, splinting
  • Treating affected joints doesn’t alter progression in unaffected joints

Barrera: CSI = Kenalog 10 mg/mL + 1% lido (never Depo).

PIP Joint

Surgical Decision Framework

  • Index + middle (pinching partners with thumb) → prioritize stability (often arthrodesis or modified arthroplasty rehab)
  • Ring + small (grasp) → prioritize mobility (arthroplasty)
  • Lateral deformity > 30° → arthrodesis in radial digits
  • Woodworth: simulated PIP fusion 40° on all 4 fingers → significant ADL impairment (precision tasks)

PIP Arthrodesis

Indications

  • Severe instability + deformity
  • Difficult bone (post-infection, post-trauma)
  • Revision after failed arthroplasty
  • Lateral deformity > 30° in radial digits

Fusion Angles

  • Radial digits: 15-20° flexion
  • Ulnar digits: 25-40° flexion

Techniques

  • Tension band wiring: active compression with motion; cost-effective; risk pin protrusion
  • Plate fixation
  • Herbert screw: immediate strong fixation, low complication

Technique Pearls

  • Dorsal curvilinear, central slip midline split
  • Remove osteophytes, release collaterals
  • Osteotomy: desired angle on proximal phalanx; perpendicular on middle phalanx
  • Lift hand off table to check 3D rotation
  • Avoid malrotation — disabling
  • Suture extensor over hardware

PIP Arthroplasty

Indications

  • OA, post-traumatic
  • Intact tendons + at least some residual stability

Contraindications

  • Insufficient bone stock
  • Missing/dysfunctional tendons
  • Severe tendon imbalance (contracted boutonniere, swan neck)
  • Severe instability or deformity > 30°

Implants

Silicone (Swanson 1968 — gold standard) - Joint spacer concept - ROM 40-60° active flex/ext - 10-30% fracture at long-term (less than MCP rate) - 90% survival at 10 yr - Good pain relief - Less silicone synovitis than at MCP

Surface Replacement (2-component) - Chrome-cobalt/poly, ceramic/ceramic, pyrocarbon/pyrocarbon - CapFlex: short-stem subchondral fixation - Pyrocarbon: excellent biocompatibility but squeaking + osteointegration issues - Kulkarni systematic review (1324 joints, 54 mo): - VAS pain ↓ 4.5; ROM ↑ 10.5°; DASH ↑ 18 - Reoperation 21%, revision 11%

Approaches

Dorsal (most common): - Longitudinal incision, preserve dorsal veins + sensory branch - Midline central slip split (preferred) OR Chamay V-shaped flap (calcification + hyperextension risk) - Release dorsal collaterals - Cannot correct deformity intraoperatively — even with rehab

Volar (Herren): - Tendons NOT violated - Bruner incision, radial flap; protect NV bundles - Open A3 pulley, release accessory collaterals - Immediate passive motion (no extensor protection) - Technically demanding, less space

Lateral (least common): - Bain modification: split collateral + side-to-side repair → less instability + early ROM

Surgical Approach Selection (Burton-Pellegrini → modern)

  • Old: arthrodesis for radial, arthroplasty for ulnar
  • Current: arthroplasty all digits, but modified rehab for index (later mobilization + prolonged splinting to protect radial collateral)

DIP Joint

Arthrodesis (First Choice for Painful DIP OA)

  • Screw fixation (preferred), K-wires, tension band, endomedullary staples

Screw Technique

  • Headless or 2.0 mm AO (1.5 mm for small finger if mismatched)
  • Length ~30 mm (allow future PIP arthroplasty without removal)
  • Dorsal transverse incision, transverse tenotomy of extensor
  • CRITICAL: protect germinal nail matrix (few mm distal to joint line) — violation = permanent nail deformity
  • Bone prep: precision saw proximal (minimal); rongeur distal
  • Fusion angle: 5-10° flexion
  • Screw aim: middle phalanx side → dorsal recess of PIP (slight flexion); distal phalanx side → subungual
  • Distal phalanx over-drilled (lag); screw flush with subungual bone
  • Splint 4-6 wk

K-wire

  • Insert proximal-dorsal → distal-volar (avoid fingertip)
  • 2 crossed K-wires for rotation
  • Higher infection rate

DIP Arthroplasty

  • Alternative for selected ulnar fingers
  • NOT for index (pinch loads → ulnar deviation)
  • Swanson 00 implant typical
  • Dorsal approach + transverse tenotomy
  • NO passive motion testing (extensor disruption risk)
  • Immobilize 3-4 wk
  • Expected ROM: 30-40°

Post-op / Rehab

  • PIP fusion: stable construct → buddy tape + early adjacent joint motion
  • PIP arthroplasty (dorsal): protected motion 6 wk; index = later mobilization + prolonged splinting
  • PIP arthroplasty (volar): immediate passive motion OK
  • DIP fusion: splint 4-6 wk; radiographic union confirm
  • DIP arthroplasty: 3-4 wk immobilization
  • For fixed swan neck or boutonniere with PIP arthritis → primary fusion (not arthroplasty); arthroplasty cannot correct already-fixed deformity

Complications & Outcomes

PIP Fusion

  • Nonunion (biological or technical)
  • Malunion (malrotation, lateral deviation) = disabling
  • Hardware irritation → removal
  • Rotation check intraoperatively essential

PIP Arthroplasty

  • Silicone: 10-30% fracture long-term; doesn’t always need revision
  • Forster meta-analysis:
    • Silicone: 11% complications, 1% reoperation
    • Pyrocarbon: 14% complications, 7% reoperation
    • Metal-poly: 10% complications, 10% reoperation
    • Revision 4% all types
  • Helder registry: surface 20% complications vs silicone 11%
  • Literature revision rate 2-13%
  • Preexisting deformity recurs (silicone esp); ROM rarely improved
  • Newer pyrocarbon/ceramic/resurfacing: dislocation + squeaking
  • RA patients fare worse

DIP Arthrodesis

  • High fusion rates; complications dependent on technique
  • Permanent nail deformity if germinal matrix violated

DIP Arthroplasty

  • Limited publications
  • Extension deficit common (cosmetic limit)

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Index = stability priority (arthrodesis or modified arthroplasty rehab)
  • Ulnar fingers = mobility priority (arthroplasty)
  • Lateral deformity > 30° → arthrodesis
  • Silicone = gold standard PIP; 10-yr survival 90%
  • Always check rotation by lifting hand off table
  • Fusion angles: PIP radial 15-20°, ulnar 25-40°, DIP 5-10°
  • DIP nail matrix protection mandatory
  • Painful DIP OA = fusion is first choice
  • Fixed swan neck/boutonniere with PIP arthritis = fusion, not arthroplasty
  • Failed arthroplasty → fusion with structural bone graft

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What are the recommended fusion angles for PIP arthrodesis? A: Radial digits 15-20° flexion; ulnar digits 25-40° flexion.
  2. Q: What is the 10-year survival rate for silicone PIP arthroplasty? A: ~90%.
  3. Q: Why is DIP arthroplasty contraindicated for the index finger? A: Lateral pinch loads cause ulnar deviation and instability.
  4. Q: Per Kulkarni systematic review, what is the reoperation rate for surface PIP arthroplasty? A: 21% reoperation, 11% revision.
  5. Q: What is the fusion angle for DIP arthrodesis? A: 5-10° flexion (functional + cosmetic).
