Digital Replantation

Review Guide — Source: Handthology

Hybrid format: tight board-prep bullets layered with Barrera-voice clinical pearls. Target length: 1-3 pages. Drop unused sections; do NOT pad.

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Dorsal venous system = dominant digital outflow
  • Dominant (larger) digital arteries: ulnar in thumb/index/middle; radial in ring/small (0.3-0.4 mm larger)
  • Digital artery is dorsal to digital nerve in the finger (reverses in the palm)

Classification

Tamai Zones

Zone Level
I Distal to nail base
II Lunula to DIP
III DIP to FDS insertion
IV FDS insertion to MCP
V MCP and proximal

Mechanism

  • Sharp: minimal surrounding damage; best prognosis
  • Crush: bone comminution + bilateral tissue destruction
  • Avulsion: longitudinal NV injury; soft tissue avulsed proximal to fracture; worst prognosis

Indications & Contraindications

Indications: multiple digits, thumb (any level), wrist/hand amputation, pediatric, single digit zone I

Relative contraindications: life-threatening injuries, crush/avulsion, multilevel, single digit zone II (adhesions, poor function), prolonged ischemia, noncompliance

Ischemia limits: digits ~12h warm (longer reported); hand/forearm 4-6h warm or 10h cold (muscle necrosis)

Treatment

Operative Sequence

  1. Back-benching (optional — many experts skip; desiccation risk)
  2. Skeletal fixation first: shorten up to 1 cm; save joints; trans-articular = primary arthrodesis
  3. Osteosynthesis: preferred = 90-90 interosseous wiring or hybrid (1 K-wire + 1 IOW) for rigid fixation + early therapy; K-wires alone impede therapy
  4. Flexor tendon repair (meticulous, with epitendinous suture, finger in full extension)
  5. Extensor tendon repair
  6. Microsurgical: venous repair, then arterial

Arterial Repair Pearls

  • Signs of vascular injury: red line sign (ecchymosis), measles/paprika sign (vasa vasorum ruptures), ribbon/pigtail sign (intimal fractures from traction)
  • Debride back to normal intima; flush with heparinized saline (10,000 U/100 mL)
  • Back-wall first technique: less torsion/manipulation than 0-180°
  • Suture: 9-0 to 11-0 nylon

Vein Grafts

  • Use liberally if any tension; donor: volar forearm or dorsum of foot
  • Reverse the graft (valves); distal anastomosis first then flush before proximal

Nerve Repair

  • Primary repair often possible after bone shortening
  • Avoid conduits in replantation (complex soft tissue → extrusion risk)
  • Avoid nerve autograft (donor morbidity if digit fails)
  • Allograft excellent if needed
  • Distal fingertip: nerve repair often not possible AND not required (spontaneous neurotization)

Barrera: OR sequence: antibiotics → tourniquet → prep/drape → local → Esmarch + inflate.

  • Max 2 hours per tourniquet run, 20-minute break between; save tourniquet time for the microsurgical portion

Artery-Only Fingertip Replantation (Tamai I)

  • Venous anastomosis often impossible distally
  • Alternative outflow: surface bleeding (fish-mouth incisions, nail plate removal), medicinal leeches, pulp reduction
  • Systematic review: 76% survival surface bleeding; 68% leeching
  • 5-10 days for neovascularization; transfusion typically 1+ units (up to 10)

Multiple Digit Priority

  • Thumb → ulnar digits → radial digits (thumb + small = tip pinch + grasp)

Post-op / Rehab

  • Color + Doppler q1h x 24h → q2h x 3 days → taper
  • Warming 3-5+ days; bed rest day 1, slow mobilization
  • Arterial loss early → OR immediately (likely technical error)
  • Venous congestion more common postop; controlled bleeding/leeches; revision less likely to succeed

Leeching Protocol

  • Hirudo medicinalis: bite wound bleeds 3-4 hours (hirudin = antithrombin)
  • Apply every 3-4 hours; 7-10 days for proximal amputations
  • Aeromonas hydrophila in leech saliva — prophylactic ciprofloxacin or 3rd-gen cephalosporin

Anticoagulation

  • No consensus; standard practice: aspirin 325 mg daily x 1 month for all; IV heparin for high-risk/vein grafts (PTT 60-90); transition to LMWH x 1 month if indicated

Complications & Outcomes

  • Overall survival >90% in published series; sharp 91.5%; crush/avulsion 66-68%
  • US average: 5 cases/hospital/year; success as low as 29% at low-volume centers
  • Cold intolerance: 40-100%, near-universal
  • Secondary surgery: 40-60%; most common early = wound healing; late = tenolysis + bony procedures
  • Sensibility: static 2PD 10-12 mm; fingertip replant <7 mm (near-normal)
  • TAM: 129-192° (45-56% of contralateral); Zone I best, Zone II worst
  • Only 50% return to same occupation; 30% don’t return to work at all

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • 90-90 IOW or hybrid fixation enables early therapy — K-wires alone are insufficient
  • Red line, measles, and ribbon signs identify extent of vascular injury intraop
  • Reverse vein grafts; distal anastomosis first
  • Avoid circumferential wrap — expose fingertips for monitoring
  • Volume matters: high-volume centers significantly outperform low-volume
  • Aspirin 325 mg daily x 1 month is universal; heparin is selective
  • Cold intolerance is near-universal — counsel patients preop

Board-Style Questions (5)

  1. Q: Which digital artery is dominant in the thumb? — A: Ulnar digital artery (also dominant in index and middle).

  2. Q: What is the single strongest relative contraindication for replantation of an isolated finger? — A: Single digit amputation in Tamai zone II (FDS insertion to DIP) due to tendon adhesions and poor functional outcome.

  3. Q: What organism must be covered prophylactically during medicinal leech therapy? — A: Aeromonas hydrophila; treat with ciprofloxacin or 3rd-generation cephalosporin.

  4. Q: What are the signs of intimal arterial injury seen under the microscope during replantation? — A: Red line sign (ecchymosis along artery), measles/paprika sign (vasa vasorum ruptures), ribbon/pigtail sign (coiling from traction with intimal fractures).

  5. Q: A digit is replanted with arterial repair only (Tamai zone I). What is the expected survival rate with surface bleeding techniques? — A: 76%.
