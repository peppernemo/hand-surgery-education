Ulnar Nerve Compression

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Cubital tunnel borders: medial epicondyle (anterior), olecranon (posterior), Osborne’s ligament/FCU aponeurosis (roof), MCL posterior/transverse bands (floor)
  • Cubital tunnel CSA decreases 40% at 135° elbow flexion; intraneural pressure increases 45% at 120°
  • 5 compression sites at elbow: arcade of Struthers (5.7 cm proximal) → medial head triceps → Osborne’s ligament → between FCU heads → intermuscular ligament FCU-FDS
  • Guyon’s canal: 3 zones (Gross & Gelberman 1985)
    • Zone I: motor + sensory (ganglion, thrombosis, mass)
    • Zone II: motor only (hook of hamate fracture, ganglion)
    • Zone III: sensory only
  • Nerve hypermobility at elbow: present in 37% of asymptomatic individuals — not automatic indication for transposition
  • DCU branches 7 cm proximal to wrist → dorso-ulnar sensation affected at elbow level but spared with wrist-level compression

Workup

History

  • Pain + paresthesia in ulnar distribution (volar/dorsal small + ulnar ring finger)
  • Worse with elbow flexion: sleep, resting on elbow, driving, phone use
  • Advanced: intrinsic weakness, cramping, atrophy

Exam

  • Duchenne sign: ring/small clawing (weak lumbricals/interossei + unopposed extrinsic flexors)
  • Wartenberg sign: small finger abduction (paralyzed 3rd volar interosseous + unopposed EDM)
  • Froment sign: thumb IP flexion with lateral pinch (lost adductor pollicis → compensatory FPL)
  • Nerve stability: “stable” (stays in groove) vs “perching” vs “dislocating” with flexion
  • Elbow flexion-compression test (Novak): highly reliable
  • Scratch collapse test: variable accuracy; ethyl chloride can unmask secondary sites
  • 2PD: abnormal only in severe compression

Barrera: For 2PD I always test in order: thumb, index, middle, ring, small. Compare ulnar to radial side of ring finger — that’s your median-ulnar watershed.

Electrodiagnostics

  • AAEM criteria; conduction velocity (demyelination) → CMAP amplitude reduction (axonal loss = later)
  • Reduced CMAP at FDI predicts preoperative weakness (Power 2019)
  • 26% with clinical cubital tunnel have no EDX evidence (Kortlever) — diagnosis can be clinical
  • Up to 80% motor neuron pool can be lost with near-normal muscle function (single motor units enlarge 5x to compensate)

Imaging

  • US: increased CSA correlates with severity; detects some cases EDX misses (Beekman: 18.5%)
  • MRI: discriminates focal compression + space-occupying lesions

Severity Classification

  • McGowan/Dellon with EDX integration (Power 2022)
  • Clinical exam doesn’t correlate linearly with axonal loss

Treatment

Non-operative

  • Mild/early: activity modification, avoid prolonged flexion
  • Night splinting: rigid orthosis or soft towel block in antecubital fossa x 3-4 weeks

Operative — In Situ Decompression (Preferred for Most Cases)

  • 3-6 cm incision between olecranon and medial epicondyle
  • Preserve MABC branches (neuroma in 73% of revision failures — Mackinnon)
  • Release cubital tunnel retinaculum + deep brachial fascia (8 cm proximal) + FCU aponeurosis distally
  • Do NOT dissect circumferentially → causes instability
  • Check stability post-release: perching → evaluate triceps fascia; subluxation → stabilizing flap or transposition

Operative — Endoscopic In Situ

  • 2-4 cm incision; endoscopic blade divides fascia 10 cm proximal + distal
  • Check for subluxation after release
  • Outcomes equivalent to open (Aldekhayel meta-analysis: 79.8% vs 81.8%)

Operative — Transposition

  • Subcutaneous: excise medial intermuscular septum (proximal AND distal); sling/adipofascial flap to prevent fall-back; preserve FCU motor branches
  • Submuscular: most protected, well-vascularized; often used for revision; Z-lengthen flexor-pronator origin
  • Intramuscular: theoretically straighter; risk of scarring within muscle

Medial Epicondylectomy

  • Removes perching prominence; preserves vascularity
  • Risk: medial elbow pain (0-52%), MCL injury, flexion contracture (0-17%)

Supercharge AIN-to-Ulnar Motor (SETS)

  • Indication: severe compression, reduced CMAP, spontaneous activity on FDI/ADM EMG, normal PQ EMG

  • Distal forearm incision; AIN harvested at PQ; epineurial window in ulnar motor fascicle ~9 cm proximal to wrist crease

  • Doherty: 73% achieved M3 intrinsic strength

  • Xie RCT: 13% greater pinch strength + lower DASH at 2 yr vs transposition alone

  • Treatment algorithm: injections are not part of cubital tunnel management. Stepwise approach: activity modification → night splinting → surgical decompression. Default to simple in situ release unless nerve subluxes post-decompression.

Guyon’s Canal (Ulnar Neuropathy at Wrist)

  • Ganglion = most common cause; also hook of hamate fracture (Zone II), ulnar artery thrombosis
  • Spares dorsoulnar hand (DCU branches proximal to wrist)
  • Allen test to evaluate ulnar artery patency
  • Non-operative: activity modification, gel-padded gloves; no evidence for splinting/steroids
  • Surgical: open Guyon canal, decompress deep motor branch around hook of hamate; release distal antebrachial fascia proximal to wrist crease

Complications & Outcomes

  • Song (39 pts): improved pinch (7.0→8.3), SWM, DASH (30.4→19.8), MHQ (55.5→70.7) at 12 mo
  • Subjective scores plateau at 3 mo; objective improvement continues > 12 mo (Giladi)
  • 4 RCTs: no difference between simple decompression and transposition
  • Excellent results 45-57% regardless of technique; simple decompression most cost-effective
  • Trend: transposition declining (67%→16%), in situ rising (~80%)
  • Revision rate: 1.4-19%; predictors: trauma history (OR 7.1), McGowan I (OR 3.2), workers’ comp
  • MABC neuroma in 73% of revision failures; perineural fibrosis in 68%
  • McGowan I patients = OR 3.2 for failure (often other causes: cervical, polyneuropathy, TOS)

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Incorrect diagnosis = critical complication — r/o C8-T1 radiculopathy, thoracic outlet, polyneuropathy before surgery
  • McGowan grade I patients have highest failure rate — low threshold for additional workup
  • Dorso-ulnar sensation affected → compression at elbow (not wrist)
  • 37% nerve hypermobility is normal — don’t transpose for hypermobility alone; 79% do transpose for true subluxation
  • Average 2.2 compression sites missed at revision (Gabel) — complete release from arcade of Struthers through FCU
  • MABC injury during initial surgery → most common source of post-op pain; careful identification + preservation is essential
  • Simple in situ decompression is now the dominant approach — supported by 4 RCTs showing no inferiority to transposition

Board-Style Questions (5)

  1. Q: A patient with ulnar neuropathy has numbness of the small finger and ulnar ring finger volarly but intact dorsoulnar hand sensation. Where is the compression? — A: Guyon’s canal (wrist level); the dorsal cutaneous branch of the ulnar nerve arises 7 cm proximal to the wrist and is spared in wrist-level compression.
  2. Q: After cubital tunnel release, a patient has new pain along the medial forearm without ulnar distribution symptoms. What is the most likely cause? — A: MABC neuroma — the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve branches cross the surgical field and are injured in 73% of revision cases (Mackinnon).
  3. Q: A patient with cubital tunnel syndrome has a normal EDX study. Does this rule out the diagnosis? — A: No; 26% of patients with clinical cubital tunnel have no electrodiagnostic evidence (Kortlever). Up to 80% of the motor neuron pool can be lost before clinical detection due to compensatory motor unit enlargement.
  4. Q: What physical exam finding distinguishes a Zone II Guyon’s canal lesion from a Zone I lesion? — A: Zone II is motor-only — patient has intrinsic weakness/clawing but preserved sensation; Zone I involves both motor and sensory fibers.
  5. Q: Four RCTs comparing simple decompression vs anterior transposition for cubital tunnel syndrome showed what? — A: No significant difference in outcomes between techniques; simple decompression is preferred as the most cost-effective approach.
