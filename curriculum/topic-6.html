Topic 6 · Soft Tissue & Vascular

Fingertip injuries · replantation · flap selection · vascular disorders

Flap-selection questions are 6% of the OITE hand section, and the fingertip is the most common hand injury you will see all year. This week is coverage thinking: what heals on its own, what needs a flap, what needs a microscope.

Interns own: the ED fingertip (bacitracin + Coban, secondary healing default, follow-up 10-14 days), how to transport an amputated part, and the replant phone call.

Test yourself

  1. A fingertip amputation with exposed fat but no bone: ED management and expected outcome. What changes when bone is exposed?
  2. State the absolute and relative indications for replantation and the ischemia windows for a digit vs a muscle-containing part.
  3. Match the flap to the defect: volar thumb pulp loss · volar oblique index tip with exposed bone in a 25-year-old · dorsal finger defect with exposed tendon.
  4. Post-replant hour 18: the digit turns dusky with brisk cap refill and dark bleeding on pinprick. Arterial or venous, and what do you do?
  5. A carpenter has ulnar-sided hand pain, a positive Allen’s with poor ulnar refill, and a cold small finger. Name the syndrome, the study, and the treatment options.
  6. How do the reconstruction priorities change in a mangled hand vs a clean single-digit amputation?
  7. What anticoagulation does a digital replant get in this practice, and what monitoring schedule?
Answers
  1. No exposed bone: secondary intention is the default: bacitracin + Coban dressing changes, healing over 3-6 weeks with near-normal sensation, follow-up 10-14 days. Exposed bone: options are minimal shortening + primary-ish closure, or a flap chosen by geometry (below); tip losses in children do remarkably well conservatively.
  2. Absolute: thumb at any level, multiple digits, transmetacarpal/partial hand, any pediatric amputation, wrist/forearm sharp. Relative: single digit distal to FDS. Relative contraindications: single digit zone II, severe crush/avulsion (ring avulsion), prolonged warm ischemia, unstable patient. Windows: digit ~12 h warm / 24 h cold; muscle-containing ~6 h warm / 12 h cold. Transport: moist gauze, sealed bag, ON ice water, never direct ice.
  3. Volar thumb pulp: Moberg volar advancement (the thumb tolerates it; fingers risk flexion contracture). Volar oblique index with exposed bone: cross-finger flap (or thenar flap in the young for middle/index tips). Dorsal defect with exposed tendon: reverse cross-finger or a homodigital island; larger dorsal-hand defects climb the ladder toward regional (first dorsal metacarpal artery “kite” for the thumb/index) and distant options.
  4. Venous congestion (dusky, brisk refill, dark bleeding). Loosen every dressing and suture under tension, elevate, leeches with ciprofloxacin coverage (Aeromonas) while deciding; return to the OR for re-exploration if it does not reverse. Arterial failure is the opposite picture: pale, cool, slow refill, no bleeding → OR.
  5. Hypothenar hammer syndrome: ulnar artery thrombosis/aneurysm in Guyon’s canal from repetitive palm trauma. Study: angiography (or CTA); Allen’s is the bedside screen. Treatment: stop the trauma, calcium channel blockers for spasm; resect the thrombosed segment with ligation if the arch back-fills, or vein-graft reconstruction if it does not.
  6. Mangled hand: life before limb, then a reconstruction plan built around function: stable skeleton, perfusion, early durable coverage, secondary reconstruction. Parts that cannot be replanted become spare parts (skin, bone, tendon donors). The goal is a sensate, painless, positioned hand, not the maximum number of digits.
  7. Lovenox plus aspirin 81 for two weeks. Monitoring: hourly checks (color, turgor, cap refill, Doppler) for the first 24 hours, then q2h for 24, then q4h; warm room, no nicotine or caffeine, loose dressings.
