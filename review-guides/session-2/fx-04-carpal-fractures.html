Carpal Fractures (Non-Scaphoid)

Review Guide — Source: Handthology

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • 8 carpal bones: proximal row (scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum); distal row (trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate)
  • Pisiform = sesamoid in FCU tendon; pisotriquetral joint; not part of mechanical proximal row
  • Frequency of carpal fractures (excl scaphoid):
    • Triquetrum 18% (most common after scaphoid)
    • Hamate 5%
    • Trapezium 1-5%
    • Capitate 1-2%
    • Lunate < 1%
    • Trapezoid + pisiform 0.5-2% (rare)

Triquetrum

Anatomy

  • > 90% are dorsal cortical (chip) fractures
  • Avulsion of dorsal radiocarpal + dorsal intercarpal ligaments; or direct impact

Treatment

  • Nonoperative: cast/orthosis based on pain — mainstay
  • High rate of dorsal ligament injury on MRI (Becce) — but does not correlate with bone marrow edema or change management
  • Concomitant TFCC injury can cause persistent ulnar-side pain (Lee)
  • Proximal radial corner triquetrum fracture (near LT) on PA = marker for perilunate instability / LT injury / VISI — surgical stabilization warranted

Hamate

Hook of Hamate

  • Classic in racket sports, baseball, golf (butt of club/bat impacts hypothenar)
  • Adjacent to ulnar NV bundle; attachment for TCL
  • Nonunion → flexor tendon attrition (esp ulnar digits), ulnar neuropathy

Treatment

  • Nonop trial OK with early recognition: thumb-spica cast (minimize TCL pulling force)
  • Hirano: 2/5 cast-treated → nonunion needing surgery
  • Symptomatic nonunion → excision (gold standard)
  • Approach: volar incision over hook; protect motor branch of ulnar nerve (dives deep around distal-ulnar hook); release hypothenar attachments
  • Smooth bone base after excision
  • Most athletes return to play promptly post-excision (Devers)
  • ORIF (Watson) and ultrasound bone stimulator alternatives — limited evidence

Dorsal Hamate Fracture with CMC Dislocation

  • 4th + 5th CMC fracture-dislocation = surgical
  • Cain classification of hamato-MC fracture-dislocations
  • Compression screw or K-wire to restore congruency; cross-pin to adjacent stable MC or scaphoid body if fragment too small
  • Athletes with persistent hypothenar pain after “wrist sprain” — get a carpal tunnel view or CT to rule out hook fracture; excision is the standard operation

Trapezium

Anatomy

  • Saddle-shaped articulation with thumb MC
  • TCL attachment + FCR groove
  • Body fractures: sagittal split, horizontal split, transarticular, dorsoradial tuberosity, comminuted
  • Volar ridge: type I (base), type II (tip avulsion)
  • Mechanism: axial load on dorsiflexed wrist (thumb MC driven into trapezium)
  • Can occur with Bennett fracture

Treatment

  • Nonop: thumb spica × 4-6 wk for non-displaced
  • Displaced body / joint incongruity → ORIF (compression screw, K-wire, or ex-fix/dynamic traction for comminution)
  • Volar ridge fractures: often missed, treated as contusion; symptomatic nonunion → excision

Capitate

Anatomy

  • 1-2% of carpal fractures
  • Central pillar of wrist; articulates with lunate + scaphoid proximally
  • Historically thought tenuous proximal blood supply (retrograde) but Kadar micro-CT: 70% have proximal vessel → AVN less common than predicted

Treatment

  • Non-displaced: SAC × 4-8 wk
  • Displaced: headless compression screw via dorsal approach (3rd/4th compartments along radial border long finger)

Scaphocapitate Syndrome (Fenton’s)

  • Transcaphoid perilunate dislocation + capitate head rotated 90-180° sagittal plane
  • Easily missed on x-ray
  • Operative reduction mandatory — missed → nonunion + midcarpal arthritis

Lunate

Anatomy

  • Primary axial load transmitter to radiocarpal joint
  • Fractures < 1% of carpal — distinct from Kienböck AVN

Treatment

  • Non-displaced dorsal/chip: cast 1 wk + weekly reexam (rule out missed concomitant injury)
  • Volar body fracture → extended carpal tunnel approach, headless compression screw
  • Small avulsion with ligamentous injury → suture anchor; K-wire SL + LT stabilization
  • Pin scaphoid-capitate to prevent proximal capitate migration compressing lunate
  • Body fractures with intra-articular extension → dorsal approach between 3rd + 4th compartments
  • Perilunate-associated lunate fractures: pinning if fragments too small for screw

Trapezoid

Anatomy

  • Base of 2nd MC; isolated fracture rare; usually with other injuries
  • Most missed carpal injury — Kain: only 5/11 initially recognized; coronal patterns visible only on CT

Treatment

  • Isolated non-displaced: cast immobilization
  • Joint incongruity or 2nd MC base instability → screw or K-wire fixation

Pisiform

Anatomy

  • Sesamoid in FCU; pisotriquetral joint
  • 0.5-2% of carpal fractures; sports-related (direct blow, repetitive)
  • Pediatric: multiple ossification centers (CT confirms vs fracture)

Treatment

  • SAC immobilization successful
  • Symptomatic nonunion → pisiform excision; wrist function preserved (van Eijzeren)

Any ulnar-side wrist injury without obvious x-ray finding: low threshold for CT — triquetrum, hamate hook, pisiform, and lunate all present with ulnar/dorsal-ulnar pain and are commonly missed.

Post-op / Rehab

  • Hook of hamate excision: short cast for comfort × 1 wk → motion
  • Lunate ORIF: 4-6 wk SAC; CT at 8-12 wk to confirm healing
  • Capitate ORIF: 4-6 wk cast → motion
  • Athletes (post-hook excision): typically RTP by 6-8 wk

Complications & Outcomes

  • Missed injury → nonunion, persistent pain, secondary arthrosis
  • Hook of hamate nonunion → FDP attrition/rupture, ulnar neuropathy
  • Capitate AVN concerns historically overstated; rare in practice
  • Trapezoid + capitate fractures (when missed) → midcarpal arthritis
  • Pisiform: post-traumatic pisotriquetral arthrosis if untreated nonunion

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Triquetrum dorsal chip = stable, splint for comfort — but proximal radial corner = perilunate marker
  • Hook of hamate — get carpal tunnel view + CT, excise if symptomatic
  • Always image opposite wrist for pediatric pisiform (ossification variation vs fracture)
  • Scaphocapitate syndrome = look for rotated capitate head in perilunate injury
  • Capitate AVN concerns historically overstated — recent CT data shows reliable proximal supply
  • Trapezoid fractures need CT — easily missed coronal split pattern
  • Volar ridge trapezium fractures often misdiagnosed as contusion

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What sports are classically associated with hook of hamate fractures and what’s the standard treatment? A: Racket sports, baseball, golf; excision of the fragment for symptomatic nonunion (preferred over ORIF for most athletes).
  2. Q: What is scaphocapitate (Fenton’s) syndrome? A: Transcaphoid perilunate dislocation with capitate head rotated 90-180° in sagittal plane; requires operative reduction.
  3. Q: A proximal radial corner triquetrum fracture on PA radiograph should make you suspect what? A: Perilunate instability / LT ligament injury / VISI deformity.
  4. Q: What is the most commonly missed carpal fracture and what imaging confirms it? A: Trapezoid fracture; CT (coronal patterns are invisible on plain films).
  5. Q: Which nerve branch is at greatest risk during hook of hamate excision? A: Motor branch of the ulnar nerve as it dives deep around the distal-ulnar aspect of the hook.
