Syndactyly and Symbrachydactyly

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Syndactyly: 1 in 2,500 live births; M > F; unilateral = bilateral
  • Third web most commonly affected, then fourth, second, first
  • Symbrachydactyly: ~0.6/10,000; primarily males, usually unilateral (left), central digits most hypoplastic/absent
  • Symbrachydactyly: mesoderm primarily affected with ectodermal preservation (nail plate, distal phalanges)

Classification

Syndactyly

Feature Type
Soft tissue only Simple
Bony fusion Complex
Accessory/abnormal bones Complicated
Extends to fingertip/nailbed Complete
Connection ends proximal to tip Incomplete

Tentamy-McKusick subtypes: Type 1 (long-ring, most common, AD) → Type 5 (MC fusion)

Key syndrome associations: - Poland syndrome: ipsilateral chest wall + syndactyly - Apert syndrome: FGFR2 mutation, craniosynostosis + complex syndactyly (Upton Types I-III) - Timothy syndrome: border digit syndactyly + long QT — requires EKG before GA

Symbrachydactyly — Blauth-Gekeler (Modified Foucher)

Type Features
I All bones present, brachydactyly + syndactyly
IIA ≥ 2 fingers, normal thumb, hypoplastic fingers
IIB Functional border digits, central nubbins
IIIA Monodactyly, normal thumb
IIIB Monodactyly, hypoplastic/unstable thumb
IVA/B Peromelic (± wrist mobility)
  • Etiology: subclavian artery supply disruption sequence (Bavinck-Weaver)
  • May be associated with Poland syndrome

Treatment

Syndactyly Release

  • Surgery at 12-24 months; 6 months for border digits (prevent tethering)
  • Never release both sides of one digit simultaneously (vascular risk)
  • Staging: thumb-index + long-ring first; index-long + ring-small second

Technique

  • Normal web: volar extent at midpoint of proximal phalanx, 45° slope proximal-dorsal to distal-volar, hourglass shape
  • Dorsal commissural flap: 2/3 distance MCP to PIP
  • Zigzag incisions on remaining syndactyly
  • Buck-Gramcko flaps for lateral nail folds in complete syndactyly
  • FTSG for defects (inguinal crease lateral to femoral artery, antecubital fossa, volar wrist, hypothenar)
  • Long-arm mitten cast with supracondylar mold for 4 weeks

Graftless technique: V-Y dorsal metacarpal island flap for commissure; defatting distal flaps allows primary closure

Apert Hand

  • Upton Type I (Spade): free thumb, complex syndactyly index-ring
  • Upton Type III (Rosebud): all digits fused — most severe
  • First web deepening + delta phalanx osteotomy for thumb clinodactyly

Amniotic Constriction Band (Patterson Classification)

  • Type 1: simple band; Type 2: distal atrophy/lymphedema; Type 3: acrosyndactyly; Type 4: amputation
  • Deep bands with distal ischemia = emergency
  • Upton technique for band reconstruction

Symbrachydactyly Treatment

  • Most unilateral children function almost normally

  • Nonvascularized toe phalanx transfer (Goldberg): extraperiosteal harvest of entire proximal phalanx of 3rd/4th toe; best at 6-18 months; relies on intact periosteum for growth

  • Free vascularized toe-to-hand transfer: for absent thumb or all fingers absent; average 2PD = 5 mm

  • Distraction osteogenesis: high complication rate (43-75%), rarely indicated

  • Timothy syndrome is the rare-but-fatal association: any child with border-digit syndactyly needs a preoperative EKG to rule out long QT, which can trigger fatal arrhythmia under general anesthesia

Complications & Outcomes

  • Web creep: 4.2% (Barabas-Pickford); higher in complex syndactyly (up to 1/3 reoperation)
  • Groin graft hair growth in 17/24 web spaces (Lumenta)
  • Nonvascularized toe phalanx: considerable donor site morbidity increasing with growth, early epiphyseal closure (24%), phalanx resorption

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Never release both sides of the same digit at once — risk of digital necrosis
  • Border digits (thumb-index, ring-small) released first and earlier (6 months) to prevent angular deformity
  • Timothy syndrome (border digit syndactyly + long QT): mandatory EKG screening before any GA
  • Symbrachydactyly preserves ectodermal structures (nails) — distinguishes from transverse deficiency
  • Graftless techniques decrease morbidity but web creep risk is similar

Board-Style Questions (4)

  1. Q: A child with syndactyly of the ring and small fingers also has a prolonged QT interval on EKG. What syndrome should be suspected? — A: Timothy syndrome — border digit syndactyly with long QT, requiring cardiac management before any surgery.
  2. Q: During syndactyly release, why should both sides of a single digit never be released simultaneously? — A: Risk of devascularization and digital necrosis from compromising both digital arteries.
  3. Q: What distinguishes symbrachydactyly from transverse deficiency? — A: Symbrachydactyly preserves ectodermal structures (nail plates, distal phalanges) because mesoderm is primarily affected.
  4. Q: A 10-month-old has complete thumb-index syndactyly. When should release be performed? — A: Around 6 months for border digits to prevent tethering deformity — this child should be booked promptly.
