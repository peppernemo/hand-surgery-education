Phalangeal Fractures

Review Guide — Source: Handthology

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Incidence 12.5/10,000; most common hand injury 35-49 yo
  • Phalanges = anchor points for flexor + extensor tendons → high adhesion + stiffness risk
  • Distribution: middle phalanx least common
  • Single nutrient vessel to middle third (proximal phalanx most consistent); disruption → nonunion or AVN of head
  • Characteristic deformities:
    • Proximal phalanx: apex volar (intrinsics flex proximal, extensor extends distal)
    • Middle phalanx distal to FDS insertion: apex volar
    • Middle phalanx proximal to FDS insertion: apex dorsal
    • Distal phalanx: variable (FDP vs terminal extensor)

Workup

Exam

  • Always check rotation (digits extended + flexed; compare contralateral)
  • 1° malrotation MC = 5° finger malrotation (same applies to phalanx)
  • NV exam BEFORE digital block

Imaging

  • AP + lateral of specific digit
  • Lateral with superimposed condyles confirms normal alignment
  • Traction views for comminution
  • CT for delayed unions, malunions, combined articular

Acceptable Deformity (Phalanx)

  • Tolerance: sagittal > coronal > rotation
  • Coronal angulation ≤ 15°
  • Sagittal angulation ≤ 20°
  • Shortening > 2-4 mm → swan neck or boutonniere risk
  • No rotation tolerated

Treatment

Non-displaced

  • Splint × ~3 wk + buddy tape + active motion
  • Intrinsic-plus position: MCP 70-90° flexion + PIPs extended
  • Wean to hand-based splint when stable
  • Dynamic splinting acceptable in stable patterns (active flexion compresses; extensor tendon = dorsal tension band)

Surgical Indications

  • Open fractures
  • Multiple fractures
  • Irreducible/unstable
  • Associated nerve/artery/tendon injury or bone loss
  • Failed closed reduction (sagittal > 20°, coronal > 15°, any rotation, shortening > 2-4 mm)

Fixation by Pattern

Transverse

  • Multiple K-wires (crossed) OR intramedullary
  • Avoid pinning across PIP/DIP unless necessary

Oblique / Spiral (length ≥ 2× shaft width)

  • Open + lag screw compression (multiple screws)
  • Countersink heads; keep screws spaced apart (avoid iatrogenic fragmentation)
  • Stable construct → gentle early motion at 2 wk
  • Horton RCT: closed reduction + percutaneous pin = open + lag screw — equal outcomes (motion, malunion)
  • Basar: screw-only > plate+screw for ROM + QuickDASH (longer immobilization but better motion)

Very Proximal Proximal Phalanx

  • Transmetacarpal head pinning: MCP flexed 70-90°, antegrade K-wires through MC head into proximal phalanx
  • Faruqui: no significant difference vs extra-articular cross-pinning

Comminuted / Periarticular

  • Plate + screws or locked plate
  • Dorsal approach: extensor split OR midaxial between lateral bands + central tendon
  • Sacrifice lateral band if it improves exposure + reduces adhesions
  • Expect plate removal + tenolysis later
  • Plates only for severe injuries / soft tissue compromise (high adhesion rate)

High-Energy Comminuted

  • External fixation (temporary or definitive)
  • Pin-track infection risk with prolonged use
  • Bone graft (distal radius) after wound clean

Distal Phalanx Shaft

  • Crush mechanism; usually transverse/oblique/comminuted

  • Always check for nail bed injury — subungual hematoma evacuation for pain + bed inspection

  • Displaced tuft → simple nail bed repair (6-0 chromic) + soft tissue closure

  • Surgical skin glue equivalent to suture for nail bed (faster)

  • DIP splint protection

  • Seymour fracture (pediatric): physeal fracture with nail bed laceration = open fracture → I&D + K-wire reduction

  • Intrinsic-plus splinting (MCP 70-90° flexed, PIPs extended) is essential for proximal phalanx fractures — MCP collateral contracture in extension is harder to correct than the fracture itself

Post-op / Rehab

  • Cast / K-wire: mobilize ~3 wk (longer if comminuted + soft tissue)
  • Re-XR at 1-2 wk for non-displaced
  • 2-week check for pinned/screwed (incision + alignment)
  • Stable lag screw: gentle early motion at 2 wk
  • Plate: early motion to prevent adhesions
  • Therapy ASAP — stiffness is the #1 complication

Complications & Outcomes

  • Stiffness most common complication
  • Tendon adhesions (extensor + FDS/FDP)
  • Malunion: correct at original site if < 3-4 wk; well-healed → MC-level osteotomy to avoid phalanx adhesions
  • Nonunion rare in phalanges (infection, open, severe bone loss); tuft nonunion → HCS + bone graft
  • Hardware failure, NV injury, infection
  • Healing: clinical decrease in pain ~3-4 wk; radiographic 6-12 wk

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Check rotation at every visit
  • Apex volar = proximal phalanx (intrinsics + extensor) — must reduce
  • Lag screw works for long oblique with same outcomes as K-wire (Horton)
  • Use plate only when nothing else works — adhesion + removal rates high
  • Skin glue acceptable for nail bed repair (faster than suture)
  • Don’t miss Seymour fracture in pediatric distal phalanx — physeal + nail bed = open
  • Hand therapy referral within first week — edema control + protected motion within the construct’s constraints prevent the stiffness that defines this injury

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: What’s the characteristic deformity of an extra-articular proximal phalanx shaft fracture? A: Apex volar — intrinsics flex proximal fragment, central slip + lateral bands extend distal fragment.
  2. Q: What angulation is acceptable in a phalangeal shaft fracture? A: Sagittal ≤ 20°, coronal ≤ 15°, shortening ≤ 2-4 mm. No rotation.
  3. Q: What does the Horton RCT tell us about long oblique/spiral proximal phalanx fractures? A: Closed reduction + percutaneous pinning gives equivalent outcomes to open + lag screw (motion, malunion).
  4. Q: What is the Seymour fracture and why is it considered open? A: Pediatric distal phalanx physeal fracture with nail bed laceration — the laceration through germinal matrix penetrates the joint; requires I&D + reduction + pinning.
  5. Q: Why is the intrinsic-plus position critical when splinting proximal phalanx fractures? A: MCP 70-90° flexion prevents collateral ligament contracture; PIP extension prevents PIP capsular contracture.
