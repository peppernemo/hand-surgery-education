Psychological Aspects of Arm Illness

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Patient’s thoughts, emotions, beliefs, behaviors, and circumstances may matter more than pathophysiology in determining symptom intensity and disability
  • Mind uses pattern-based shortcuts (heuristics, automatic thoughts) — adaptive for danger, maladaptive for benign pain
  • Negative affect amplifies symptoms; positive affect buffers
  • Depression should be treated as a distinct pathology, not a consequence of pain

Classification

Coping Constructs

  • Self-efficacy: belief in one’s ability to cope — strongest protective factor reducing symptoms
  • Catastrophizing (Pain Catastrophizing Scale): magnification, helplessness, rumination → greater symptoms, delayed RTW, larger pain diagrams
  • Heightened illness concern (Illness Anxiety Disorder): conviction of serious illness despite normal workup + reassurance; > 6 months with anxiety

DSM-5-TR Conditions in Hand Patients

Condition Key features
Somatic Symptom Disorder Unexplained symptoms beyond pain (pruritus, numbness); sensory amplification; denial of psychological issues
Illness Anxiety Disorder Misinterpretation of benign symptoms as serious disease; persists despite reassurance
Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder (conversion) Motor/sensory deficit not explained by pathophysiology; not intentionally feigned
Factitious Disorder Deliberately produces/feigns symptoms for attention (not conscious material gain)

Workup

History

  • Screen for depression, anxiety, life stressors, financial/legal context
  • Ask about prior surgeries for nonspecific pain (Munchausen pattern: many illnesses, many surgeries, migrates between centers)
  • Secondary gain (unconscious environmental reinforcement) vs malingering (conscious plan for objective gain) — a spectrum, not categorical

Exam

  • Symptoms disproportionate to pathology — the core red flag
  • Clenched fist/posturing: functional neurological symptom disorder; DDx UMN lesion
  • Factitious lymphedema: surreptitious tourniquet → “broken windowpane” collateral pattern; cast immobilization resolves it
  • Secretan syndrome: repetitive self-striking of hand dorsum → swelling → peritendinous fibrosis

Measurement Tools

  • PHQ-2/PHQ-9, CES-D, Beck Depression Inventory — depression

  • Pain Catastrophizing Scale (PCS) — magnification, helplessness, rumination

  • PROMIS Depression CAT + PROMIS-Pain Interference CAT — recommended validated instruments

  • Health Anxiety Inventory, Whitley Index — illness concern

  • When symptoms are wildly disproportionate to pathology, resist the urge to order more tests or offer surgery. The trap is stress contagion — the patient’s distress drives the physician to act, and acting (imaging, injections, procedures) reinforces illness behavior without improving health.

Treatment

Non-operative

  • CBT consistently effective in trials for disproportionate pain, catastrophizing, illness constructs
  • Pathophysiology-targeted treatments for illness constructions (repetitive strain injury, whiplash) often ineffective or harmful
  • Build self-efficacy: acceptance, adaptation, resiliency
  • Treat comorbid depression and anxiety as primary conditions
  • Biomedical labels help avoid stigma but risk iatrogenic harm, medicalization of normal symptoms, and missed opportunities for self-management

Operative — Pitfalls

  • Operative treatment is not associated with more relief of depression than nonoperative management (Crijns et al.)
  • Unnecessary surgery reinforced by placebo effect, regression to mean, self-limited natural history
  • Multiple CTRs or surgeries for nonspecific pain → suspect factitious disorder

Complications & Outcomes

  • Depression + anxiety predict: more pain, more limitations, difficult-to-treat diagnoses, poor treatment response
  • High psychological distress predicts onset of forearm pain (prospective data)
  • CRPS + phantom limb pain may be conceptualized as disproportionate pain syndromes
  • Nonspecific arm pain (crossing anatomical areas, disproportionately disabling) strongly associated with catastrophizing + depression + heightened illness concern

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Symptoms disproportionate to pathology = think psychosocial, not “harder workup”
  • Catastrophizing (PCS) is the single strongest psychological predictor of poor surgical outcomes
  • Treat depression directly — it is not just a reaction to pain
  • Self-efficacy is the most powerful protective factor; help patients build it
  • Surgery does not treat depression; operating on somatizers causes harm
  • Secondary gain is unconscious; malingering is conscious — most patients are on the secondary gain end
  • “Illness constructions” (RSI, whiplash, cumulative trauma disorder) imply known pathophysiology where none exists — CBT works, pathology-targeted treatments do not
  • Stress contagion is the physician’s trap: the patient’s distress drives you to act when watchful support is the right answer

Board-Style Questions (4)

  1. Q: A patient with bilateral hand pain out of proportion to exam, normal imaging, and high PHQ-9 score asks for MRI and surgery consultation. What is the most appropriate next step? — A: Address depression as a primary condition (CBT referral, consider pharmacotherapy); additional imaging and surgical consultation are unlikely to help and may reinforce illness behavior.
  2. Q: What validated instrument specifically measures catastrophizing and predicts poor hand surgery outcomes? — A: Pain Catastrophizing Scale (PCS) — measures magnification, helplessness, and rumination.
  3. Q: A patient presents with recurrent dorsal hand swelling and fibrosis without a clear mechanism. What factitious condition should be suspected? — A: Secretan syndrome (repetitive self-striking of the hand dorsum).
  4. Q: What is the difference between secondary gain and malingering? — A: Secondary gain is unconscious environmental reinforcement (e.g., doting spouse, escape from stressful job); malingering is a conscious plan for objective material gain. They exist on a spectrum.
