Burns — Thermal / Electrical / Chemical + Frostbite + Extravasation

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Dermal appendages (hair follicles, sweat glands) contain keratinocytes for regeneration — deeper burns destroy these
  • Jackson’s 3 zones: coagulation (irreversible, central), stasis (potentially recoverable), hyperemia (reversible)
  • Burn injury evolves over 24-72 hours
  • Hand: little soft tissue coverage; volar glabrous skin specialized + difficult to replace; dorsal thin skin → tendon/joint exposure risk

Classification

Degree Findings Treatment
1st (superficial) Erythema, no blisters Moisturize, aloe vera
2nd superficial partial Blisters, very painful, pink dermis Topical dressings; heals 2-3 wk
2nd deep partial Blisters, painful Topical; excise + graft if not healed 2-3 wk
3rd (full) Leathery, white, insensate Tangential excision + grafting
4th Bone/tendon/nerve/joint Debridement ± amputation

Workup

  • ABC for major burns; BSA via rule of nines (palm ≈ 1% BSA; entire UE ≈ 9%)
  • 15-20% BSA = physiologic/metabolic effects; inhalational injury = common cause of mortality
  • Circumferential burns → escharotomy if vascular compromise
  • Tetanus prophylaxis

Electrical Burns

  • Resistance: nerve > vessel > muscle > skin/tendon > fat/bone
  • Muscle = largest volume → most thermal injury
  • High voltage >1,000 V; cardiac arrhythmia (monitor); myoglobinuria → renal failure

Chemical Burns — Exceptions to Water Irrigation

Agent Treatment
Phenol Polyethylene glycol (insoluble in water)
Elemental Na/K/Li Mineral oil (combusts with water)
Dry lye Dust off first
Hydrofluoric acid High-flow water → topical calcium gluconate; systemic hypocalcemia, potentially fatal
White phosphorus UV light to identify particles; hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia

Treatment

Topical Agents

  • Silver sulfadiazine: painless, broad-spectrum; can cause leukopenia
  • Mafenide acetate (Sulfamylon): good eschar penetration; painful; metabolic acidosis risk
  • Mepilex Ag / Acticoat / Aquacel Ag: decreased dressing changes

Hand Therapy

  • Splint in safe position: wrist 20-30° extension, MCP 70-90° flexion, IP fully extended, thumb max palmar abduction
  • ROM + pressure garments + silicone sheeting
  • Prevention of contracture > correction

Surgical

Escharotomy (circumferential constriction): - Forearm: axial along radial + ulnar borders, extend to MCP - Hand: intermetacarpal spaces (avoid extensor exposure) - Digit: midaxial at glabrous/nonglabrous junction

Fasciotomy: Δ pressure <30 mm Hg or absolute >30 mm Hg; hand = 2 dorsal (2nd + 4th web), thenar + hypothenar

Tangential excision: Weck blade/Goulian knife; endpoint = capillary bleeding

Skin grafting: sheet grafting for hand (no mesh for cosmesis); intermediate-thick to thick (0.012+ inch) for less contracture; glabrous donor = foot instep

Z-plasty for contracture: 30° = 25%, 60° = 50%, 90° = 75% length gain; 4-flap + 5-flap for web space deepening

Barrera: Skip Esmarch exsanguination for burn cases with concern for active infection or tumor.

  • Escharotomy of insensate full-thickness eschar does not require anesthesia — this is a bedside procedure

Blister Management

  • Don’t routinely unroof intact blisters (biological dressing)
  • Aspirate if tense/painful; debride if ruptured

Frostbite

Classification

  • Superficial (1st-2nd): white plaque/erythema (1st), clear blisters (2nd) — recoverable
  • Deep (3rd-4th): hemorrhagic blisters (3rd), hard/insensate/mottled (4th) — tissue loss
  • Hemorrhagic blisters = subdermal plexus disruption = worse prognosis

Treatment

  • Core temp >35°C before extremity rewarming (avoid paradoxical drop)
  • Rapid rewarming 40-42°C x 15-30 min (hydrotherapy)
  • Oral ibuprofen + topical aloe vera (thromboxane inhibitors)
  • Intra-arterial tPA: 80%+ salvage — greatest advance in frostbite care
  • Avoid early debridement — wait for clear demarcation (3-4 weeks dry gangrene)
  • Tc-99m bone scan: non-uptake → amputation; normal → heal

Extravasation Injury

3 Groups

Group Mechanism Examples Treatment
Osmotic Protein precipitation K, Ca, contrast Gault flush-out
Vasoconstrictive Vessel ischemia Dopamine, NE Phentolamine subq
Cytotoxic Direct cell injury Doxorubicin, vinca alkaloids Gault flush-out
  • Gault flush-out within 24h: 89% heal without tissue loss; >24h without flush-out: 52% extensive damage
  • Doxorubicin: cold pack; DMSO topical; dexrazoxane IV within 6h; UV light identifies doxorubicin-laden tissue (reddish-orange fluorescence)
  • Vinca alkaloids: heat (not cold)
  • Recall phenomenon: enhanced necrosis at prior extravasation site with subsequent doxorubicin

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Jackson’s zone of stasis is the critical zone — can be salvaged or lost depending on resuscitation
  • Chemical burns: know the water irrigation exceptions (phenol, alkali metals, dry lye, HF acid)
  • HF acid causes systemic hypocalcemia — can be fatal; needs calcium gluconate
  • Frostbite: core rewarm first, rapid extremity rewarm second; tPA is a game-changer
  • Doxorubicin extravasation: Gault flush-out within 24 hours is the critical window
  • Sheet grafts only for hand burns — mesh grafts produce unacceptable cosmesis

Board-Style Questions (5)

  1. Q: Name the three zones of burn injury (Jackson model) and which is the critical zone for salvage. — A: Zone of coagulation (irreversible), zone of stasis (potentially salvageable — this is the critical zone), zone of hyperemia (reversible).

  2. Q: A chemical burn from hydrofluoric acid is treated with water irrigation. What additional treatment is essential and what systemic complication must be monitored? — A: Topical (and potentially IV/IA) calcium gluconate; monitor for systemic hypocalcemia, which can be fatal.

  3. Q: What is the critical core temperature threshold before extremity rewarming in frostbite? — A: Core temperature must be >35°C before extremity rewarming to avoid paradoxical core temperature drop.

  4. Q: A patient develops extravasation of doxorubicin from a dorsal hand IV. What is the most important early surgical intervention? — A: Gault flush-out technique within 24 hours — saline flush via multiple small incisions; 89% heal without tissue loss.

  5. Q: What is the safe position for hand burn splinting? — A: Wrist 20-30° extension, MCPs 70-90° flexion, IPs fully extended, thumb in maximal palmar abduction.
