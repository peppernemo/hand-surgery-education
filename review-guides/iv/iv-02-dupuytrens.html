Dupuytren Disease

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Systemic fibroproliferative disorder → palmar nodules → cords → flexion contractures
  • Most common inherited connective tissue disorder
  • Myofibroblast = characteristic cell; TGF-beta, EGF, PDGF, CTGF drive fibrosis
  • Luck’s 3 stages: proliferative (hypercellular nodules) → involutional (aligned cords) → residual (hypocellular fibrotic)
  • Type III collagen elevated in early stages, normalizes but never reaches baseline

Cord-Contracture Map

Cord Contracture NV Bundle Risk
Central MCP Safe (bundles lateral)
Spiral PIP HIGH — displaces NV midline + superficial
Retrovascular DIP Deep to NV bundle
Natatory Webspace Low
  • Spiral cord = key surgical hazard: NV bundle spirals dorsal-to-volar around cord
  • Suspect spiral cord when subdermal cord passes beneath a soft compressible mass + tender on tensioning
  • PIP contracture > 45° often involves retrovascular cord + multiple cords
  • Digital cords may involve Grayson’s OR Cleland’s ligaments (contrary to older teaching)

Secondary Deformities

  • Pseudo-boutonniere: PIP > 60° → central slip attenuation, lateral band subluxation, DIP hyperextension
  • Secondary swan neck: chronic MCP contracture → PIP volar plate stretch

Epidemiology

  • Caucasian 3-6%; Hispanic 1/3, African American 1/5, Asian American 1/10
  • M:F ratio decreases with age: 30:1 at < 40 yo → 1:1 at 85
  • Ring + small fingers most affected (50-60%); thumb + index least (5-7%)
  • Risk factors: diabetes, smoking, heavy ETOH, hyperlipidemia, psoriasis, manual labor
  • RA and obesity are protective

Hueston Diathesis (Predicts Early Recurrence)

  • Family history, bilateral disease, knuckle pads (Garrod), Ledderhose (plantar fibromatosis)
  • Onset < 50, Peyronie, > 2 digits, male, Caucasian, thumb involvement

Workup

Exam

  • Diagnosis is clinical — no imaging needed
  • Modified Tubiana stages (0/N/1/2/3/4) — total extension deficit per ray (MCP + PIP + DIP)
  • Measure MCP extension with PIP flexed; PIP extension with MCP flexed
  • Hueston table-top test — cannot place palm flat on table
  • Document neuro + digital Allen test pre-procedure (especially in previously treated hands)

Treatment

Non-operative — Nodules

  • Do not excise isolated nodules — triggers inflammation + progression
  • Intralesional corticosteroid for tender/itchy nodules

Barrera: CSI = Kenalog 10 mg/mL + 1% lido (never Depo).

  • Reserve intralesional steroid for symptomatic nodules without contracted cords

Operative Indications (Hueston Table-Top Test)

  • MCP contracture ≥ 30°
  • PIP contracture > 15°
  • Adduction contracture
  • Worse correction outcomes: MCP > 50°, PIP > 40°, composite > 90°

Percutaneous Needle Fasciotomy (PNF)

  • Hypodermic needle under local; cords tight, tendons loose on passive extension
  • Work distal → proximal to preserve patient feedback (paresthesias)
  • Complications: skin tears 8%, dysesthesia 3%, tendon rupture 0.2%
  • Anticoagulants do NOT need to be held
  • Recovery: light activities < 1d, RTW 1-2d

Collagenase Injection (CCH)

  • FDA 2010; inject day 0, manipulate day 1-7
  • Two cords same session OK (Gaston RCT)
  • Degrades collagen I + III; rare tendon/pulley rupture, CRPS, anaphylaxis
  • Recovery: light activities < 1 wk, RTW 1-2d

Fasciectomy

  • Regional (all visibly diseased) — US standard; radical abandoned (high complications, no benefit)
  • Incisions: Bruner zigzag, Watson V-Y, transverse (McCash open palm — less pain/hematoma)
  • Dissection: magnification, known to unknown, do NOT skeletonize NV bundle
  • Loose closure; sutures left 2-3 wk (abnormal wound healing biology)
  • Recovery: light activities 2 wk, RTW 5 wk, full unrestricted 2 mo

Dermofasciectomy

  • Fasciectomy + skin excision + FTSG — lowest recurrence in diathesis patients
  • Tendon exposure up to 1 cm: skin graft on vascularized bed OK
  • Recovery: light activities 4 wk, RTW 9 wk

Fasciectomy Complications (Denkler)

  • Major: CRPS 6%, digital nerve 3%, infection 2%, hematoma 2%, arterial injury 2%
  • Minor: wound healing 23%
  • Revision = 10x artery injury, 5x nerve injury vs primary
  • More complications with PIP > 60°

Severe / Recurrent PIP Contracture

  • Routine open joint release NOT recommended (gains lost, flexion decreases)
  • Two-stage for severe: external fixator (Digit Widget) for progressive lengthening → fasciectomy/dermofasciectomy
  • Dorsal closing wedge osteotomy = ineffective
  • Salvage: amputation, arthrodesis, middle phalangectomy

Outcomes

  • MCP: 80-90% success (< 5° from full extension) — all methods comparable
  • PIP: 25-50% success — all methods; small finger worst
  • Fasciectomy > PNF/CCH for severe contractures
  • CORDLESS 5-yr: 47% recurrence after CCH

Recurrence Rates (Per Year)

Method Rate
Dermofasciectomy < 2%/yr
Fasciectomy ~5%/yr
CCH/PNF MCP ~10%/yr
CCH/PNF PIP ~20%/yr
  • “Recurrence is an inevitable consequence if the patient lives long enough” (Bulstrode)

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Spiral cord displaces the NV bundle superficially — palpate before any procedure
  • PNF + CCH best for MCP; fasciectomy for PIP or severe contractures
  • Nodule excision worsens disease — inject instead
  • Revision surgery carries 10x vascular and 5x nerve injury risk
  • PIP > 60° = suspect pseudo-boutonniere with central slip attenuation
  • Anticoagulants safe for PNF
  • Dermofasciectomy for young diathesis patients — lowest recurrence

Board-Style Questions

  1. Q: Which cord displaces the neurovascular bundle and makes it vulnerable during surgery? A: The spiral cord — displaces the NV bundle midline and superficial (“spiral nerve”).
  2. Q: What is the recurrence rate of CCH at 5 years (CORDLESS)? A: 47%.
  3. Q: What defines Hueston diathesis? A: Family history, bilateral disease, knuckle pads (Garrod), Ledderhose (plantar fibromatosis) — predicts early recurrence.
  4. Q: What is Luck’s characteristic cell of active Dupuytren disease? A: The myofibroblast.
  5. Q: Why are revision fasciectomies higher risk? A: 10x arterial injury and 5x nerve injury compared to primary, due to scarring and distorted anatomy.
