Start Here · Hand Exam & Consult Survival

For everyone new on service, any level. Read in your first week; the wheel continues regardless.

Every decision downstream of a hand consult starts with the exam. By the end of your first week you should be able to do a complete, systematic hand exam in under 10 minutes, describe it in language the attending can act on, and put on the right splint before leaving the bedside. Dr. Barrera runs the live “hand exam in 10 minutes” walkthrough when new residents join (rounds or clinic downtime).

Read

  • Kenney RJ, Hammert WC. Physical examination of the hand. J Hand Surg Am 2014;39(11):2324-34. (Drive bundle) — the one assigned article
  • OP-01 Imaging — what views to order before you call

The exam, in order

  1. Doorway: perfusion, resting cascade, posture, swelling.
  2. Motor by nerve: FDS/FDP each finger in isolation · FPL · APB opposition against resistance (median) · interossei against resistance, crossed fingers, Froment (ulnar) · EPL retropulsion, wrist/finger extension (radial).
  3. Sensory: 2-point discrimination by territory, reported thumb to small (normal 3-5 mm at the pulp).
  4. Vascular: cap refill, Allen’s test when either artery is in question.
  5. Report upward as structures at risk by depth and location, never “moves fingers fine.”

Consult survival

  • Call now, any hour: no perfusion · sheath signs (FTS) · tense compartments with stretch pain · high-pressure injection · fight bite · unreduced dislocation.
  • Splints: thumb spica for any suspected scaphoid; intrinsic-plus (safe position: wrist 20-30 extension, MCPs 70-90, IPs straight) for most everything else.
  • Never say “negative x-ray, no fracture” for a tender snuffbox. Splint it, arrange follow-up.
  • Elevation instructions to patients: “above your heart, on pillows, don’t let it dangle.”

Test yourself

  1. What are the 10 structures in the carpal tunnel?
  2. How do you isolate FDS from FDP, and what is the small-finger pitfall?
  3. A patient cannot lift the thumb off a flat table but extends the MCP fine. Which tendon, what compensates?
  4. List the intrinsics by nerve supply.
  5. Which nerve covers the radial dorsum, the palmar thumb-through-radial-ring, the dorsal small finger?
Answers
  1. 4 FDS + 4 FDP + FPL + median nerve. FCR is in its own sheath; PL, ulnar and radial vessels/nerves are outside.
  2. Hold the other fingers in full extension, flex the test PIP. 15-20% of people lack independent small-finger FDS.
  3. EPL rupture; EPB extends the MCP. Classic after a nondisplaced distal radius fracture.
  4. Median (recurrent branch): APB, opponens, FPB superficial head, radial 2 lumbricals. Ulnar (deep branch): everything else.
  5. Superficial radial; median; dorsal cutaneous branch of the ulnar.
