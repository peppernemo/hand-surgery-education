DRUJ Arthritis

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • DRUJ = bicondylar articulation; ulna is the stable unit around which radius + carpus rotate
  • Forearm rotation 150-180° at DRUJ; bony constraints provide only 20% of stability; 80% from soft tissues
  • Ulnar head: seat (articulates with sigmoid notch) + dome/pole (articulates with TFCC disc)
  • 220° of head covered with articular cartilage (dual articulation)
  • Ulnar variance: neutral → 82% force through radius, 18% through ulna; +2.5 mm → 42% through ulna; -2.5 mm → 4% through ulna
  • DOB (distal oblique bundle): present in 40% of specimens; part of distal IOM; contributes to DRUJ stability
  • Sigmoid notch inclination varies; cartilaginous thickness may neutralize radiographic inclination differences

Workup

History

  • Pain localized to DRUJ, limited forearm ROM
  • Difficulty with doorknobs, keys, opening jars; grinding/snapping with rotation
  • Progressive worsening over years; increasing distal ulnar prominence

Exam

  • Fovea sign: press interval between ulnar styloid and pisiform → sensitive + specific for TFCC/UT pathology
  • Piano key sign: volarly directed pressure on ulnar head in pronation → springs back with pain
  • Grind test: compress + rotate DRUJ → pain/crepitus = arthrosis
  • DRUJ compression test: compress ulna against radius → pain
  • Table-top test: push up from seated → dorsal dimple sign
  • Compare ROM, grip strength, and stability to contralateral side

Imaging

  • PA + lateral (neutral rotation): joint space narrowing, sclerosis, osteophytes, ulnar variance
  • Scheker: dynamic weighted PA demonstrates radioulnar convergence under load
  • CT: articular surface detail, subluxation, 3D fracture orientation
  • MRI: soft tissue pathology, early arthritis; 3-phase bone scan for early/multifocal disease
  • Kakar-Garcia-Elias “four-leaf clover” algorithm: systematically evaluate bony deformity, cartilage damage, TFCC injury, ECU stability

Treatment

Non-operative

  • Activity modification, OT, NSAIDs, immobilization (must include elbow to eliminate forearm rotation)
  • Intra-articular injection: 0.25 mL 1% lidocaine + 20 mg triamcinolone — significant improvement in DASH/pain/ROM at 1, 3, 6 months (Nam)

Barrera: CSI = Kenalog 10 mg/mL + 1% lido (never Depo).

  • For DRUJ arthritis, an intra-articular injection doubles as a diagnostic test — complete temporary relief confirms the DRUJ as the pain generator. Repeated steroid injections should be discouraged.

Surgical Options

Darrach Procedure (Distal Ulnar Resection) - Osteotomy just proximal to sigmoid notch; resect entire ulnar head - Best for low-demand patients; contraindicated if ulnar carpal translation risk (inflammatory arthritis without radiocarpal stabilization) - Modified Darrach (Sotereanos): oblique radial osteotomy + ECU tendon stabilization → 14/15 pain-free, no impingement at 37 months - Complications: radioulnar convergence (radiographic in 50%, symptomatic in 8%), ulnar stump instability, extensor tendon rupture - Long-term: sustained results at 12.8 years (Grawe), VAS 0.1 at rest; resect minimum bone to clear sigmoid notch

Hemiresection Interposition (Bowers/HIT) - Resect ulnar seat only; preserve styloid + foveal TFCC attachment - Requires intact or reconstructable TFCC - Interpose dorsal capsular flap ± tendon graft to prevent convergence - Less convergence risk than Darrach; 75% favorable results - Contraindication: irreparable TFCC (loses advantage over Darrach)

Matched Distal Ulnar Resection (Watson) - 270° arc convex resection matching sigmoid notch curvature - 97 wrists over 21 years — excellent results in isolated DRUJ problems

Sauvé-Kapandji Procedure - DRUJ arthrodesis + proximal ulnar pseudarthrosis (10-15 mm resection gap) - Preserves ulnocarpal buttress + TFCC attachments; controls ulnar variance precisely - Best for: rheumatoid arthritis (prevents ulnar carpal translation), young active patients, chronic DRUJ instability - Fixation: 1-2 cannulated compression screws; interpose PQ or ECU strip at pseudarthrosis to prevent regrowth - Pain reduction 73-100%; forearm arc 134-171°; grip 80-82% contralateral; nonunion 4% - Complications: proximal ulnar stump instability, bone regrowth across pseudarthrosis, hardware pain

Implant Arthroplasty

Partial Ulnar Head (Eclypse, First Choice): - Replace ulnar seat; preserve styloid + TFCC - Requires intact sigmoid notch + TFCC; press-fit stem - Limited long-term data; bony erosion in 50% at 5 years (rheumatoid)

Total Ulnar Head (uHead, Herbert ceramic): - Complete head replacement; no inherent stability — needs soft tissue support + intact sigmoid notch - Also for failed Darrach/salvage - Survival 82-87% at 5-10 years; 35% require additional procedures (Kakar)

Total DRUJ Arthroplasty (Aptis semiconstrained): - Replaces ulnar head, sigmoid notch, and TFCC function - Linked device → does not require soft tissue stability - Survival 93-100% at 5 years; complication rate 28-31% (ECU tendinitis, heterotopic bone, loosening) - Requires ≥ 11 cm proximal ulna for stem

Failed Darrach Salvage

  • Achilles allograft interposition (Sotereanos): rolled tendon “pillow” (5 x 3 cm) sutured between radius and ulna; VAS 8.1 → 1.3, arc improved 69°, grip improved 72% at 7-yr f/u
  • Ulnar head replacement or total DRUJ replacement for more severe cases

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • Darrach: resect minimum bone; more resection = more stump instability + convergence
  • Sauvé-Kapandji: proximal osteotomy must be < 35 mm from radiocarpal joint to maintain stump stability
  • HIT requires intact TFCC — always assess preoperatively
  • For young, active patients: Sauvé-Kapandji or HIT preferred over Darrach
  • Radiographic convergence after Darrach is common (50%) but rarely symptomatic (8%)
  • One-bone forearm = ultimate salvage for multiply failed forearm reconstruction

Board-Style Questions (3-5)

  1. Q: A patient with rheumatoid arthritis, DRUJ arthrosis, and risk of ulnar carpal translation needs surgical treatment. What procedure preserves the ulnocarpal buttress? — A: Sauvé-Kapandji procedure.
  2. Q: What is the primary advantage of the hemiresection interposition technique (Bowers) over the Darrach procedure? — A: Preservation of the TFCC attachments (foveal and styloid), reducing stump instability.
  3. Q: In a modified Darrach procedure with ECU stabilization (Sotereanos), what is done to the ulnar stump to prevent radial impingement? — A: Oblique radial osteotomy of the ulnar stump combined with ECU tendon stabilization through a bone tunnel.
  4. Q: What percentage of DRUJ stability is provided by bony constraints vs soft tissue? — A: 20% bony, 80% soft tissue.
  5. Q: What is the minimum proximal ulnar length required for the Aptis total DRUJ prosthesis? — A: 11 cm.
