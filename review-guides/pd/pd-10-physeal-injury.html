Physeal Injury (Traumatic and Acquired)

Anatomy / Basic Science

Growth Plate Zones (Proximal to Distal)

Zone Function
Resting Adjacent to epiphysis; supplies proliferative zone
Proliferative Longitudinal columns; increases bone length
Hypertrophic Cells enlarge 5-7x; provisional calcification
Primary spongiosa Metaphyseal bone synthesis via osteoblasts
  • Groove of Ranvier: proliferative cells for bone diameter increase
  • Ring of LaCroix: structural support around the growth plate
  • Most fractures occur through the zone of provisional calcification (hypertrophic zone)

Blood Supply

  • Epiphyseal arteries (through resting zone)
  • Nutrient artery (diaphysis → metaphysis capillary loops)
  • Perichondrial arteries (Ring of LaCroix, Groove of Ranvier)

Ossification Timing — High-Yield

  • Elbow (CRITOE): Capitellum (< 1 yr), Radial head (4-5), Internal epicondyle (6-7), Trochlea (8-10), Olecranon (10), External epicondyle (11). Medial epicondyle apophysis is last to fuse (15-20 yr)
  • Distal radius: epiphysis appears 0.4-2.3 yr; 75-80% of radius/ulna growth from distal physis; fusion 16.5 (F) to 17.5 (M) yr
  • Proximal humerus: 80% of humeral length; fusion begins 14, ends 17 yr
  • Hand: thumb physis at base of MC; digits 2-5 at distal MC end; close distal to proximal

Classification

Salter-Harris

Type Description Key point
I Through physis only May be radiographically occult
II Through physis + exits metaphysis (Thurston-Holland fragment) ~75% of all UE physeal fractures
III Through physis + into epiphysis/articular surface Articular involvement
IV Across all zones (metaphysis → physis → epiphysis) Highest arrest risk
V Axial crush injury Not visible initially; arrest apparent 6-9 months later

Workup

Distal Radius Physeal Arrest

  • Occurs in 1-7% of distal radius fractures (most common UE location for arrest)
  • Risk factors: forceful/late manipulation (> 10 days), SH III/IV, compression, infection, irradiation, repetitive stress (gymnasts)
  • Presentation: prominent ulnar head, radial deviation, decreased rotation, DRUJ instability
  • CT: size and location of physeal bar for surgical planning
  • MRI: assess non-ossified bars, edema, 3D bar mapping

Treatment

Physeal Fractures

  • Stable SH I/II: cast/splint 4 weeks; gentle closed reduction under adequate anesthesia
  • SH III/IV with > 2 mm articular displacement: surgical fixation
  • Limit reduction attempts to 1-2; no reductions after 10-14 days
  • Extra-octave fracture (proximal phalanx SH II): closed reduction + buddy taping; open reduction rarely needed (entrapped flexor/soft tissue)

Physeal Bar Resection and Interposition

  • Reserved for young children with substantial growth remaining + small/peripheral bar
  • Remove bar without destroying healthy physis; fat interposition (local or upper lateral thigh)
  • CT/MRI mapping essential for planning

Epiphysiodesis

  • Percutaneous cannulated drill technique; predictable results
  • Short arm cast 10-14 days; XR at 8-12 weeks for bridge formation
  • Can be applied to radius and/or ulna as indicated

Corrective Osteotomy

  • For angular deformity from partial arrest
  • Closing wedge, opening wedge (tricortical iliac crest autograft), or dome
  • Addresses sagittal and coronal malalignment

Ulnar Shortening Osteotomy

  • For ulna positive variance with impaction symptoms
  • Between FCU and ECU, as distal as possible; volar plate decreases hardware irritation

Specific Overuse Injuries

Gymnast Wrist (Distal Radial Epiphysitis)

  • 70% of adolescent gymnasts have wrist pain; repetitive loading → stress injury to physis

  • XR: asymmetric widening of distal radial physis + sclerosis (compare contralateral)
  • Treatment: cessation of weight-bearing minimum 6 weeks (casting for compliance); serial XR at 6 and 12 weeks
  • Premature arrest → ulnar shortening + distal ulnar epiphysiodesis

Little League Shoulder

  • Repetitive microtrauma to proximal humerus physis; male pitchers age 11-14
  • GIRD (glenohumeral internal rotation deficit) on exam; XR may show physeal widening, sclerosis, periosteal reaction
  • Treatment: rest + PT (sleeper stretches, periscapular strengthening); no throwing until symptoms resolve (6-12 weeks); gradual return per protocol
  • 21/23 returned to baseball without pain at 3 months (Carson-Gasser)

Little League Pitch Count Rules

Age Pitches/day
7-8 50
9-10 75
11-12 85
13-16 95

  • Curveball not recommended before age 14; slider/splitter not before 16

  • Prevention is paramount: limit reduction attempts to one or two gentle tries; every forceful manipulation increases arrest risk

  • No reductions after ~10 days — the physis is already healing; further manipulation does more damage than good

  • Follow at-risk fractures for at least 6 months to confirm continued growth

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • SH II is by far the most common UE physeal fracture (~75%) — the Thurston-Holland fragment is pathognomonic
  • SH V is invisible on initial XR — diagnosed retrospectively when arrest appears at 6-9 months
  • Forceful or repeated reductions are the leading iatrogenic cause of physeal arrest
  • 75-80% of radius/ulna growth is from the distal physis — arrest here is consequential
  • CRITOE mnemonic for elbow ossification: the medial epicondyle is last to fuse and most commonly avulsed
  • Gymnast wrist: rest is the treatment — minimum 6 weeks cessation; casting ensures compliance
  • Pitch counts are preventive medicine — curveballs before 14 are a risk factor for Little League shoulder

Board-Style Questions (5)

  1. Q: What percentage of upper extremity physeal fractures are Salter-Harris type II? — A: ~75%.
  2. Q: A child’s distal radius fracture was reduced at 14 days post-injury. Six months later, there is asymmetric growth arrest. What is the likely cause? — A: Iatrogenic physeal arrest from late reduction (reductions after 10-14 days increase arrest risk).
  3. Q: An adolescent gymnast has chronic wrist pain. XR shows asymmetric physeal widening of the distal radius with sclerosis. What is the diagnosis and management? — A: Gymnast wrist (distal radial epiphysitis); cessation of weight-bearing for minimum 6 weeks with serial radiographs.
  4. Q: In the CRITOE mnemonic, which ossification center is last to fuse? — A: The medial (internal) epicondyle apophysis, fusing at age 15-20.
  5. Q: What is the imaging study of choice for mapping a physeal bar before surgical resection? — A: CT (for size and location) complemented by MRI (for non-ossified bars and 3D mapping).
