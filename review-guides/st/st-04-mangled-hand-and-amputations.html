Mangled Hand + Amputations

Anatomy / Basic Science

  • Prehension requires at least 2 painless digits — one movable to grasp + one stable post to pinch (Moran + Berger)
  • 7 prehensile maneuvers: terminal pinch, subterminal pinch, key pinch, directional grip, hook grip, power grasp, span grasp
  • Thumb = 40% of hand function — preserve length first
  • 2nd + 3rd CMC immobile (center of hand); 4th + 5th CMC mobile (palmar arch)
  • Median injury: affects thumb sensation/power, fine motor; ulnar injury: finger intrinsics for object handling

Perfusion Assessment

  • SpO2 >95% rules out vascular injury; <84% requires vascular repair for salvage; in-between = explore
  • Direct pressure for bleeding control preferred over tourniquet; avoid blind clamping/cautery

Treatment

Non-operative

  • Abx within 60 minutes (cephalosporin); delay increases infection rate
  • Stabilize fractures + splint in ED
  • Regional blocks: delay until NV exam by operating surgeon complete
  • No decrease in infection at 8 vs 24h debridement — senior hand surgeon + consistent OR team preferred over rushing

Debridement

  • Organized superficial → deep; sharp excision (scalpel/scissors, NOT scraping/washing)
  • Excise skin/SC until brisk red bleeding at edge
  • Senior surgeon required, NOT delegated to junior

Irrigation (FLOW Study)

  • Saline + gravity pressure = lowest infection rate
  • No additives (soaps/antiseptics increase tissue necrosis)
  • No high-pressure lavage (drives bacteria deeper)

Barrera: Skip Esmarch if infection or tumor — go straight to tourniquet inflate.

  • For mangled extremity cases, save tourniquet time for the microsurgical portion

Fasciotomies

  • Indicated when dysvascular >6 hours OR crush with significant swelling
  • Hand: interossei via dorsal 2nd + 4th MC incisions; volar thenar + hypothenar; open carpal tunnel
  • Forearm: avoid midline (median nerve coverage challenge)

Structure-Specific Management

Bone/Joints: - Skeletal fixation first (efficient, not complex); shortening ~1 cm phalanges/MCs tolerated - Forearm shortening up to 2 cm well-tolerated; supination decreases >2 cm, pronation >4 cm - External fixation for severe contamination, multifocal, or speed

Perfusion: - Temporary shunts: carotid shunts (heparinized) or trimmed pediatric feeding tubes - Vein grafts: similar caliber, reverse for valve direction, mark direction

Nerves: - Insensate digit/hand = minimal value — salvage may be wasted - Repair: debride to healthy fascicles, tension-free, vascularized bed - In blast: zone of injury not immediately apparent — delay nerve resection/grafting ~2 weeks - Nerve tubes inferior; autograft gold standard for mixed nerves

Soft Tissue: - Replace like with like; reconstructive elevator > ladder - Reverse radial forearm flap: workhorse for dorsal hand defects - NPWT temporizes but is NOT definitive coverage; decreased infection 5x (prospective RCT)

Spare Parts Surgery

  • Fillet flap from non-replantable finger
  • Heterotopic replantation: place most functional digit in most functional position
  • Salvage index finger stump for future pollicization

Amputations

Decision to Amputate

  • Immediate amputation when 4 of 6 systems injured (bone, joint, skin, tendon, nerve, vessel)
  • Injury scoring systems (MESS) NOT validated for upper extremity
  • Patient communication critical — Bastidas demonstrated litigation correlation

Finger Amputations

  • PIP-level or proximal phalanx loss = loss of extrinsic flexor power
  • FDS tenodesis to remaining proximal phalanx: enhances MCP flexion + preserves grip strength (82° MCP, 70% grip)
  • Thumb IP amputation: FPL + EPL tenodesis to proximal phalanx
  • Don’t suture flexor + extensor together (depowers tendons)

Ray Resection

  • For amputation at base of P1 or MCP; 3rd or 4th ray closes potential space
  • 20% loss of grip + pinch strength regardless of border vs central
  • Better with early resection (within 14 days)

Neuroma Management

  • Traction neurectomy = standard
  • TMR: transfer transected nerve to motor branch of healthy muscle; 14/15 pain resolution
  • RPNI: thin free muscle graft + nerve insertion; 0% symptomatic neuromas vs 13% traction; phantom limb pain 51% vs 91%; technically easier than TMR

Complications & Outcomes

  • ~75-80% survival; half the strength + motion of unaffected side
  • 94% of severe hand injuries have ≥1 PTSD/ASD sign within 2 months
  • First web space contracture highly limiting → 4-flap Z-plasty (60° most effective)
  • Tenolysis only after passive ROM achieved + active ROM plateaued; wait 3+ months through thick scar

Pearls / Pitfalls

  • ATLS first — life before limb, always
  • Gravity-pressure saline irrigation without additives (FLOW study)
  • Senior surgeon debrides — this is not a junior task
  • Insensate hand has minimal functional value; discuss with patient early
  • RPNI is emerging as superior to traction neurectomy for neuroma prevention
  • Heterotopic replantation: most functional digit → most functional position
  • First web space contracture is a devastating complication — plan coverage early
  • Forearm can be shortened 2 cm without significant rotational loss

Board-Style Questions (5)

  1. Q: What minimum hand function is required for effective prehension? — A: At least 2 painless digits — one movable for grasp and one stable post for pinch.

  2. Q: According to the FLOW study, what irrigation method produces the lowest infection rate in open fractures? — A: Normal saline at gravity (low) pressure with no additives.

  3. Q: What is the expected grip and pinch strength loss after single ray resection? — A: 20% loss of both grip and pinch strength, regardless of which ray is resected.

  4. Q: A patient has a painful neuroma after transradial amputation. What procedure has the lowest rate of symptomatic neuroma recurrence? — A: Regenerative Peripheral Nerve Interface (RPNI) — 0% symptomatic neuromas vs 13% with traction neurectomy.

  5. Q: What digit SpO2 value rules out significant vascular injury? — A: SpO2 >95% rules out vascular injury; <84% requires vascular repair.
